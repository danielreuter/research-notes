### Up is evaluated per sequence

The F cap is on `Work(Up_R(g))`, the ancestral closure of a gate inside its RU. In a forward (inference or rollout) RU of m layers x t tokens, attention couples tokens only within a sequence and the residual stream is per token, so the closure of any gate is m layers x *one* sequence: `Up = m seq w_fwd`, while `Work = m t w_fwd`. F therefore caps *serial depth* -- data-parallel forward tokens do not add to Up -- and at F_hat >= 1 it never binds a forward RU (even all layers x one Q_inf-sequence is 1.0 fwd(Q_inf) < F). This is why an honest inference session is one legal RU, and why dynamic rollout is G-bound: the only cap on a forward RU is total work, so the adversary tiles in blocks of `m t <= G_hat Q_inf layers` token-layers and, at G_hat large enough for `Q_roll <= G_hat Q_inf`, runs the whole rollout as one RU with I* = P + tokens exactly. F re-enters only for training, where the dW reductions couple all sequences of a step. The exact solver agrees at tiny scale (whole tiny-moe rollout = one legal RU when Work <= G). The rollout planner hash with this rule is `9d3fdcc962`; earlier rollout U values (whole-chunk Up, `48ee653bf3`) are kept as 'previous planner' in the rollout tables.

## 2. The simple bound and why it is not enough

`I* >= P` (state floor) does not grow with the number of tokens: at 8B `P/Q_step` = 123 KB/tok (30,632x inf) at Q_step = 131072 and falls as 1/Q_step. The policy that replaces the per-RU input cap prices *work*: an RU is legal iff `Work(R) <= G` and `Work(Up_R(g)) <= F` for every gate g, with `F = 1.5 fwd`, `G = 4 fwd`, `fwd` = checker Work of `inference-dense` at Q_inf = 8192. Honest inference is one RU per session with token ids as the only runtime input (4 B/token); the question is the runtime input the same policy forces on training and on dynamic rollout generation, per useful MAC, relative to that reference (DELIVERABLES §0).

## 3. The certificate and the constructive partition

`L` = max of two certificates, `U` = `upper_coarse` (a checked legal partition), `L <= I* <= U`. (i) `lower_coarse`: root floor (every dynamic root byte actually read) + token ingress + the token x layer block term (Loomis-Whitney under G), solved as a MILP whose dual bound is reported (`gap` when not closed). (ii) `lower_wp` (weight presence, multi-step training only): for every chained weight tensor `W_k` (produced by the step-k update, consumed by step k+1 and its update), the convex hull of gates between the producer element and its own step-(k+1) update gate is a lower bound on the upstream work of any RU that contains both; it is evaluated per element at op level restricted to layers >= l+2 and the head (every such gate is on a producer-to-update path because the transformer mixes all coordinates and positions), and gate-checked exact on tiny 2-4 layer circuits (`redteam/wp_gatecheck.py`, 0 violations). When that hull exceeds F or G the element cannot share an RU with its update and `bytes(W_k)` crosses an RU boundary; the charge is additive per step boundary and disjoint from the roots, so `L = max(L_coarse, root_read + tokens + L_wp)`. Steady state: a *difference* of lower bounds is not a bound on the marginal (it came out negative where the MILP closed at one K and not the other), so the certified steady-state rate is the wp rate `(L_wp/(K-1) + token bytes per step)/Q_step` (= lim I*(K)/(K Q_step) lower bound); the U rate is the marginal of the constructive family over the two largest K (its per-step partition repeats, so the slope is an achievable rate). Validation of both against the exact micro-solver is in §6. Analytic reference (`analytic_reference.md`): area cap `A = min(1.5 F, G)/w_tl` token-layers, steady-state block cost `4d/(m w_c) + c_W P_l/(u w_c)` with `m u <= A`; transient (rows only) until `K Q_step m w_tl > 1.5 F`.
