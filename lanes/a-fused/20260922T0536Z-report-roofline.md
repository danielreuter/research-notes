# Roofline: the fused degree-3 GKR round on an RTX 4090 (lane a-fused, 2026-09-22)

Generated by `roofline.py`; numbers are ideal floors, not measurements.  Peaks used (Ada datasheet / whitepaper, no
sparsity): **1008 GB/s** GDDR6X; **330.3 T MAC/s** INT8 dense tensor (660.6 TOPS); **20.6 T INT32 op/s** SIMT
(128 SMs x 64 INT32 lanes x 2.52 GHz; an IMAD, IMAD.HI, IADD or SEL is one op at one per lane per clock -- the FP32
number 82.6 TFLOPS is 16384 lanes x 2 flop; only half the lanes do INT32).

## What one fused kernel does

Round `i` of Candidate A's `c`-rounds (PROTOCOL.md 4.1): three tables `f, g, h` (eq, V_l, V_r) of BabyBear^6 elements,
24 B each (6 x u32).  The S-evaluation of round `i` needs the tables *after* the fold with `r_{i-1}`; nothing about
`S_i` can be computed before `r_{i-1}` is known, and the fold must be written for round `i+1`.  So the fusion that
removes a whole HBM pass is **fold(r_{i-1}) + S_i in one kernel**: read the round-`(i-1)` tables once, fold pairs in
registers, evaluate `S_i(0..3)` over pairs of folded elements, write the folded tables once.  Indexed by the kernel's
*input* size `n` (elements per table):

~~~text
reads    3 n x 24 B          (each input table once)
writes   3 (n/2) x 24 B      (the folded tables, once)
emits    4 x 6 field elements (the 24 partial sums; one small reduction across programs)
work     fold  3 n/2 products  (x_e + r (x_o - x_e): ONE extension product per folded element)
         S     8 n/4 products  (f_k(x) g_k(x) h_k(x) at x = 0, 1, 2, 3: two products per point per pair;
                                direct evaluation, not TensorZKP's 4 Hadamards + 8 inner products = 12)
         = 3.5 n BabyBear^6 products, 108 n bytes
~~~

Over a layer's sequence `n, n/2, ..., 2` the fused kernels move `108 sum(n) + 72 n_0` bytes (the un-fusable first round
reads its base tables for `S_1` alone) against `180 sum(n)` for an ideal *unfused* pipeline (S pass reads 72 n, fold
pass reads 72 n and writes 36 n): the fusion is worth 40% of the traffic even against a perfect two-kernel design, and
far more against the limb-decomposed torch composition (INT32 fold outputs are 192 B per element, limb tensors 4-8x
the field bytes, every stage a round trip).  `eq` is a tensor product `prod_j eq(gamma_j, c_j)` and could be
generated in-kernel from 12 challenge values instead of being read and written (-1/3 of the bytes); this roofline
keeps the three-table form the reference and the previous lane measure.

## Cost of one BabyBear^6 product

~~~text
INT8 tensor core   576 limb MACs (24 x 24 signed 8-bit limbs, INT32 accumulate); the fold as TensorZKP's 48 x 48
                   convolution matrix is 2304 MACs per folded element (8 limb positions per output coefficient: 2x)
SIMT, schoolbook   36 + 5 (X^6 = W wrap) Montgomery products x 7 ops + 35 modular adds x 3 ops  = 392 INT32 ops
SIMT, Karatsuba    (2 x 3)-split: 18 + 5 products, ~60 adds                                     = 341 ops
~~~

the ~5-10 us launch floor: the 65 tail rounds of a 6-layer model are a latency problem (previous lane: 170-250 us
each) and must be fused across rounds or graphed, not optimised.

## What "good" is

1. **The round is HBM-bound at the roofline on both engines.**  At n = 2^21 the tensor-core compute floor is 29 us,
   the SIMT compute floor 140 us, the HBM floor 225 us.  Neither engine can make the fused round compute-bound; the
   attainable target is the HBM floor: **~225 us at n = 2^21 (vs 4636 us for the torch composition's round at
   t = 2^21, which reads twice as much and computes S over 2x the pairs: 5.6 ms-equivalent), ~449 us at 2^22.**
2. **SIMT must sustain 62% of the INT32 peak (54% with Karatsuba) to hide under the memory time.**  That is the
   whole tensor-core question for this round: if a fused SIMT kernel reaches ~60% IMAD efficiency it is HBM-bound
   and tensor cores buy nothing; if it lands at the usual 30-40% it is compute-bound at ~350-450 us and the tensor
   core's 13%-of-peak requirement is the way to get back to 225 us.  Base-input round 2 is the one kernel where SIMT
   is predicted compute-bound even at 100% (172 us vs 200 us HBM: 86% required).
3. Tensor-core version must not re-materialise limbs: the input bytes of a u32 coefficient *are* its unsigned limbs,
   so a fold tile can be loaded as int8 directly; the decomposition of the folded values (for the `S` inner product)
   and the recomposition of INT32 dot outputs are SIMT work inside the kernel (~150-250 ops per element), i.e. a TC
   kernel still carries ~45% of the SIMT kernel's integer work (Hadamards `g o h` are not a matmul).
4. Layer model (6 layers, 130 rounds, `bench.py` widths; per layer 12 n_0 (round 1) + 48 n_0 (round 2) + 108 n_0
   (the geometric tail) = 168 n_0 bytes; n_0 = 2^22 x 5 layers + 2^20): sum of the HBM floors = **3.7 ms** for the fused sequence
   against the measured 87-91 ms of the torch composition; the previous lane's per-round floor argument
   ("~0.5 ms at 2^21") was the unfused two-pass number.

Assumptions to check against the measurement: HBM sustained ~90% of 1008 (the Triton split-K kernel reached 0.79-0.91
TB/s); INT32 issue rate on Ada is 64 lanes/SM for IMAD *and* IMAD.HI (if `mul.hi` is half rate the SIMT floor grows
by ~25%); no L2 reuse (streaming).
