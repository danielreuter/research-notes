## (a) What is like-for-like and what is not

- **Proofs vs buckets.**  Four rows are complete proofs, verified by an independent verifier every rep (A non-ZK; B non-ZK and HVZK) -- those are the gate-4 rows.  The four bucket rows are kernels timed on resident pseudo-random data with transcript-independent coins, summed, plus a modelled remainder (13-32% of the total); they bound what the *same arithmetisation* would cost if the glue were free.  A's e2e-to-buckets gap is 17.6x (5.02 s vs 0.285 s: host openings, per-round Python Fiat-Shamir, un-fused encode); B's is 21x (2.39 s vs 0.112 s: torch encode at 1.08 s vs the 33 ms SIMT NTT, host hash placeholders, per-sub-batch launches).
- **v1 vs v2 checker.**  Same relation and statement, different arithmetisation: v1 is the WP2 checker (bits + one-hot selectors, 310,602 Ligero rows for B); v2 is the checker-min gadget set (104,217 rows, helper_rows table side).  Every *proof* in the table is v1; v2 exists only as buckets for both designs.  v2 buys 1.55x (A) and 2.5x (B) on buckets.
- **One proof vs 25 sub-batches.**  A proves all 4096 VUs in one Ligero commitment (sequential depth 438 transcript rounds, 34 MB proof); B proves 25 independent 170-VU sub-batches (depth 2 each, 111-122 MB total proof, verifier 0.8-0.9 s vs A's 6.1 s in Python).  The security column for B is the union bound over the 25 proofs (2^-128.05/-128.25), which is the honest number for "4096 VUs accepted".
- **Non-ZK vs HVZK.**  A has no ZK mode; B's HVZK row is the only GPU row with a complete privacy claim (masked codewords, ZK masks 0.40 s = 14% of the batch) and is `COMPLETE_HVZK_BACKEND`.  The B non-ZK row is the like-for-like comparator to A: **B non-ZK 2.39 s vs A 5.02 s on the same card, same relation, same B, same target 2^-128**.  Neither is a `COMPLETE_ZK_BACKEND`: B lacks the malicious-verifier coin commitment and external authentication; A lacks ZK entirely.
- **Cited rows are not like-for-like.**  SP1 is 1 VU per proof on a 4090 (per-VU number, sound row includes authentication hashing that the A/B rows omit); C is CPU, one thread, designated-verifier, and the 4096-VU row is at 2^-30 -- its 2^-128 row is 8 VUs.  They bound the field, they do not compete at gate 4.

## (b) H100 vs RTX 4090 vs A100: the observed scaling per kernel family

Run r20260922-093941-aebe (`anchor_a100.microbench`, `hash_gpu.bench`, `encode.bench`, `tensor.fused.bench`, `tensor.fused.bench_logup`) re-measured the anchor lane's shared kernels on the H100; the 4090 column is each lane's recorded number, the A100 column is `note:r20-proof/anchor-a100/20260922T0820Z-report-anchor-a100`.  Speed ratio > 1 = the H100 is faster.  Expectations if a kernel were bandwidth-bound: H100/4090 = **3.3x**, H100/A100 = **1.6x**.  If INT32-issue-bound (SM count x INT32 lanes x clock): H100/4090 = **0.8x**, H100/A100 = **1.7x**.

Reading.  The anchor lane's finding holds with the third column: **every INT32-SIMT kernel in the campaign (both hash trees, Poseidon2, the SIMT NTT, both fused sumcheck rounds, the logUp round, all of B's buckets) lands within 0.6-1.5x of the 4090 on a card with 3.3x its bandwidth**, i.e. it scales with SM x clock, not HBM.  Only the plain and Montgomery packed sumchecks (a-packed, k=0/k=3, streaming 2^26-2^28 base-field words) and the raw copy scale with bandwidth.  The INT8 tensor kernels (Triton matrix DFT, TC fused round, cuBLAS `_int_mm`) are *slower* on the H100 than on the 4090 (0.57-0.65x): Triton 3.2 emits Ampere-style `mma.sync` on sm_90 and cuBLAS INT8 reaches 5% of the Hopper peak, while BF16 cuBLAS hits 786 TFLOPS -- the tensor lane's INT8 path is the wrong instruction on this card.  Consequence for the gate-4 provers: A's e2e (Python host, 438 rounds, un-fused encode) is 5.0 s here vs 4.43 s recorded on the same card and would be no faster on a 4090; B's torch e2e went 5.63 -> 2.39 s from the L40S to the H100 because its `encode` is a torch NTT that *is* bandwidth-bound (1.08 s of 2.39).  Caveat: `microbench` and `fused.bench` printed "fraction of floor" against the 4090/A100 peak tables (no H100 table exists); the fractions above use the measured 2,995 GB/s copy figure.

## (c) Where A and B stand at gate 4

On one H100 at the full 4096-VU K=1536 batch, B's Ligero direct prover is the front-runner among complete proofs: 2.39 s non-ZK (5.9e7 overhead) and 2.91 s HVZK (7.2e7), statement-bound, union 2^-128.05 over 25 sub-batches, verified 0.8-0.9 s, and it is the only GPU row with a privacy claim (`COMPLETE_HVZK_BACKEND`).  A's GKR+LogUp+Ligero prover is 5.0 s (1.2e8) in one proof at 2^-127.7, statement-bound, non-ZK, with a 6 s Python verifier and 438-deep transcript; its improving branch (a-gpu2) does not yet run at B=4096.  Both provers are 18-21x above their own measured-bucket floors on this card (A 0.285 s v1 / 0.184 s v2; B 0.112 s v1 / 0.045 s v2), and those floors are issue-rate-bound, not bandwidth-bound, so a bigger card does not close the gap -- fusion and host-elimination do.  To reach `COMPLETE_ZK_BACKEND` at 2^-128 on this card, **B** needs (i) the malicious-verifier coin commitment (HM96) or a Fiat-Shamir argument accepted by the accountant, (ii) external authentication of the statement digest, (iii) the GPU Blake3 / SHAKE expander in place of the host placeholders, and (iv) either a single 4096-VU commitment or an explicit accounting that keeps the union bound at 2^-128 -- none of which changes the arithmetisation, so the 0.11 s bucket floor is the target; **A** needs ZK it does not have (masked GKR/LogUp claims and a masked Ligero, an unstarted design item), a fixed a-gpu2 at B=4096, the SHA-256 collision term retired (2^-127.7 -> 2^-128), a compiled verifier, and the 438 host-driven rounds graphed -- its 0.18-0.29 s bucket floor is 1.6-2.5x above B's for the same relation, so even fully fused A would trail B on this card.  SP1 (3.6 s per single VU, 4090) and C (0.41 s/VU at 2^-30, 4.7 s/VU at 2^-128, CPU) remain 3-4 orders of magnitude off the GPU rows.
