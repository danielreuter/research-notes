## 1. A hash as a replay unit's output (stands as sent)

**A unit's output can only be a Program value.** A unit is correct when its committed outputs equal its gates applied to its committed inputs (§2.1), and the Glossary says of the instrumented program's hashing that "no proof claims anything about it". So a hash can be an output only as a Definition in the Program, at about $38{,}400 \cdot \lceil b/136 \rceil$ ANDs of SHAKE256 per tile for $b$ bytes of checked words.

**For layout B, my recommendation is unchanged: no hash Definition.**
- Make the checked words **outputs of the tile Definition**.
- The per-tile digest is then the tile's leaf over those words. The row block's root over its tiles' digests is a subtree of the commitment (Q8).
- The words are committed values of the tile partition: bound at serving, and never stored because they're regenerated by replay (Q5).
- They're program outputs, since nothing reads them. Otherwise the partition checker refuses them as `committed-unread`.
- #311 refuses PoUW beside sampled proofs until its Definition is registered (`traced_as`). This tile Definition, with its checked words as outputs, is that Definition.
- A drawn tile is proved, not recomputed by the verifier (D7).

## 2. A replay-unit class inside `vllm-v1` (stands; the second half no longer arises)

- **The law supports classes; `vllm-v1` doesn't.** `TwoStageLaw` takes any classes, each with its own `Stage(p, k)`. But `vllm-v1` builds one class at $p = 1$ and draws only the VU stage (`challenge.stratum_picks`, `replay_key` = `vu_key`), and #311's adapter describes exactly that.
- Under D2, what `vllm-v1` needs isn't a Bernoulli class but **a stratified tile draw**, plus the replay of the blocks it hits. That's new code in `commit/challenge.py`, in the Commit's replay, and in #311's adapter.
- **The draw is the verifier's own randomness** (D6).
  - `main`'s `law.py` and `PROTOCOL.md` still describe `ru_key` and `vu_key` as beacon-keyed; #132 (open) fixes that.
  - Under `LEGACY = True`, the draw is a function of the prover's own run root and can be ground, so it gives PoUW no guarantee.
- Skipping steps 3 and 4 no longer arises (addendum 1).

## 3. One δ across replay-unit classes

**This falls away under D2.** A fixed-size stratified draw over tiles, with one stratum per template, is **one** `IntegrityProfile` with a `Stratified` draw, at **one** $\delta$. That is on `main` (#161: `bound`, `stratum_bounds`, `harm_bound`) and in Lean (`Audit/Stratified.lean`: `stratified_escape`, `stratified_escape_floor`).
- The wrong-work bound is `harm_bound({template: work per tile})`: the exact linear-relaxation optimum at $\delta$. Several work classes inside one template are allowed, so H2 isn't needed for it.
- A union bound across per-class profiles is still valid, but costs what you measured: 6.7× the draws at $\delta/5$.
- A rate per RU within one class: no. Neither `Stage` nor the profile can express per-RU rates.

## 4. An input derived from a serving-time root (current text: a derived Input class)

**No such class exists, and every in-semantics option fails for root_A-derived noise.** Your size argument against (ii) holds: committing $E_1$, which is as large as A, plus Y is prohibitive. Option (iii) needs root_A as a Program value, and modeling how commitments are computed is out of scope (Daniel, Sep 27).

**My recommendation, for Daniel's approval (D3): a narrow class of derived inputs.**
- **What they are:** Input families whose values are a pinned, public, deterministic function of fields fixed by the registration: root_A, the run's salt (which must then be *in* the registration record), the call index and the weight id.
- **Their treatment:** they are **not committed** and not in the committed set. Each drawn unit's statement includes the values, derived from the registration.
- **Soundness:** they're fixed at registration, so each unit's correctness is fixed before the draw (your H5), and the one-stage argument goes through with the derived values as part of each unit's function.
- **Conditions:**
  - the derivation is pinned by a spec and vectors, and implemented in core and in the Lean verifier of record;
  - its inputs can't be ground cheaply (Q8);
  - the class is restricted to protocol randomness (e.g. `inputs.derived: [{"family": "pouw-noise", "rule": ...}]`), so "no public inputs" stays true for all workload data.
- **What it changes:**
  - the partition invariant: all inputs committed and private, and input units checked against anchors;
  - Daniel's rule that the verifier evaluates no part of the computation, because deriving the noise is a PRF the verifier runs;
  - the profile's `prescribed` "noise", which would mean "derived by rule" instead of "checked exhaustively".

## 5. Opening boundary values (now A rows only, per addendum 1)

- **The prover may regenerate A rows by replay and open them against root_A.** The verifier checks only that each opening matches its registered leaf, and a failed opening after the draw is a rejection (R6).
- **Merkle paths:** keep enough of the A-row tree to rebuild authentication paths.
- **Salts:** an unsalted subtree (Q8) needs no salt retention. With hiding leaves, keep the salt seed until the openings are done.
- **The limit is bit-exactness.** A rows come out of upstream units (norms, attention), and vLLM serving isn't bit-reproducible in general. A differing value fails its opening, and an honest prover is rejected.
- **Your 1.1 KB/token of roots** holds only if A rows regenerate bit-exactly. Retaining A rows instead costs the linear inputs per token: about $80 \times (3 \cdot 8{,}192 + 28{,}672) \times 2$ bytes ≈ 8.5 MB/token for a 70B forward in BF16, or about 4.3 MB in int8 (my estimate).

## 7. Pinned interiors: may the profile count proof units, and may core gain your profile form?

**Counting tiles: yes, under pinning. I recommend it, for Daniel's approval (D1).**
- In layout B, each row block's committed output binds its tiles' digests (the root), and each tile's inputs are committed (A rows, W) or derived (noise, D3) before the first draw.
- So the tile-level transcript is fixed at registration. That is the condition `IntegrityProfile` states for a profile below the RU level ("committed before the first draw"), and it's what variant (a) provides.
- The step-3 "commit" becomes an **opening** checked against the pinned root, and pitfalls 1 and 6 don't arise.
- In Lean: the one-stage `audit_profile` over the tile partition applies, with the digest roots as registered roots. No new theorem is needed.
- The whole $n_v/k$ gain rests on the pinning. Without it (the plain three steps), the per-RU form is forced.

**Your profile form (fixed count, with replacement, stratified, two levels) is sound, but I recommend against adding it (D2):**
- **Your bound is correct.** With replacement and H3, a draw hits each tile of stratum $s$ with probability $1/T_s$, so the escape is exactly $\prod_s (1 - \sigma_s)^{k_s}$. With $k_s \ge t W_s/W$ and H2, that is at most $(1 - \sigma)^t$, since $\ln(1 - x)$ is concave.
- **The existing stratified law over tiles does better.** Draw $k_s$ tiles uniformly **without replacement** from the stratum's $T_s$ tiles, and replay every block that holds a drawn tile, once. Its escape $\binom{T_s - m_s}{k_s}/\binom{T_s}{k_s} \le (1 - \sigma_s)^{k_s}$, so your constants (4,605 draws for 0.1%) stay valid as an upper bound.
  - It needs neither H3 nor a new core form, Lean law or profile.
  - It replays at most $k_s$ blocks, fewer when two drawn tiles share one.
  - Its cap is $k_s \le T_s$ tiles, not $N_s$ blocks. At $t \ll$ total tiles that can't bind; if it ever did, prove the whole stratum. One LM-head row block alone is 8,016 tiles at a vocabulary of 128,256.
- **Your first-pass draw was also fine below the cap.** $k_s$ distinct blocks, one uniform tile each, escapes with $\mathbb E_S\big[\prod_{u \in S}(1 - m_u/n_v)\big] \le (1 - \sigma_s)^{k_s}$ by Maclaurin's inequality under H3. Its only problem was the cap $k_s \le N_s$, which the tile-level draw removes.
- **To keep Daniel's three-step shape,** the verifier can sample the tiles up front from its own coins and reveal them in two parts: first the blocks (step 2), then, after the root-match check (step 3), the tiles (step 4). Pinning makes the delay unnecessary for soundness, but it's harmless.
- If Daniel prefers your form anyway, it needs:
  - a core law with an exact escape $\prod_s (1 - \sigma_s)^{k_s}$, and a count or harm bound by the same greedy fill as `Stratified`;
  - a Lean law and its escape lemma;
  - the Lean sampler.
- **Your conditions.**
  - H1 (rounded-up sizes), H5 (pinning), abort as rejection (R6) and one registration per salt: all needed.
  - H3: only your form needs it.
  - H2: not needed if the bound is `harm_bound` with work per tile.

## 8. A replay unit whose output commits to its own interiors; the A-row tree

**What's supported, and whose check it is.**
- The lifecycle supports this only as **pinning at serving**, where the root is registered before any draw (Q7).
- The root-match check (the opened digests hash to the registered root) is **the protocol's**. The verifier runs it before revealing the tiles, and a mismatch is a rejection. It isn't a consumer's check.
- The per-tile leaf over checked words and the row block's subtree over tile digests are **new `vllm-v1` leaf and tree kinds**. That's a commitment-scheme change: schemes are defined only in `verity.commitments`, each with a spec, a reference implementation and conformance vectors. So it goes through the commitments owner (D4).

**The PoUW A-row subtree, lifted verbatim under the run root.**
- Fixing root_A mid-forward is structurally fine, but it changes `vllm-v1`'s tree rules, so it needs the same spec-and-vectors route.
- **Unsalted conflicts with two decisions:** Halevi–Micali hiding for serving leaves (Daniel, Sep 26), and "every value is private". A rows are activations, and an unsalted tree lets anyone confirm a guessed row.
- **Your grinding concern is real for per-leaf salts,** where one path recompute re-rolls root_A and so the noise.
- **Middle option:** derive every A-row salt from one per-run seed fixed before the call. A grind attempt then rebuilds root_A over all the call's A rows, costing about a $1/n$ share of the call's GEMM per attempt. Whether that's enough is PoUW's grinding analysis. If it isn't, unsalted needs an explicit privacy exception from Daniel (D4).

## 9. k RUs, or each RU with probability p (current: a fixed-count stratified draw with replacement)

- **The bug, concretely.** PROTOCOL.md's lifecycle commits interiors after the replay draw, and `TwoStageLaw.profile` gives a replay unit with $m$ wrong proof units (the code's verification units) the escape $1 - p + p\binom{n_v - m}{k}/\binom{n_v}{k}$. But the prover chooses $m$ after seeing the replay draw, and can always make it 1 (§3.3). With the test's `TOY` (512 replay units of 4, $p = 1/2$, $k = 2$, $\delta = 0.01$), `worst_case(lambda m: m)` certifies at most 26.6 units of skipped work, while a prover that skips 16 whole replay units, replaying honestly with one wrong proof unit whenever one is drawn, skips 64 and passes with probability $0.75^{16} = 0.01$. The "touched" reading (16.0) is right, because it already uses $m = 1$. vLLM is unaffected: it commits everything at serving.
- **The name.** `IntegrityProfile` keeps its meaning, the audit's output, now tied to one partition. Circuit privacy's "integrity profile" (Part I) is already "partition" there; the README Glossary should say so when the profile's v1 fields land.

### 3.3 Variant (b): nested sampling

1. Serving commits $W(P_c)$ under $R_c$ and registers; the registration names $H(C, Q_f)$ too.
2. The verifier draws $S_1 \sim L_1$ over the coarse units.
3. The prover replays each drawn coarse unit and commits its interior under $R_I$, registered before anything else happens. Interiors of undrawn units are never committed.
4. Either **(b1)** every fine unit of every drawn coarse unit is proved, or **(b2)** the verifier draws $S_2(u)$, a uniform $k_2$-subset of $u$'s fine units, fresh after step 3 and independently per drawn $u$, and those are proved.

**Its profile is over the coarse partition**, the only one committed everywhere, with the escape function

$$e_{\text{eff}}(B) = \mathbb E_{S_1}\Big[\prod_{u \in B \cap S_1} \big(1 - \tfrac{k_2}{n_v(u)}\big)\Big],$$

whose factor is 1 for (b1), where $k_2 = n_v(u)$. With a Bernoulli($p$) first stage it becomes $\prod_{u \in B} (1 - p \cdot k_2/n_v(u))$. Why: by the key lemma, each drawn wrong coarse unit has at least one wrong fine unit whatever interior the prover chose; the second draw is fresh after the interior and independent across units, so each such unit escapes with probability at most $1 - k_2/n_v(u)$; and the first draw is independent of $B$.

**Relations.** (b1) equals one-stage over $P_c$: $e_{\text{eff}} = e_{L_1}$, the same curve, with no dilution. (b2) equals one-stage over $P_c$ with the thinned law, at inclusion $\pi_1(u) \cdot k_2/n_v(u)$. The (b2) bound is tight: when the prover can move the lie to any fine unit (a chain of steps with preimages, such as an accumulator chain), it achieves $e_{\text{eff}}$ exactly.

### 3.4 The pitfalls, as attacks (rows 4 and 6)

| # | Naive design | Attack | What stops it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | the second draw derived from the interior root (Fiat–Shamir) | The prover re-salts its interior until the derived draw misses the wrong fine unit, about $1/(1 - k_2/n_v)$ tries | the verifier's own fresh randomness after the interior's receipt |
| 6 | reading $m$ wrong fine units per coarse unit (`TwoStageLaw.profile`) | The prover always makes $m = 1$: 64 against the certified 26.6 at `TOY` (§2.9) | the profile is over the coarse partition with $e_{\text{eff}}$ |
