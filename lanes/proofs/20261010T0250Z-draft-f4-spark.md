# F4 candidate 1: Spark-style memory checking for holographic C-Flock

Front F4, option 5. This is a first full draft, one of three candidate sparse-evaluation arguments, which are chosen by Lean
proof complexity first and cost second. It builds on rec-holo §3.1, §3.3, §3.4, §4, §8 and §9 (the measured kernels and
baselines come from there). Paths are as of `origin/main` `dc67b1542`; the cited Lean files are unchanged since
`1593a685d`.

Marks: **M** measured (in the cited run), **C** computed from measured numbers by the formula given, **E** estimated.

## Answer in short

- **Protocol (§1).** Each unit type's matrices are registered once per shape, as bit columns: row bits, column bits, two
  side bits and per-address multiplicity bits, for all of the shape's types in one hiding commitment `com_s`.
  - Each repetition (rep) of a session runs the paper-form lincheck (rec-holo §1). The prover then commits two fresh
    vectors `E_x[κ] = eq-table_x[row_κ]` and `E_y[κ] = eq-table_y[col_κ]`, and proves `v_M = Σ_κ w_κ·E_x[κ]·E_y[κ]`
    with a degree-3 sumcheck.
  - Read-only memory checking shows that `E_x` and `E_y` really are reads of the public eq tables. It is a grand product
    of fingerprints, proved by product GKR, in which **multiplicities enter as Frobenius exponents**. That needs no
    timestamps and no proof at registration (§1.4).
  - A selection sumcheck over the type index τ, which stays hidden in the witness, turns the registered claims into
    openings of `com_s` at public points.
- **Soundness (§2)** adds only statistical terms, plus `cr/sha-512` for the new roots through the existing fork-finder
  reductions. There is no new assumption. The fingerprint term dominates: 2^-77 to 2^-92 per rep at one GF(2^128)
  fingerprint (C), against today's 2^-97.75 per rep.
- **Lean (§4).** 16 lemma groups: 7 that any holographic candidate needs, and 9 specific to Spark. The Spark part is about
  2,900–5,200 new lines (E), on top of a common frame of about 2,200–3,800 (E). Nothing in Verity or ArkLib proves a
  product GKR, a fingerprint or memory checking.
  - The riskiest lemma is threading the **second commitment per rep** (`E`) through `rep_sound`'s doom, and through the
    executable model's rule of one root per table.
- **Cost at 2^20 nonzeros (§5, C), with T = 1 types per shape.** Spark adds 0.42–1.12 s of prover time per session.
  - That is 0.16–0.42× a 2.68 s `--zk` session at 2^27 rows, or 5–13× an upstream M0 session of the same size.
  - It adds 1.7–2.5 MB of proof (2.0–3.0× the session's 843 KB) and four more Ligerito openings for the verifier.
  - At T = 16 types the prover's addition is 0.72–1.76 s.
- **Kill (§6).** Spark dies if the bit-committed sumcheck (candidate 3) proves the same shape within about 1.5× of Spark's
  prover time. My estimate is 0.4–1.5× at block sizes 2^11–2^19 (C), so I expect Spark to be dominated. It also dies if
  "one commitment per session" excludes a commitment made inside the proof.
- **View.** In characteristic 2 the lookup candidate has to be a grand product with Frobenius multiplicities, which is this
  draft's memory check. Candidate 3 needs neither E nor GKR nor a multiset lemma, and is about as fast. I recommend
  candidate 3, and keeping Spark only if §6's experiment shows candidate 3's degree-(2k+1) kernel to be at least 2× slower
  at the largest shape.

## Where I disagree with rec-holo

1. **Timestamps (§3.2).** rec-holo uses multiplicative timestamps `g^t`, with a one-time proof at registration that every
   timestamp is nonzero. It sets aside the Frobenius repair as "one extra term per bit of the largest multiplicity".
   - I recommend the Frobenius multiplicities. They touch only the address side, about one cell per nonzero (§5).
   - Timestamps add 256 registered bits per nonzero to every audit's registered opening, and a second proof system at
     registration.
   - In Lean, timestamps also need `ord(g) > N`: the factorization of 2^128 − 1 and kernel-checked exponentiation in
     GF(2^128), with no `native_decide`.
2. **The variant without E (§4.4).** rec-holo prices "eq through a product tree over the registered address bits" at
   760–1,000 ns per nonzero, with 14–19 ns GKR cells. That was before §9.2's fast GKR (6.5 ns per cell, M).
   - At 2k cells per nonzero it is now about 140–250 ns at k = 11–19 (C). That is no more than committing E (120–264 ns)
     plus the memory check (50–230 ns).
   - That variant is candidate 3 in layered form. Within Spark's own family, then, memory checking no longer pays for
     itself (§6).
3. **The registered commitment's decoding radius (not in rec-holo).** At the Johnson radius up to `listBound₀ = 200`
   messages (`Accounting/Bound.lean` · `listBound`, `100·2^logInvRate`) can lie near `com_s`. Different sessions could then be consistent
   with different matrices. So `com_s` must be opened at the unique-decoding radius, where the list has at most one
   element (§2.3).
4. **`RegHides(A)` (§5) is not a premise.** It follows from `level0_openings_uniform` and `card_translate_fiber`
   (`ZK/Masking.lean`) once the padding covers every position opened over A sessions (§3). `SparkSound` is a theorem, as
   rec-holo also says.
5. **Price.** rec-holo's §4.4 gives 245–295 ns per nonzero. Mine is 177–512 ns at T = 1 (C), wider because I add the
   address side, the registered opening and the kernels' spread.
   - rec-holo's §4.5(b) gives 1.03× at the RoPE example (about 2^15 nonzeros). That holds at 2^16. At 2^20 it becomes
     1.16–1.42×, and at 2^24 3.3–6.8×, against a 2^27-row `--zk` session (C).
   - The hidden type index, which rec-holo did not have, multiplies the registered opening by T.
6. **Agreed:** item (e) needs `v_M` as a masked unknown with the paper-form lincheck (§1). A grand product is right and
   logup is wrong in characteristic 2 (§3.2). Padding entries carry zero weight. Route P (§3.4) is for units that do not
   repeat, whereas F4's sessions repeat one unit circuit, which is rec-holo's case (b).

## 1. The protocol, fitted to C-Flock over GF(2^128)

### 1.1 Setting

- **Shape** `s = (k, N)`: a block of `2^k` rows, and at most `N = 2^ν` nonzeros for `A_0` and `B_0` together.
  - The shape also fixes public parameters: the type count `T` padded to `2^lt`, and a multiplicity width `μ`
    (per-address counts below `2^μ`, §1.4).
  - Real circuits have 23–47 nonzeros per row (M, arch-recursion; 36.8 in rec-holo §8), so I take `k ≈ ν − 5` (E).
- **Session.** One C-Flock table `A = I ⊗ A_0` with `2^m` rows and `2^{m−k}` copies (blocks), which are the batch's units,
  and two reps on one root as today.
  - `A_0 = A_pub + A_τ` and `B_0 = B_pub + B_τ`. The template's public part (slots, pins, I/O) stays public, and the
    verifier computes its part of the lincheck in O(nnz_pub).
  - `(A_τ, B_τ)` is the matrix pair of the hidden type τ < T. Its true nonzero count is padded to N, and T to `2^lt`
    with zero matrices.
- **Fields.** Sumchecks, GKR and fingerprints run over F = GF(2^128) (`qF = 2^128`), and Ligerito over K = GF(2^256), as
  in C-Flock.

### 1.2 Registration: what `com_s` commits

There is one hiding Ligerito commitment per shape per registration (salted hm96-sha512 leaves, level-0 padding sized for
the budget in §3). Its bit columns are indexed by `(t, region, position)` for every type t < 2^lt:

| region | index | bits per entry | content |
|---|---|---|---|
| nonzeros | `(t, κ)`, κ < N | `2k + 2` | `row_i(t,κ)` (k bits), `col_i(t,κ)` (k bits), side bits `a(t,κ)`, `b(t,κ)` |
| addresses | `(t, side, a, j)`, a < 2^k, j < μ | 1 | the bits `m^side_{a,j}(t)` of the number of nonzeros reading address `a` on that side |

- That is `b = 2k + 2 + 2μ·2^k/N` bits per nonzero per type: `2k + 3` at μ = 16, k = ν − 5.
- **Every bit string defines a matrix pair**: `A_t[i,j] = Σ_κ a(t,κ)·[row(t,κ) = i]·[col(t,κ) = j]` over GF(2), and `B_t`
  likewise. Duplicate entries cancel mod 2, padding entries have `a = b = 0`, and their addresses are spread round-robin
  so that multiplicities stay small. Registration therefore needs no well-formedness proof.
- **The multiplicities are only an aid to the prover.** Wrong multiplicities make the memory check fail; soundness never
  relies on them being right.
- **Public:** k, N, μ, T and the root. The registration commit costs `T·b·N` bits at 0.18–0.31 ns per bit (M, rec-holo
  §4.1 and §9.4), which is 0.10–0.17 s at N = 2^20, T = 16 (C), once per A sessions.

### 1.3 One session

The witness `z` holds the type bits τ (bound to the units' committed type by F2's coverage proof) and the one-hot vector
`e_τ[t] = eq(τ, t)`, computed by about `T·lt` AND rows (E). Each rep runs:

| step | what | rounds | new hidden products (triples) |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | zerocheck as today (verifier PROTOCOL.md §9). It yields the row point `x = (ζ, ρ_in)`: ζ on the 6 skip bits, `ρ_in` on the other `k − 6` | as today | as today |
| R2 | **paper-form lincheck** (rec-holo §1): α, β, then `k` multilinear rounds over the block's column variables, which replace the degree-63 skip step. It ends at a column point `r ∈ F^k` with `v_M·ẑ(r,·) = claim`. `v_M = v_pub + v_hid`: the verifier computes `v_pub = α Â_pub(x,r) + B̂_pub(x,r) + β·eq(r,pin)`, and `v_hid` is a masked unknown | `k + 2` | 1 |
| R3 | **commit E** (after R2's coins): `E_x[κ] = T_x[row_τ(κ)]` and `E_y[κ] = T_y[col_τ(κ)]` for κ < N, where `T_x[a] = L_{a_lo}(ζ)·eq(ρ_in, a_hi)` and `T_y[c] = eq(r, c)`. That is 2N elements of F, 256N bits, with its own level-0 pads and mask lanes under `--zk` | 1 root | – |
| R4 | **main sumcheck**, degree 3 over ν variables: `Σ_κ (α·â_τ(κ) + b̂_τ(κ))·Ê_x(κ)·Ê_y(κ) = v_hid`. It ends at `κ_1` with claims on `â_τ`, `b̂_τ` (registered) and `Ê_x`, `Ê_y` (E) | ν | 2 |
| R5 | **read side**: fingerprint coins (φ, γ), drawn after R3's root. A batched product GKR over the leaves `h^x_κ = γ + enc(row_τ(κ)) + φ·E_x[κ]` and the `h^y` leaves, depth ν. Its last claim at `κ_2` is linear in the opened `row_i`, `col_i` (registered) and `Ê` values at `κ_2` | ≈ ν(ν+1)/2 | 2 per layer |
| R6 | **address side**: a batched product GKR over `2·2^k·μ` leaves `g^x_{a,j} = 1 + m^x_τ(a,j)·D^x(a,j)`, where `D^x(a,j) = 1 + γ^{2^j} + enc(a)^{2^j} + φ^{2^j}·T_x[a]^{2^j}`. Then a degree-3 leaf sumcheck reduces the leaf claim to `m̂_τ` at `(a', j')` (registered) and to `D̂(a', j')`, which the verifier computes (§1.4). A masked constraint checks read root = address root on each side | ≈ c(c+1)/2 + c, with c = k + log μ | 2 per layer |
| R7 | **selection**: every registered claim is at `(τ, κ_1)`, `(τ, κ_2)` or `(τ, a', j')`. A degree-2 sumcheck over t of `Σ_t eq(τ,t)·G(t)` turns them into claims at the public points `(r_t, ·)`, plus one claim `eq(τ, r_t) = ê_τ(r_t)` on `ẑ` (verifier PROTOCOL.md §16.2's extra-claims path) | lt | 1 |
| R8 | **openings**: `z` with its extra claim; `E` at `κ_1, κ_2`; `com_s` at three public points, at the unique-decoding radius | three Ligerito runs | – |

- `enc(a) = Σ_i a_i·e_i` with `e_i = X^i`, the polynomial basis of F, so it is GF(2)-linear and injective on `{0,1}^k`.
- **Rounds added per rep (C):** 291, 443 and 627 at ν = 16, 20 and 24, plus two Ligerito openings' rounds. Today a rep has
  about 2m + k ≈ 70–80 (E). Running R5 and R6 in lockstep on shared coins cuts this to about 170, 250 and 350 (C).
- **Hidden type index.** τ appears only as witness bits, in masked messages, and in the masked value `eq(τ, r_t)`.
  `com_s` is opened at uniformly random `r_t`. The price is that every session opens all T types' columns (§5): no
  opening of a single type can hide which type it is, short of hashing inside the circuit.
- **Padding.** Entries up to N carry zero weight. Types up to `2^lt` are zero matrices. μ is the shape's public bound on
  per-address counts.

### 1.4 Characteristic 2

- **logup is unsound.** Its identity sums multiplicities in the field, and a value read twice contributes `2/(X − t) = 0`.
- **A counter is impossible.** `t + 1` is `t XOR 1`. Multiplicative timestamps work only with nonzero timestamps proved at
  registration and `ord(g) > N` proved in Lean (disagreement 1 above).
- **The grand product works, with multiplicities as Frobenius exponents (my S-mult).** Write `m_a = Σ_j m_{a,j}·2^j`. Then
  `∏_j (γ^{2^j} + H_a^{2^j})^{m_{a,j}} = (γ + H_a)^{m_a}`, by `add_pow_char_pow` (Mathlib, `Algebra/CharP/Lemmas.lean`).
  For a bit `m`, `X^m = 1 + m·(X + 1)`, which is the address leaf of R6.
  - The read-only memory identity is `∏_κ (γ + enc(row_κ) + φ·E_κ) = ∏_a (γ + enc(a) + φ·T[a])^{m_a}`. No write set,
    no final set, no timestamps.
- **The verifier evaluates D̂ in closed form.** For a Boolean `a`, `eq(ρ, a)^{2^j} = eq(ρ^{(2^j)}, a)`, because
  `(1 − ρ_i)^{2^j} = 1 − ρ_i^{2^j}`. The Lagrange weights twist to `L_s(ζ)^{2^j}`, and `enc(a)^{2^j} = Σ_i a_i·e_i^{2^j}`
  stays linear in `a`.
  - So `D̂(a', j') = Σ_j eq(j', j)·(1 + γ^{2^j} + Σ_i a'_i e_i^{2^j} + φ^{2^j}·T̂_x^{(j)}(a'))`, about μ·(k + 384) field
    operations (C).
- **Fingerprints are bivariate in (φ, γ).** A pair (address, value) is mapped to `enc(a) + φ·v`, so no injective packing
  into K is needed, and the error stays `deg/|F|` (§2.2).
- **The row-regular variant (optional, E).** Place each row's nonzeros in `d_max` fixed slots. Then `row_κ = κ_hi` and
  `E_x` is virtual, `Ê_x(κ) = T̂_x(κ_hi)`, which halves E and removes the row side's memory check. The cost is padding
  every row to `d_max` nonzeros; whether that pays depends on the circuits' row-weight spread, which I have not measured.

## 2. Soundness

### 2.1 The statement

As a guarantee in `Models/Flock/Guarantees` (C-Flock has no `Definitions/` area yet), in the form of
`Proofs/Flock/Recursive/Statements.lean`. This is a sketch: names and types follow `RepSound.lean`.

```lean
/-- **A holographic C-Flock session is sound.** `Creg` was registered for shape `sh` before the session. For every
prover `σ` (two reps on one root, each with its `E` commitment and the Spark phase), the session accepts and yet no
type `τ` and codeword `M` near the committed table have `M`'s type bits `= τ` and satisfy the R1CS
`I ⊗ (S.pub + regMat Creg τ)` with the pins and region claims, with probability at most the reps' errors multiplied,
plus the far-registration term. `regMat Creg τ` is type τ's matrix pair in the unique message within the
unique-decoding radius of `Creg`; when there is none, the session rejects except with probability `εFar`. -/
def HoloSessionSound : Prop :=
  ∀ {F K : Type} [Field F] [Fintype F] [DecidableEq F] [Field K] [Fintype K] [DecidableEq K]
    (A : Arith F K) (_hA : A.Correct) (sh : HoloShape) (S : HoloStatement sh) (sch : Schedule)
    (_hsch : fast100 S.m = some sch) (Creg : Oracle F) (σ : Strategy (holoSession A S sch Creg)),
    prob (fun o => o.accepts ∧ ¬ HoloHolds A S sch Creg o.table) (holoSession A S sch Creg) σ ≤
      ENNReal.ofReal ((repError' sch S.shape + εHolo sch sh) ^ 2) + ENNReal.ofReal (εFar sch sh)
```

- `repError'` is today's `repError` with `εLc` replaced by the paper-form lincheck's term.
- The executable refinement, in the form of `zk_session_soundR`, adds one fork-finder term under `cr/sha-512` per root:
  the table's (as today), each rep's E root, and `com_s`'s.
- `com_s` is fixed before the session by registration and record custody. One fork finder per session binds it across
  sessions.
- F2's coverage proof binds τ to the sampled units' committed type.

### 2.2 Error terms, per rep

`L = listBound₀ = 200` (Fast100 level 0, `100·2^logInvRate`). `L_E` is E's list size: 200 at the Johnson radius, 1 at the
unique-decoding radius. `c = k + log μ`.

| term | bound (× `1/2^128`) | at ν = 16 / 20 / 24, `L_E = 200` (C) | with `L_E = 1` (C) |
|---|---|---|---|
| paper-form lincheck (replaces `εLc`'s `2 + 2(k−6) + 63`) | `L·(2 + 2k)` | ≈ 2^-115 | same |
| main sumcheck | `L·L_E·3ν` | | |
| read GKR | `L·L_E·Σ_{i≤ν} (3i + 2)` | GKRs together: 2^-102.9 / -102.3 / -101.8 | 2^-110.6 / -110.0 / -109.5 |
| address GKR + leaf sumcheck | `L·L_E·(Σ_{i≤c} (3i + 2) + 3c)` | (included in the line above) | |
| **fingerprints, two sides** | `L·L_E·2·max(N, 2^k·(2^μ − 1))` | **2^-84.7 / -80.7 / -76.7** | **2^-92.4 / -88.4 / -84.4** |
| selection | `L·L_E·(2·lt + 1)` | negligible | |
| E's opening + Ligerito | `L_E·(7·2 + 1) + εLig(E)` | as one table's (`εOp`, `εLig`) | |
| `com_s` opening + Ligerito (unique decoding) | `7·3 + 1 + εLig_ud` | as one table's | |

- **The fingerprint dominates.** A cheating registrant can register counts up to `2^μ − 1` at every address, so the
  polynomial's degree is `2^k·2^μ`.
- Against today's per-rep 2^-97.75 (verifier PROTOCOL.md §15: 2^-195.5 per table over two reps), the session drops to
  2^-153 to 2^-185 (C) unless one of these fixes is applied:
  1. A smaller public μ (each unit of μ saves one bit).
  2. Two independent fingerprints: their error is squared, to 2^-168.7 per rep at ν = 24 with `L_E = 200` (C). This
     doubles the read and address GKR work, adding 20–110 ns per nonzero (C).
  3. (φ, γ) drawn from K: 2^-205 to 2^-213 per rep (C). The GKRs then multiply in K, about 3× per multiplication (E).
- **Recommended:** `L_E = 1` (E opened at the unique-decoding radius, about 241 instead of 218 queries, E). It also
  removes the pair list from the doom (§4, S3).

### 2.3 Assumptions

None is added.
- **Statistical:** Schwartz–Zippel (sumchecks, GKR, fingerprints), the existing list-decoding bound (ArkLib's Johnson
  bound, already imported in `ListSize.lean`), and unique decoding (ArkLib's `BCIKS20.AffineLines.UniqueDecoding`, already
  imported in `Discharge/ZkSession/InnerSound.lean`).
- **`cr/sha-512`** enters only through the fork-finder reductions, for the new roots.
- **Record custody** now covers the E roots and the `com_s` root as well.
- **The OS's randomness:** the salts and pads of E and `com_s` (A3).
- **The coin server's randomness:** φ, γ, the GKR, sumcheck and selection coins, each issued after the framed bytes it
  answers.

## 3. Zero knowledge

**What the auditor sees.** It sees the padded sizes (k, N, μ, T, m, block counts) and the roots of the table, of E per rep,
and of `com_s`. It sees masked messages, values opened at public points (masked by level-0 pads and mask lanes), and the
public outputs. Only the ZK server sees the matrices, τ, E and the masks.

- **Every new message is a masked unknown with an M1 pad.** This covers the R2, R4, R5 to R7 rounds, the GKR layers'
  two-value claims and both GKR roots.
  - At live coins every round check is affine in masked unknowns, with coefficients drawn from public coins, public
    points, `γ^{2^j}`, `enc(a)^{2^j}`, `e_i` and φ. None depends on the matrix or on τ, so **item (e) holds**.
- **Products of two hidden values get sacrificed triples**, as M1's zerocheck already does. These are each GKR layer's
  `V0·V1`, the three-way product at the end of R4, R7's `eq(τ,r_t)·G(r_t)` and R2's `v_M·ẑ(r)`.
  - That is about `2ν + 2c + 4` triples per rep, 82 at ν = 20 (C), at negligible cost (E; rec-holo §3.3 counts about
    70 at μ = 35).
  - The address leaf's `m̂·D̂` is affine, because the verifier computes D̂.
- **E is one more masked table.** It has its own level-0 padding (`t_pad = 2·Q0` for its one opening per rep) and mask
  lanes, so the opened `Ê(κ_1)` and `Ê(κ_2)` are uniform (live PROTOCOL.md §6–§7, applied to E).
  - The abstract model already takes a family of tables: `session_shvzk (hrank : ∀ j, (T j).Rank) (hpad : ∀ j,
    (T j).PadOnto)` (`ZK/Session.lean`). E is one more `j`.
- **`com_s` is opened twice per session** (once per rep), at public random points.
  - Hiding across A sessions needs level-0 padding for `2A` openings, plus the re-registration's (rec-holo §9.4: two per
    commitment, one when made and one when retired).
  - `RegHides(A)` is then a theorem: `level0_openings_uniform` (`hι : #positions ≤ t`) together with
    `padded_openings_uniform` and `card_translate_fiber`.
  - Re-registration carries over unchanged: commit the same bit columns with fresh salts, and prove equality at one
    random point.
- **τ** never appears in the clear: `e_τ` sits in `z`, the selection rounds are masked, `r_t` is independent of τ, and
  `eq(τ, r_t)` is masked.
- **Counts.** The true nonzero count, type count and row and column weights are hidden by N, T and μ. The multiplicities
  are registered hidden bits.
  - Session counts per shape are public, and padding them is F2's job.
- **Trust.** Only the ZK server is trusted for privacy. The hiding is statistical: hm96's gap is proved
  (`CROnly.hm96Hiding_gap`), and the one named hiding premise left is `hash-derived-key`, a fact about one constant
  (`note:proofs/20261009T1940Z-report-salted-hiding-reduction`). Spark adds no premise.

## 4. Lean proof complexity

Lean today: sumcheck rounds of degree 2 (`Rounds.lean`), Schwartz–Zippel (`Core/Game/Poly.lean`), the phase and doom
framework (`RepSound.lean`, `Game.value_bind_le`), Ligerito and openings generic in `C : Oracle F`, and the ZK masking
model. There is **no product GKR, no grand product, no multiset fingerprint and no memory checking** in Verity or in ArkLib
at the pinned `b2e456fc`.
- ArkLib's `MemoryChecking.lean` and `Lookup.lean` are definitions only.
- ArkLib's sumcheck soundness (`Sumcheck.Interaction.Native.execute_soundness`) is proved, but in its own
  oracle-reduction framework, not in Verity's `Game`. Bridging it would cost more than restating the chain.

## 5. Cost

**The baselines (M).**
- C-Flock's CPU `--zk` session at `k_log` 24, m 27, 8 copies: 20 ns per row, **2.68 s**, proof **843 KB**, verification
  about **1.2 s in Rust and 21 s in Lean** (`r20261004-010035-179c`, rec-holo §2 and §4.2).

## 6. What would kill it, and the cheapest experiment

**Kill criteria.**
1. **Dominance.** Candidate 3 proves the same shape within about 1.5× of Spark's prover time.
   - Candidate 3 is one degree-(2k+1) sumcheck over N terms. That is about `2·(2k+1)²` multiplications per nonzero per
     rep: 1,058, 1,922 and 3,042 at k = 11, 15 and 19.
   - At 0.14–0.23 ns per multiplication (M, 32 threads) that is 148–243, 269–442 and 426–700 ns. Round 1 runs on Boolean
     inputs and can roughly halve it (C/E).
   - Spark is 177–512 ns at the same shapes (C). Pairing optimistic ends with optimistic ends (and pessimistic with
     pessimistic), candidate 3 over Spark is 0.42–0.47× at k = 11, 0.75–0.93× at k = 15 and 1.17–1.52× at k = 19 (C).
   - Candidate 3 also has no E (−2 openings per session, −1 to −1.5 MB, C/E), no GKR, no S3 to S8, and 20–28 rounds per
     rep instead of 290–630.
   - **I expect this criterion to fire** through k = 19. Spark's prover lead passes 1.5× only beyond k ≈ 20–22 (blocks of
     1M–4M rows), where the degree-(2k+1) rounds grow quadratically.
2. **Structure.** "One commitment per session" might be read to exclude a commitment made inside the proof. E is
   comparable to Ligerito's own round roots, but it is a protocol-level oracle (§4.3). If it is excluded, Spark is dead:
   evaluating eq at a point not known at commit time needs either a committed E, or E computed from the bits, which is
   candidate 3.
3. **Soundness budget.** If the per-session target is today's 2^-195.5, the fingerprint (§2.2) forces two fingerprints or
   coins in K, adding 20–300 ns per nonzero (C/E). This makes 1 worse.

**The cheapest experiment.** It is local, one core, about 2 hours, with no pod. I have not run it, per this brief.
- (a) Write candidate 3's round kernel (a product of 2k+1 affine factors evaluated at 2k+2 points, k = 11, 15, 19) and
  Spark's per-nonzero loops (E gather, GKR layer, degree-3 sumcheck) on upstream's GF(2^128) multiplier.
  - Time both single-threaded over 2^16 pairs, and add the measured 0.47–1.03 ns per committed bit for E.
  - The ratio decides kill 1. A 2× margin either way is decisive given the spread of the measured prices.
- (b) On the Lean side, spend a day on a statement skeleton of S3: the rep game with an oracle sent after R2, and
  `rep_sound`'s chain restated over it.
  - If that changes `RepDoomed` and all four phase lemmas' signatures (I expect it does), the Lean ranking is settled
    with no further work.

## View against the other two candidates

- **The lookup-based argument.** Over GF(2^128) logup's sums are unsound (multiplicities mod 2). A lookup must be a grand
  product with multiplicities as Frobenius exponents, which is exactly §1.4's memory check.
  - Its looked-up values (`E`) must still be committed after the lincheck point. So it inherits S3 to S8 and Spark's
    proof size, and the two candidates converge.
  - Its only possible gain is Lasso-style decomposition of large tables. The eq tables here are already
    verifier-evaluable, so that gains nothing.
- **The direct sumcheck with indices committed bit by bit.** It shares the common frame (H1–H7) and adds only S1 at degree
  2k+1, the identity `eq(x, a) = ∏_i (1 + x_i + a_i)` over committed bits, and a phase lemma.
  - About 2,900–4,900 lines against Spark's 5,000–8,900 (E), with no new commitment, no GKR and no multiset reasoning.
  - Its prover is about as fast as Spark's at k ≤ 19 (§6). Its proof is about 1–1.5 MB smaller per session and its
    verifier has two openings fewer.
  - If its high-degree kernel disappoints, a layered form computes the eq products by product trees over the bits (2k
    cells per nonzero at 6.5 ns, about 140–250 ns, C). That trades S1 at high degree for S4, still without E or memory
    checking.
- **Recommendation.** Candidate 3 first. Keep Spark (S-mult, unique decoding for E and `com_s`) as the fallback only if
  §6(a) shows candidate 3 at least 2× slower at F6's largest shape.
