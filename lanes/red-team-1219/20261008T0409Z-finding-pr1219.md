## Fail-closed (`tools/verity/lean/check.py`, `judge` and `assumption_records`)

- **A closed `Prop` hypothesis that isn't listed** fails ("a closed Prop that is not a named assumption"). `named` is set
  only for a bare constant (`Facts.lean` `pinInfo`), and the rule is now `named in assumptions`, not "a constant of an
  assumptions module". Covered by `test_a_closed_hypothesis_must_be_a_named_assumption` and by the `unlisted` case of
  `test_an_assumption_is_listed_by_name_with_its_record`.
- **A listed name is a spec definition a guarantee reads, with its record.** `read` is built from the build's `reads`,
  which hold only definitions under `meaning`. An entry outside them fails "listed, but no guarantee reads it", and a
  pinned dependency's constant is never in `reads`. Every entry, not only those used as hypotheses, must have an owner
  that is a handle, and a digest equal to the build's `reads` digest.
- **A changed assumption definition** fails under its own name, and under its `reads` record as well. The digest is the
  per-definition SHA-256 of `readCanon`: type and value. Covered by the unit test, and by the new Lean control
  `assumption_changed` (`violations.json`) on a real build in `test_the_controls`.
- **An entry no guarantee reads** fails without `--update`; `--update` drops it. Covered by the `unread` case.
- **An old-format lock** (a list of modules) fails without `--update` ("the policy lists assumption modules"); with
  `--update` it is converted. Covered by the `modules` case. Malformed values (null, a string, a non-dict entry) raise
  in `assumption_records`, which is also fail-closed.
- `lean_changed.records` now passes `assumptions:` failures through. `merge.py` refuses a merge in which one side
  converts the list and the other edits it ("changed on both sides"), and merges dict entries one by one.
- I probed the real `flock_verifier` audit at this head. The base (module-list) lock FAILs with the
  module-list message. The head lock with its one entry removed PASSes (see the first non-blocking point).

## Non-blocking points for the body or a follow-up

1. **30 of Security's 86 entries aren't `Prop`s.** The conversion lists every definition read in the old modules, which
   includes structures, their fields, an inductive and its constructors, and functions. They come from
   `H100Assumptions` (21: `Prices100` and its fields, `Shape100` and its constructors, `nominalMac`, `regPrice`),
   `Dimension.Assumptions` (5: `Machine` and its fields, `wordSet`), `DeviceChainCap` (3) and `TTOutUOnly` (1). This
   is harmless to the gate, since only a bare closed `Prop` constant can be a hypothesis, and conservative for views,
   which overcount. But the README's `assumptions` line and `assumption_records`' docstring call each entry a `Prop`.
   The smallest fix is to reword ("the definitions of the spec the guarantees read as assumptions") or to filter the
   conversion to `Prop`-valued definitions. The body's "86 assumptions" is really 56 `Prop`s plus 30 definitions of
   their vocabulary.
2. **Listing is enforced only for hypotheses.** 84 of the 86 entries, and flock's one, are read inside a statement rather
   than taken as hypotheses. The audit can't tell such an assumption from any other read definition, so removing one
   from `assumptions` still passes, as the flock probe shows. `spec_alert` diffs only `guarantees` and `reads`, so a
   removal reaches no DM. This is not a regression: dropping a module from the old list passed silently too, and the
   definition stays recorded under `reads`. But the AGENTS.md bullet ("…read in a guarantee's statement, and listed by
   name in the lock with its own record") sits in the list of what the audit checks, which overclaims for this case.
   The smallest fix is to say that a closed `Prop` hypothesis must be listed and that each listed entry carries its
   definition's digest, or to have `spec_alert` show additions and removals in `assumptions`.
3. The body's run citation should say that the run is at `72c623ea0`, that it is fast-path records only, and that it
   covered only `security`. `flock_verifier`'s conversion is reproducible: the PR's code on the base lock gives the
   committed section, and the head audit passes.
4. New failure messages say `audit.py --update`, while the tool and the docs say `check.py`. This naming predates the PR
   and is used across the file.
