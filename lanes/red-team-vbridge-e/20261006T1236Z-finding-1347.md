## 2. Public salts

The binding argument is right, and it needs only `cr/sha-512`. To open the verifier's `b || c` to another row, a prover
must break one of these three links:

- `c = H(salt prefix ‖ y)` fixes the salt `y`.
- `b = x ⊕ mask(key, y)` then fixes `x`.
- `x = SHA-512(row prefix ‖ row)` fixes the row.

Salt secrecy plays no part. Hiding isn't needed either, because the rows are the verifier's own public values.

Reuse across programs, value names or positions fails the path check even with salts set aside. The tree leaf is
`row_leaf(D, r, ·)`, and the domain `D` binds the program, value, schema, words and leaves, while `r` binds the position.
So a `b || c` registered elsewhere does not verify against this root. In addition, `verifier_salts` separates salts by
program, value and index, and `Registered.commit` rejects a repeated salt.
`test_a_value_the_verifier_registers_is_a_function_of_its_rows_alone` checks that the registration is deterministic and
that another name, another program or a one-bit change gives another root.

## 3. Forgeries

The `coef` and `dirs` forgeries flip bit 0 of word 0 in the prover's instance-0 row, in `inst.bin` only. The slow test
asserts three things: exactly that byte moves, `pub.bin` is unchanged, and the salt is the verifier's.

Lean refuses `coef` at "ring-switch claim 8 mismatch" and `dirs` at "claim 10", first in `r20261006-105510-fa41` and again
in `r20261006-110428-b738`. The ports are `row, aux, acc, coef, dirs, out, acc_out`, and port p's digest region is claim
2 + 2p. So claim 8 is `coef`'s region and claim 10 is `dirs`'s: each refusal comes from the forged port's own registered
read. It fires before the downstream effects, which are `acc_out` for `coef` and the climb into `out` (claim 12) for
`dirs`. This matches the other forgeries: `message` fails at claim 2 (`m0`), and `top` and `top-salt` at claim 12 (`out`).

The honest runs accept:

- In `fa41`: levels L0 to L6, and both algebra parts.
- In `r20261006-112100-f467`: both parts of the bounded algebra.

A part forgery is accepted by the part it doesn't touch, as the docstring says.

The `comb` forgery is verifier-side, as before. It changes the verifier's comb, so `v` is the verifier's changed
registration and the refusal is the algebra's batched constraint. It tests the algebra, not the registration, and claims
nothing more.

## 4. Slot bound

The slot bound fails closed:

- `slots` counts the same compressions as `circuit._row_slots`: the sum of `Port.comps` over the row ports, `v` and `out`,
  divided by `PER_SLOT = 3` and rounded up. The small statement's test asserts that the circuit's `per_vu.sha512x3` equals
  `slots`.
- `parts` raises when a single residual doesn't fit.
- `compose` raises at `k_log > K_MAX` whatever `MAX_SLOTS` is, so a wrong bound costs a refusal, never an unsound
  statement.

## Non-blocking

1. A sharper negative control would flip a bit the unit never reads, such as `dirs` word 1 or higher, or a word of `v` past
   128·nv bits. Only the registration binds those bits, so a refusal there shows the registered read refusing on its own,
   with nothing downstream perturbed. The current controls already fail first at the forged port's region claim, so this
   is a refinement, not a gap.
2. In `rec_open`'s docstring, the new paragraph "The statement reads `coef` and `dirs` from values the verifier registers
   itself…" sits between the `out` bullet and the `acc_out` bullet, splitting the port list. It belongs after `acc_out`.
3. `dirs` words 1 to 63, and `v`'s words past 128·nv bits, are bound only by the registration; the unit doesn't check that
   they are zero. That is harmless because the unit never reads them, but a later change that reads them would need to know.
4. I did not re-derive the arithmetic in `MAX_SLOTS`'s comment. It isn't load-bearing, because `compose` refuses
   `k_log > K_MAX`.
