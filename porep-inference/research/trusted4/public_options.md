## Summary / recommendations

- Every public-encoder option is **timed by necessity**: the server holds the encoding key, so it can recompute any missing piece of C from W at the cost of one encode (T1 `theory/template_bound.md` Lemma 4; used here as the framing fact). The template's gate-1 game (arbitrary state, no deadline) is therefore unsatisfiable for every option in this memo; each section states the timed game it lives in (protocol A, bulk work-bound, or protocol B, tiny-probe depth-bound) and the t_min assumption that game needs. None of the phase 2–3 constructions has an arbitrary-state proof at an affordable cost point; the two that have one (family C at the complete-block point, the public hash/XOR encoding) are 10^4 to 10^6 times too slow.
- The recommended public-encoder candidate remains the phase-2 **(4,6,4) drU stack at 128 KB blocks** (option P1): measured 4.5× decode step at B = 8 (harness, fused), 6.0× TPOT at B = 1 and 4.2–5.1× at B = 8 / 30K in vLLM falling to 2.4–2.6× at 118K, KV-only session 2.71×, both 3.67×; security attack-based (0.59% of the stored layer freed at W = 2n; ε_depth envelopes; Δ\* = 130 µs at 64 KB, 367 µs at 128 KB against a 58 ns CPU adversary); no theorem beyond the trivial column bound. Status: **priced, not proved**.
- **DSaG-4 with one 11-stage weak-gate butterfly per connector** (option P2) buys zero attack leakage in the tested envelope (ε_depth = 0 through D = 2048, ε_work = 0 through W = 2n at 64 KB) for an estimated 1.26–1.30× the stack's decode cost (kernel not built); its theorem needs either strong PRP gates (4–5× the stack) or the unproved linear-connector lemma, and the arbitrary-state bridge is PIEs' Conjecture 1 in every variant. Status: **priced (estimated), conditional theorem**.
- Family C (P3a) and the public hash/XOR encoding (P3b) are the only options with an arbitrary-state proof; the proof is at a cost point (4,136 and 49,128 hash-input bytes per decoded byte) that is 10^4 to 10^6 times plaintext. Two theory results (a full-width primitive refinement and a certified degree-24–64 sparse envelope) plus one measurement (a 6 KiB-state permutation priced per byte on H100) would move C to roughly the stack's cost with a proof; nothing else does. P3b is **closed**; P3a is **proved-not-priced at the certified point, open below it**.
- PIEs as written (P4) is proved in the whole-word pebbling model conditional on Conjecture 1 and on a depth-robustness certificate that exists only at in-degree n/2; its price with strong gates is 15–17× memcpy in the decode kernel (estimated), and its recommended ℓ = 2 certifies a regeneration window an order of magnitude too short for any network deadline. Status: **closed for serving** unless the linear-connector lemma collapses it into P2.
- The sparse-graph direction (P5) is blocked twice: ℓ = 2 leaks exactly W/(2n) for every sampler (an exact universal attack), and the best sound-looking ℓ = 3 point leaks 14% at W = 2n. The only measured configuration under 2.2× is unsound. Status: **open**, with the list of what would have to be proved in section P5.1.
- **KV** (P6): under a public encoder, KV can be declared only with a protocol remedy against the nesting attack (epoch re-keying at 13–20 s of GPU per 80 GB per epoch, affordable at 4–7% only in decode-dominated serving; or computation integrity, unbuilt); its security is timed and whole-word like the weights'. Under a trusted encoder KV cannot be covered at all: the server is the encoder of its own KV, and a trusted party that encodes what the server sends still encodes the nest. Weights-only PoUS is nesting-free in both settings. One-paragraph statement in P6.5.

## Reading guide: the phase-4 reframing applied to the public branch

Phase 4 (PLAN4 sections 0–2) changed the primary setting to a trusted encoder with erased secrets and, with it, the target theorem: arbitrary retained state, no deadline, security from the HILL entropy of C given W (Moran–Wichs) composed with a raw-block audit (DGO19). Three consequences for everything in this memo:

1. **No public-encoder option can meet the template's gate-1 game as stated.** With the encoding key public, C is a deterministic function of (W, key); an adversary that keeps W (which it knows) and the key can regenerate any block by running the encoder, at zero retained state. Entropy of C given (W, pp) is exactly zero. Security has to come from the *time* the regeneration takes relative to a deadline Δ, against an adversary whose fastest dependent step is t_min. Every section below therefore fills gate 1 with the timed game of threat model v3 (`research/spotcheck3.md` section 1): protocol A (bulk read of X bytes of encoded sectors per GPU under Δ ≈ T + 0.5 ms; the adversary's per-block work budget is W = ρ n hashes with ρ = 0.74 for our own kernel and 1.6–2.0 at the INT32 ceiling of a declared H100) and protocol B (k tiny probes under Δ_p; the adversary's budget is D = Δ_p / t_min dependent rounds with t_min = 58 ns on a Xeon 8470 core, 288 ns on one H100 thread, 5–10 ns assumed for an ASIC). Both are measured facts about our hardware, not bounds on the adversary's hardware; the design table has to state which t_min it assumes (`research/spotcheck3.md` section 5, obligation 4).
2. **Arbitrary state versus whole-word state.** Every attack number in phases 2–3 (ε_work, ε_depth, single-drop costs, the response curve) is computed in the whole-word pebbling model: the adversary stores or drops complete 32-byte labels, may use the decode rule (bidirectional game), and has unbounded parallelism. The passage from that model to an adversary that keeps arbitrary bits is PIEs' Conjecture 1 for invertible constructions (our stack, the DSaG) and Pietrzak's ITCS 2019 Theorem 7 for forward-only labelings, where it costs about 2 log2(N(Q+1)) ≈ 150 bits per label and is vacuous at 32-byte labels (no bound above roughly 41% retention; `research/dsag3.md` 5.4). Where a section says "proved" it says in which model.
3. **What the reframing does not touch.** Measured decode costs are properties of the construction, not of the setting; the mixer cryptanalysis, the kernel cost surface, the vLLM and KV measurements, the nesting attack, the DCR and hash/XOR cost floors all stand. Table (b) at the end lists which phase 2–3 conclusions are unaffected and which are superseded.

Naming: options P1–P6 are this memo's public-branch labels for the chart; the trusted-encoder rows of the requirements matrix (MW-DCR, MW-lattice, ideal-TDP RSA, non-rounding twins) are filled from `research/trusted4/theory.md`, `constructions.md`, `audit.md` and left as "see tdp.md" / "see twins.md" where B1/B2 and T2 are still writing tonight.

## Option P1: the phase-2 drU stack, (ℓ, d, R) = (4, 6, 4), injection-ARX with Davies–Meyer feed-forward

### P1.0 Identity

- **Name.** Stacked depth-robust encoding, ZigZag-mirrored, "family B" in the theory side's language (XOR with a key as wide as the word, key derived from d parents in the current layer). Shipping point of phase 2; recommended public-encoder candidate of phase 3 (PROPOSALS3 section 3).
- **Construction.** Block of n = 2048 words of 32 B (64 KB; 128 KB with n = 4096 recommended by phase 3). Layers R_0 = W-block, R_j[i] = R_(j−1)[i] XOR H_j(key, i, R_j[parents_j(i)]) for j = 1..4, parent order mirrored between layers; only R_4 is stored. Graph: drU sampler, d = 6 parents per node in the current layer (predecessor, 4 log-uniform-octave slots, 1 uniform-distance slot, residue-adjusted per warp; exact spec `research/graphs2.md`). Mixer H: injection-ARX (the 6 parent words injected BLAKE-style into ChaCha quarter-round slots), R = 4 double rounds, Davies–Meyer feed-forward K = st_final XOR st_mid, umulhi node tweak, 128-bit replica key in state words 3..6 (`research/hash2.md` section 1). **Decode** of one word: 4 layers × (6 parent gathers + 1 mixer) top-down, 4 XORs; the honest decoder decodes a whole block in shared memory (fused kernel) or writes a decoded scratch (unfused). **Encoder**: the server itself; the key is seed = PRF_V(replica id, comm_D), drawn by the verifier after the server's commitment to W (`research/protocol2.md`, commit-before-seed).
- **Setting.** Encoder: **public** (the server encodes and holds the key). Deadline: **required**; protocol A with X = 2 GB per GPU and Δ = 1.05 ms (phase-3 recommendation) or X = 4 GB and Δ = 1.81 ms (phase-2), and/or protocol B with Δ_p = 100 µs, k = 1024 probes, m = 8 late tolerated; t_min assumed 58 ns (stock CPU core, measured) and stated as such. Data: weights (measured in vLLM), KV (measured in the harness; see P6 for the nesting caveat), both.
- **Idealisations.** Mixer as a random oracle (heuristic; supported by the statistics of `research/hash2.md` to 2^26 samples, not by a proof); whole-word pebbling model for every security number; PIEs' Conjecture 1 for the passage to arbitrary state (not assumed anywhere in the numbers, because there is no theorem to condition); t_min lower bound as a physical assumption; no undeclared compute or storage inside the boundary (threat model v3).
- **Assumptions.** No concrete cryptographic hardness assumption is used: the security statement is "the best attack we found in the whole-word model frees at most ε at budget W or D", plus the physical assumptions above. Key distribution: 128 bits uniform (replica_seed ‖ replica_key), derived by the verifier from comm_D and never given to the challenger (`research/protocol2.md` section 2).

### P1.1 Security sketch (gate 1)

- **Target game.** The template's game (arbitrary state ≤ r|C| bits, no deadline, knows W and pp) is unsatisfiable by definition: with the key public, the server regenerates any block from W by one encode (≈ 8,188 dependent mixer rounds per 64 KB block, i.e. 0.47 ms on one CPU core, 2.36 ms on one GPU thread, 0.06 ms at an assumed ASIC t_min of 8 ns; `research/spotcheck3.md` 3.3, measured / estimated). The timed games are: **A**: the server must return a Shacham–Waters MAC over X = 2 GB of random 32 KB sectors within Δ = T + 0.5 ms (T = 0.55–1.31 ms measured for 1.6–4 GB); the adversary's per-challenged-block work budget is W = ρ n hashes, ρ = 0.74 for our decode kernel, 1.6–2.0 at a declared H100's INT32 ceiling (`research/spotcheck3.md` 4.1, measured / derived). **B**: k = 1024 uniformly random 32 B words with authentication under Δ_p = 100 µs; the adversary's budget is D = Δ_p / t_min dependent rounds (1,724 at 58 ns). Assumption stated: t_min ≥ 58 ns (no undeclared sequential hardware); with an 8 ns circuit inside the boundary every affordable block size fails protocol B (Δ\* = 18–82 µs, inside the honest tail; `research/curve3.md` 2.2).
- **Reduction chain.** (i) Mixer ≈ random oracle: **sketched** (`research/hash2.md`: SAC 2^14, BIC 2^15, T-function trails, cube test to degree 10, linear masks 2^22, eight difference families 2^20–2^26, MITM; 2 DR broken, 3 DR has a related-parent local-collision bias 2^−7.8, R = 4 at the noise floor). (ii) Random oracle ⇒ whole-word pebbling bounds ε_work(W), ε_depth(D): **missing as a lower bound**; what exists are Knuth-optimal derivations for three placement families (window / cheapest / random) plus the checkpoint family, i.e. **attack-based** upper bounds on the adversary's cost (`research/graphs2.md` reconciliation section, `research/spotcheck3.md` 3.1–3.2, `research/dsag3.md` 3). The only proved statements are the trivial column bound (≥ ℓ hashes and ≥ ℓ dependent rounds per missing label; `research/theory.md`) and the goal-mode theorem |Fast_d| ≤ s + a, which for the identity connector has deficit a = N − 1 and says nothing (`research/dsag3.md` 1). (iii) Whole-word ⇒ arbitrary state: **missing** (PIEs Conjecture 1; the forward-only Pietrzak bridge does not apply to an invertible stack and would be vacuous at 32 B labels anyway). (iv) Pebbling bound ⇒ audit soundness: for A, a deficit above ε_work(ρ) is unanswerable and the 65,536 sampled sectors hit thousands of missing labels, so per-challenge detection is ≈ 1 (`research/spotcheck3.md` 5, item 6; derived); for B, P_att = 1 − (ε\* − ε_depth(D)) per probe and the k / m policy amplifies (`research/curve3.md` 2.1; derived from the attack-based envelope).
- **Affordances used and what breaks without them.** Deadline: essential; without it ε = 100% (the server re-encodes from W). Commit-before-seed: essential; without it the server chooses W after the seed and makes R anything it likes, e.g. all zeros (`research/protocol2.md` section 0). Random oracle for the mixer: used for every number (the attacks assume no algebraic shortcut through H); without it the reverse move (per-parent inversion) makes attacks 20–35× cheaper, which is why the feed-forward is mandatory (`research/graphs2.md` section 4 of the reconciliation, attack-based). W-dependent setup: none beyond comm_D. Trusted encoder: not used.
- **Concrete bound at r = 18/19 (ε\* = 5.26%), δ = 0.01.** Protocol A at ρ = 2 (best declared-GPU adversary): ε_work = **0.59%** of the stored layer at 64 KB, 0.68% at 128 KB (attack-based, `research/spotcheck3.md` 3.2), plus ≤ 0.65% from CPU whole-block re-encodes inside Δ if GPUs are challenged one at a time (0.08% if all eight at once; derived), against the 5.26% target; challenge cost 0.66% of GPU time at 5/s (measured). The cliff (everything droppable) is at ρ ≈ 3.5, i.e. 4.7× our hash rate (attack-based). Protocol B at Δ_p = 100 µs, t_min = 58 ns, k = 1024, m = 8: the attacker regenerates ε_depth(1,724) ≈ 2.0 points of the 5.26 for free at 64 KB (0.54 points at 128 KB), misses 3.26% (4.72%) of probes, and passes an audit with probability **1.2 × 10^−7** (64 KB) / **3.8 × 10^−13** (128 KB); honest false reject 1.4 × 10^−7 (device proxy) / 2 × 10^−5 (host proxy) per audit (attack-based envelope with a measured honest proxy, `research/curve3.md` 2.4). Crypto error: none quantified (the RO heuristic has no advantage term). Additive floor: the "η" the adversary recovers for free, 0.59% (A) or 2.0 points (B, 64 KB) / 0.54 points (B, 128 KB).
- **Known gaps (exact statements not proved).** (1) Any non-trivial lower bound on the adversary's work or depth for ℓ = 4 drU d = 6 in the random-oracle model; (2) an (α, β) depth-robustness certificate for the drU graph at n = 2048–8192 (the depth numbers δ(·) are attack-calibrated); (3) the arbitrary-state bridge (Conjecture 1 or a replacement); (4) random-oracle behaviour of injection-ARX beyond the tested statistics, algebraic degree above 10, DM preimage resistance of the 4-round 8-word ChaCha permutation (`research/hash2.md` section 7); (5) the t_min lower bound is an assumption, not derived.

### P1.2 Accounting (gate 2)

- **Persistent state per plaintext bit.** |C| = |W| (rate 1: the replica replaces the plaintext; PROPOSALS3 7.1). Public parameters on the server: 128-bit key + graph seed + PoRepParams (bytes). Tags: protocol A, one 8 B Shacham–Waters tag per 32 KB sector = 0.025% of |W|, held by the challenger, not the server (measured / derived, `research/challenger2.md`); protocol B, per-word Merkle paths (~400 B per probe, transient) or 4 KB sector tags. Tables, seeds, RNS or layout expansion: none. Transient (not persistent): 0 extra HBM for weights on the fused path (tiles decoded in shared memory); 2.2 GB decode scratch on the unfused / prefill path sized to the largest matrix (vLLM Qwen-7B incl. tiled lm_head); one layer of KV per step on the attention path (1.9 GB at B = 8 / 118K, 1/28 of the pool) (measured, `CATALOG.md` section 2). **Total persistent ≤ 1.0003 |W|; passes 1.05.** Not yet encoded: embedding table (1.09 GB = 7% of Qwen-7B), norms, biases.
- **Entropy rate of C given (W, pp).** **Zero** (C is a deterministic public function of W and the key). Margin over 1 − ε\* = 0.9474: not applicable; this option's security is timed, not entropic.
- **Block and tile sizes.** Decode tile = block = 64 KB (n = 2048; three 64 KB SMEM buffers in the ping-pong kernel), 128 KB recommended (1 CTA/SM kernel not built). Audit block: protocol A reads 32 KB sectors of the *encoded* bytes (half a block; no decode on the audit path); protocol B reads 32 B words. Tile and audit block do not coincide, which is harmless because the audit is on C, not on W.

### P1.3 Symbolic cost (gate 3)

Per decoded byte at the operating point (64 KB blocks; sources `research/kernels2.md`, `research/hash2.md` section 1, PLAN4 3.1):

- int32 instructions on CUDA cores: **≈ 25 per byte** (PLAN4 3.1, derived from hash2's 128 ALU + 16 XOR per node-layer at d = 6 plus gathers, address arithmetic and tweak: 4 layers × 144 / 32 B ≈ 18 per byte for the mixer alone).
- Tensor-core MACs: 0 (the mma.sync "mmaarx" mixer was 2.3× slower and is ruled out; `CATALOG.md` 3.9).
- Bytes moved from HBM: 1.0 (the codeword) + 0 public key + 0 tables; the fused kernel writes nothing back. Shared-memory gather traffic: ℓ·d = **24 parent-word bytes per decoded byte** (drU d = 6, 1.6× below bucket d = 6 at equal ℓ; `research/graphs2.md`, model).
- Serial depth per decoded word: **ℓ = 4** dependent mixer evaluations (decode); ℓ(n−1) = 8,188 for a full block encode or regeneration (derived).
- Working set: one block in three SMEM buffers (prefetch + ping + pong: 192 KB at 64 KB blocks, hence 1 CTA/SM; 96 KB × 2 CTAs at 32 KB), 64–94 registers per thread (measured, `research/kernels2.md` v9).
- Locality: a tile decode touches exactly its own block (block-local; parents are in-block by construction).
- Measured kernel cost: t_node = max(2.81, 2.37 + ℓ(−0.22 + 0.200 d + 0.221 R)) ns·SM per 32 B node at 64 KB → **9.83 ns·SM = 3.50× memcpy** on the surface; measured 440 GB/s = **3.42× memcpy** at 64 KB, 475 GB/s = 3.17× at 32 KB with 2 CTAs/SM (measured, pod a, `results/pod-a/phase2/rq1/`). The kernel is int-ALU *latency* bound (pipe 37% utilised at 1 CTA/SM); occupancy is the only lever that moved; remaining kernel headroom estimated ≈ 1.3× in total (`research/kernels2.md`, `research/fused2.md`).

**Batch effects.** The weight cost is a flat 32–34 ms per step, so the ratio is set by how lean the plaintext step is: it falls with context (KV-bound plaintext) and with batch, and rises on a leaner engine (vLLM is 1.1–1.4× leaner than the harness, hence 6.0× at B = 1). Fusion into the GEMM is worth 9–10% at ℓ = 4 (the kernel is 92–94% decode); at M ≥ 256 the fused kernel is 0–10% slower than unfused (X-slab re-read and atomics) and the unfused path applies (`research/fused2.md`).

**Feasibility position** (against D1's measured batch region, `research/trusted4/decoder.md` §3.8 and `decoder/out/region_vectors.csv`, primary; G1's roofline `research/trusted4/region.md` §5.1 as the interpolator, quoted where used). Decode phase (b ≤ 256): **flat at 5.2–6.0×; fits neither +100% nor +10%** at any decode batch (measured). +100% (R ≤ 2.0) is reached at **b ≈ 1,700** on the measured vector (log-linear between the 1,024 and 2,048 points, derived), ≈ 1,300 if the vector is rescaled to the real kernel's 4.52× (derived); G1's model puts it at 936 (fused) / 1,591 (unfused) for the 440 GB/s kernel (model). +10% (R ≤ 1.10): **not reached at any measured b ≤ 8,192** (1.22 at 8,192, decode-then-GEMM, measured; the d2g floor there is 1.09 with a free decoder, so 30 int32 per byte can never fit it in that implementation); G1's fused-mode extrapolation gives M_1.1 ≈ 7,129 (model, fused kernel above the ridge, none exists: `region.md` §1.5 caveat). Prefill (chunks of 2,048–8,192 tokens): **+100% yes** from ≈ 1,700-token chunks (measured vector), **+10% no** on the measured vector (1.22 at 8,192), yes only in G1's fused extrapolation. A real serving prefill is gentler than the matmul sequence because attention over the context dilutes the weight cost: vLLM's 2,048-token turn prefill measured +16–34% at 30K and +6–8% at 118K (`serving2.md`), against 1.84× for a 2,048-token matmul-only chunk. **KV**: the KV read has no batch amortisation (D1 §3.8, G1 §7), so it sits at the b = 1 column forever: **5.8× synthetic / 4.5× real per KV byte, never +100% or +10%**; measured KV-only session 2.71× at B = 8 (diluted by plaintext weights and prefill; see P6). *Previous estimate (flat 32–34 ms delta against vLLM plaintext steps): +100% at B ≥ 16 at 118K or B ≥ 64 at 30K; +10% never in the fused regime; prefill +10% at ≥ 8K tokens.* The change: the region measures the matmul sequence without attention, where the plaintext step stays 5.65 ms up to b = 64, so the +100% batch moves from tens (vLLM, attention-diluted) to ≈ 1,700 (matmul-only); the two are reconciled in the next paragraph.

**vLLM versus the synthetic sequence (cross-check).** Both measure the same thing divided by different denominators. The encoded weight pass costs a nearly constant absolute time per step below the ridge: D1's synthetic 30-op vector adds (5.78 − 1) × 5.65 ms = **27.0 ms** (measured), K2's fused kernel adds (4.52 − 1) × 8.83 = **31.1 ms** in the harness (measured), and vLLM's delta is **32.4 ms** (38.8 − 6.4 ms, measured); the three agree within 20% and are independent of batch (b ≤ 64) and of context. What differs is the plaintext step: D1's matmul-only sequence is 5.65 ms at every b ≤ 64 (no attention), so its ratio stays at 5.8× until the GEMM becomes compute-bound above b ≈ 280–512; vLLM's plaintext step includes attention over the whole context, whose KV read grows with B × context (Qwen2.5-7B: 56 KB per token, ≈ 13 GB at B = 8 / 30K, ≈ 53 GB at B = 8 / 118K at ≈ 1.9 TB/s), so the plaintext denominator is 6.4 ms (B = 1 / 30K), 8.1 (B = 1 / 118K), 10.4 (B = 8 / 30K unique), 23.2 (B = 8 / 118K) and the ratio falls: **6.04×, 4.99×, 4.24×, 2.43×** (`serving2.md`, measured). So the vLLM ratio falls with context because attention's share of the plaintext step grows, not because the decode gets cheaper; the region's ratio does not fall until the *matmul* itself grows with batch. For the chart: read the region rows as "delta ÷ matmul" and the vLLM rows as "delta ÷ (matmul + attention)"; the b at which a decode-phase deployment sees ≤ 2× is set by how much attention it carries (B = 8 / 118K is already at 2.4×), while the matmul-only region says ≤ 2× needs b ≈ 1,700 tokens per pass.

Reference points: plaintext = 1.00×; MW-DCR s = 31 measured 92 s per 12 KiB word (`research/mw3.md`); this option's decode kernel 3.42× memcpy and 4.5× decode step at B = 8.

- **Status: priced-not-proved.** Measured end to end (harness and vLLM, token-identical on the harness path, near-identical in vLLM); security attack-based in the whole-word model with a stated t_min assumption; no theorem beyond the trivial column bound.

## Option P2: DSaG-4 with partial weak-gate butterfly connectors (PIEs-style)

### P2.0 Identity

- **Name.** Dagwood Sandwich Graph with four keyed layers and three cheap connectors: "DSaG-4 + 3 bfly11 weak" in `research/dsag3.md` section 4; the phase-3 recommendation to replace the plain stack pending a kernel prototype (`dsag3.md` section 4, decision rule of `spotcheck3.md` 6.1).
- **Construction.** The P1 stack (four ZigZag-mirrored drU d = 6 layers, injection-ARX R = 4 with Davies–Meyer, 32 B words, n = 2048 per 64 KB block), with a connector inserted between consecutive layers: one 11-stage butterfly (log2 n stages, so every output word depends on every input word exactly once) of 2-in / 2-out **weak** gates, one ARX step per lane, evaluated in either direction and modelled adversarially as "any two of the gate's four words give the other two". Keyed layer j maps In_j to L_j = In_j XOR K_j with K_j[i] = H(L_j[parents_j(i)]); the connector maps L_j to In_(j+1). Only the top layer is stored. Decode of a block: invert layer 4 (keys from parents in layer 4, XOR), run the connector backwards (two shared-memory passes: bits 0–4 in registers, re-tile, bits 5–10), invert layer 3, and so on. **Decoding one word requires decoding the whole block**: a connector output depends on all n inputs, so the option has no sub-block random access to W (the audit reads C, so this does not affect the protocols). Encoder: the server, key from commit-before-seed as in P1.
- **Setting.** Encoder **public**; deadline **required** (timed games A and B of threat model v3, t_min assumption stated); data: weights and KV (KV with the P6 nesting caveat).
- **Idealisations.** Mixer as random oracle (heuristic, `hash2.md`); gates: none for the attack numbers (weak gates are the adversarial worst case), an ideal 2-in / 2-out PRP for PIEs' theorem (not this variant); whole-word pebbling model; Conjecture 1 for arbitrary state; t_min.
- **Assumptions.** No concrete hardness assumption; the same physical assumptions as P1.

### P2.1 Security sketch (gate 1)

- **Target game.** As P1: the deadline-free arbitrary-state game is unsatisfiable (public key ⇒ re-encode from W); the games are A (bulk, W = ρ n) and B (probes, D = Δ_p / t_min).
- **Reduction chain.** (i) Mixer ≈ RO: sketched (`hash2.md`), as P1. (ii) RO ⇒ whole-word bounds: **attack-based** for this variant. The exact solver (`spotcheck3/dsag_game.py`, Knuth / Dijkstra min-depth and min-work over AND-rules) with top drops (window / cheapest / random) and the new **checkpoint attack** (store a depth-reducing removal set of every layer, drop everything else; total depth = sum of the per-layer residual depths) finds ε_depth = **0** at every D ≤ 2048 and ε_work = **0** at W = n and 2n for 64 KB blocks (`dsag3.md` 3.1–3.2). What is *proved* in the whole-word model: the single-layer goal-mode theorem |Fast_d| ≤ s + a with a = 0 for a full superconcentrator and **strong** gates (orientation lemma), and its ℓ-layer pigeonhole version (PIEs Theorem 1; "a page to write", `dsag3.md` 5.2) giving zero leak below δ(0.225 n) rounds, **conditional** on a depth-robustness certificate δ(·) for drU d = 6 (attack-calibrated today: 1,055 rounds = 61 µs at 58 ns for 64 KB). Neither statement covers this variant: a single butterfly is not a superconcentrator (adversarially chosen input / output sets have few disjoint paths) and weak gates kill the orientation lemma. Bridging to this variant needs the **linear-connector lemma** (`dsag3.md` 5.3, stated as a conjecture: in a butterfly of invertible 2×2 gates, p marks anywhere in the connector plus q known input coordinates, q below n, derive at most p unmarked In words) and, for the full curve rather than a threshold, the **additive depth profile** conjecture (`dsag3.md` 5.1: ε_depth(D) = max over splits of 1 − Σ m_j / n subject to Σ δ_j(m_j) ≤ D; matches the exact solver on every tested state; proof sketch given, In-mark and connector-mark domination not proved). (iii) Whole-word ⇒ arbitrary state: **PIEs Conjecture 1**, open (the Pietrzak forward-only bridge does not apply: the construction is invertible and the labels are 32 B). (iv) Pebbling ⇒ audit soundness: as P1 (derived).
- **Affordances used.** Deadline (essential), commit-before-seed (essential), RO mixer, connector completeness (every output depends on every input: without it the sub-network attack of `dsag3.md` 3.3 returns; bfly8 at ℓ = 3 leaks 50% at W = 2n). Trusted encoder: not used.
- **Concrete bound at ε\* = 5.26%, δ = 0.01** (all **attack-based**, 64 KB, `dsag3.md` 3.1–3.3, same solver as the P1 rows so the comparison is like for like): protocol A at ρ = 2 (W = 2n): **ε_work = 0**; the cliff (single-drop work = (ℓ − 1) n = 3n) sits one full layer above the ρ = 2 adversary, versus the stack's 0.6% at 2n and 16% at 3n. Protocol B at Δ_p = 100 µs, t_min = 58 ns (D = 1,724): **ε_depth = 0**, so the attacker misses every probe of the 5.26% it omitted and passes k = 1024 / m = 8 with probability ≈ **2.9 × 10^−15** (`curve3.md` 2.4, the "full ε\* missing" column; derived from the binomial). The checkpoint attack frees **4.40% at D = 4096** (238 µs) and everything at 8,188 (475 µs), so the depth Δ\* at which the adversary can free the full 5.26% lies in 238–475 µs at 64 KB (derived from the table), against 130 µs for the stack; at n = 4096 (128 KB): 0 through D = 4096 and 13.4% at 8,192. Additive-profile prediction (**conjectural**): zero leak below 4 δ(n/4) ≈ 3,850 rounds (223 µs) at 64 KB and ≈ 6,100 at 128 KB, four times the pigeonhole bound. Crypto error term: none (RO heuristic).
- **Known gaps.** (1) the linear-connector lemma (5.3); (2) the additive-profile theorem (5.1) or the ℓ-layer pigeonhole with a full Benes connector (which changes the cost row, see P2.3); (3) a certified δ(·) for drU d = 6 (today attack-calibrated); (4) Conjecture 1; (5) mixer RO behaviour beyond tested statistics; (6) the solver's search is heuristic for partial butterflies, so the ℓ = 4 "0" cells for bfly4 / bfly8 are "not found", not "absent" (`dsag3.md` 3.3); the recommended bfly11 has the completeness property that removes the known sub-network attack, but the same caveat about heuristic search applies to every attack number in this section.

## Option P3a: family C, private compression trees over a wide primitive

### P3a.0 Identity

- **Name.** Family C of `research/fam3.md` section 4: single-layer hash-XOR encoding over a macro DAG whose per-word hash of d parents is computed as a private binary tree of wide compression calls; the design text is the "compression tree" variant of the public hash/XOR construction reviewed in `research/public3.md`.
- **Construction.** Words of w = 1,536 B (12,288 bits); block of n words (n = 2048 at the certified complete-block point, 3 MiB). C[i] = W[i] XOR root_i, where root_i is the root of a binary tree of wide calls (each call absorbs two w-byte children plus a 32 B header and outputs w bytes; sponge state 6,208 B) over the labels C[parents(i)] of the d macro parents, parents(i) earlier than i. Decode of word i: read the d parent words of C, evaluate d − 1 wide calls, XOR. Forward-only in the sense that matters for the proof: with one layer, C[i] is computable forward from its parents and W[i], and there is no inverse move that yields a parent from C[i] and W[i]. Encoder: the server (public), key-less or keyed by a public seed after commit.
- **Setting.** Encoder **public**; deadline **required** (the proof is T-round-bounded: the adversary answers within T dependent wide-oracle rounds); data: weights; KV with the P6 caveat.
- **Idealisations.** Wide compression function as a random oracle (2w + 32 → w bytes); the concrete-primitive refinement (a 6 KiB-state permutation as a sponge) is open; T-round model with a weighted-round mapping to a physical Δ still to be written (`public3.md` section 2, item 2).
- **Assumptions.** None beyond the wide RO; parameters at the certified point: retention 18/19, T = 32 rounds, k = 1,024 probes, Q = 2^64 oracle queries, β = 10^−6 (`fam3.md` section 5 row C, `public3.md` section 3).

### P3a.1 Security sketch (gate 1)

- **Target game.** Timed arbitrary-state game: adversary keeps m ≤ (18/19)|C| arbitrary bits, knows W and pp, answers k random-word probes within T dependent oracle rounds. This *is* the template's game with a deadline added, and it is the only public-encoder option (with P3b) that has a proof in it.
- **Reduction chain.** (i) Wide RO ⇒ pebbling-to-arbitrary-state: **proved** by the Pietrzak ITCS 2019 Theorem 7 shape (ex-post-facto codec) as written in the design and checked step by step in `public3.md` section 2 for the lane construction (coverage, freshness and round order, codec length, state quantifier, activation and retries, lane-to-wide reduction: all correct), and transferred to trees by the **owner-projection lemma** (replacing internal tree pebbles by their owners' roots cannot increase the count and preserves availability; `fam3.md` 4.3: reads correctly). (ii) Macro-graph envelope: at the complete-block point (d = n − 1) the envelope is exact and the certified saving is s = 1,861,633,425 bits at the baseline (`public3.md` section 1, re-derived: a = 111, c = 121, e = 12,056, f = 47,931,456, coverage margin 13,911,856; for the indegree-2 tree the recomputed widths are a = 111, c = 112 and the saving 2,293,016,529 bits, `fam3.md` 4.3). For any *sparse* macro graph (d in the tens) the envelope |Fast_T(P)| ≤ |P| + small is **missing**: the window family certifies nothing at the baseline point for any d (needs f ≥ 0.9657 N while d = 2,047 gives at most 0.954 N; `public3.md` section 3). (iii) Concrete primitive: **missing** (sponge bridge in the style of Carolan–Poremba–Zhandry is "the right kind of result"; truncation to w bits and the parallel-round schedule are expected to cost bits, `fam3.md` 4.3). (iv) Oracle rounds ⇒ physical deadline: **missing** (weighted round model; with SHA-256 lanes a late-node round is 0.3–0.5 ms so T = 32 is ≥ 10 ms for any attacker, `public3.md` section 2; with a wide ARX permutation a round is nanoseconds and the mapping must be redone).
- **Affordances used.** Deadline (T rounds, essential: the proof bounds what T rounds of oracle calls can recover, and a public encoder recomputes everything in n rounds); wide RO; W-dependent setup: none. Trusted encoder: not used.
- **Concrete bound** at the certified point (ε\* = 1/19 = 5.26%, k = 1,024, β = 10^−6): a state of m ≤ (18/19)|C| bits answers a full probe batch within T = 32 rounds with probability ≤ 10^−6 — **proved** in the wide-RO model with arbitrary state (`public3.md` section 2; `fam3.md` section 5). Additive floor: none beyond β. Crypto error: none (ideal oracle). Below the complete-block point there is **no bound** (open envelope).
- **Known gaps.** (1) a certified sparse envelope at d = 24–64; (2) the wide-primitive refinement; (3) the weighted round model and Δ; (4) the effect of the 32 B header and truncation on the codec; (5) the tightness of the complete-block envelope for windows (fam3 4.3: a tight envelope would count components exactly).

### P3a.2 Accounting (gate 2)

- **Persistent state.** Rate 1 (C = W XOR labels; the tree is private and transient). Public parameters: graph seed and parameters. Tags: per-word (1.5 KiB words; k = 1,024 probes read 1.5 MiB). Tables: none. Transient: d parent words (3 MiB at the complete point, 36 KiB at d = 24) plus a tree of depth log2 d of 6,208 B states. **Passes 1.05** (derived).
- **Entropy rate.** Zero (public encoder). Not applicable; timed.
- **Block and tile sizes.** Audit block = one word = 1.5 KiB; decode tile = the d-parent neighbourhood (whole 3 MiB block at the complete point). They do not coincide; the audit reads C.

|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2,048 (complete block, certified) | 2,047 | **4,136** | 1,024 | 49,128 |
| 128 | 127 | 257 | 128 | 3,072 |

- Wide primitive: none exists; the closest measured object is the injection-ARX mixer at 0.007 ns·SM per absorbed byte and 0.007 ns·SM per state byte per double round (H100, measured surface). A 6,208 B state needs ≈ log2(194) ≈ 8 more diffusion levels than a 32 B one: **5–10× our per-byte mixing cost (estimated, unmeasured)**. At d = 24: 46 × (5–10) × 0.007 ≈ 1.6–3.2 ns·SM per decoded byte versus memcpy's 0.088 → **≈ 20–40× memcpy, compute-bound, before fusion** (`fam3.md` 4.2, estimated); at the complete point ≈ 90× more, i.e. of order 10^3–10^4× memcpy (derived). int32 instructions per byte: ≈ 240–480 at d = 24 (derived from P1's ≈ 25 per byte at 24 absorbed bytes per byte, scaled by 46/24 and by 5–10); tensor-core MACs 0; HBM bytes 1.0 plus parent reads (in-block SMEM: d bytes per byte, 24 at d = 24, 1,024 at the complete point); serial depth per word log2 d wide-call levels (11 at 2,048; ≈ 5 at 24) times the permutation's round count; working set d × 1.5 KiB plus the tree.
- Honest decode-on-read at the certified point: 4,136 × 14.1 GB ≈ 58 TB of hash input per decode step for Qwen-7B; at a hypothetical 500 GB/s wide-hash rate that is ≈ 2 minutes per step against a 9 ms plaintext step: **≈ 10^4× plaintext** (derived from `public3.md` section 4's method with the tree's 12× advantage).
- **Feasibility position** (D1 §3.8 slope rule and G1's model; **derived / model**, nothing measured for this option). Equivalent int32 vector from the memcpy multiples: d = 24 at 20–40× memcpy is 5.7–11.4× P1's kernel ⇒ **≈ 170–340 int32 per byte**; the certified point (≈ 10^4× plaintext at b = 1) corresponds to ≈ 6 × 10^4 int32 per byte (R − 1 = 0.158 X). Decode phase (b ≤ 256): d = 24 ≈ 1.07 + 0.158 × (170–340) ≈ **28–55×**; certified point ≈ 10^4×: **nothing fits**. Decode-then-GEMM at large b (measured drU excess scaled linearly in X): d = 24 at b = 4,096: 1.14 + 0.274 × (170–340)/30 = 2.7–4.2; at 8,192: 1.09 + 0.134 × (170–340)/30 = **1.85–2.6**; so **+100% at b ≈ 7,000 (X = 170, interpolated) to ≈ 13,000 (X = 340, extrapolated beyond the 8,192 sweep)**, i.e. only in the largest prefill chunks; **+10% never**. Certified point: the excess at 8,192 would be ≈ 270, and the 1/b fall above the ridge puts +100% at b of order 10^6 tokens per pass (derived; G1's model marks decoders of this t_dec "no / no" for decode and prefill, `region.md` §5.1). *Previous estimate (flat-delta arithmetic): +100% at B in the hundreds at 118K, prefill +100% at ≈ 30K-token prefills, +10% never.* Only with d ≤ 8–16 *and* a wide permutation at ≈ 2× our per-byte cost would the option land in P1's band (4–6×), which is what `fam3.md` and `public3.md` mean by "cost near our phase-2 kernel with a proof". **KV**: the encoding applies to KV pages as to weights (a KV page is a block), with the P6 nesting caveat; at 1.5 KiB words and 3 MiB blocks it does not match the 64 KB KV page.
- Reference points: plaintext 1.00×; P1 3.42× memcpy measured; this option 20–40× memcpy at d = 24 (estimated) and ≈ 10^4× at the certified point (derived).

| attack class | best known | fraction saved / cost | accounted for by a proof? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arbitrary-state compression (any strategy) | – | ≤ (18/19)|C| retained cannot answer within T = 32 rounds except with probability ≤ 10^−6 | **yes**, at the complete-block point (wide RO) |

| Whole-word deletion / partial words / cached keys | – | inside the proof's arbitrary state | yes |

### P3a.5 Verdict

- **Status: proved-not-priced at the certified point; open below it.** The only public-encoder option with an arbitrary-state proof (with P3b), at a cost 10^4× plaintext; twelve times cheaper than P3b at every degree; a proved sparse envelope and a measured wide permutation are the two things that would price it.
- **Probability estimates.** ≤ 2× at low batch with a complete proof: **≈ 1%** (needs d ≤ 16 certified, a wide permutation at near our per-byte cost, the sponge bridge and the Δ mapping: four independent results, two of them against current evidence). Landing in P1's 4–6× band with a proof: ≈ 5% (the outcome that would matter to the chart). ≤ 1.1×: **≈ 0**.
- **What would change the verdict.** (a) A certified |Fast_T(P)| ≤ |P| + small for a macro graph of degree 24–64 (theory); (b) a full-width primitive refinement (sponge bridge with truncation and parallel rounds); (c) a measured 6 KiB-state permutation priced per byte on H100 (`fam3.md`: "the cheapest experiment with real information gain"); (d) the weighted round model. (a) + (b) + (c) at favourable values → proved and priced at roughly P1's cost.
- **Pointers.** `research/fam3.md` sections 4–5, `research/public3.md` sections 2–5 and `research/public3/calc.py`, the design text's Appendix B (owner projection), Pietrzak ITCS 2019 Theorem 7, Carolan–Poremba–Zhandry (sponge indifferentiability).

## Option P3b: the public hash/XOR complete-block encoding (48 lanes)

### P3b.0 Identity

- **Name.** The "public encoding" of the theory handoff reviewed in `research/public3.md`: complete-block hash-XOR with lane-parallel 256-bit compression.
- **Construction.** Words of w = 1,536 B = 48 lanes × 32 B; block of n = 2,048 words (3 MiB); C[i] = W[i] XOR H_lanes(C[1..i−1]) where each of the 48 lanes hashes all i − 1 earlier words of the block (Merkle–Damgård, e.g. SHA-256). Every word depends on every earlier word (d = n − 1 = 2,047 average parents ≈ 1,023.5). Decode of word i: hash all earlier words through 48 lanes, XOR. Encoder: the server (public).
- **Setting.** Encoder **public**; deadline **required** (T = 32 dependent rounds); data: weights (KV would need 3 MiB pages).
- **Idealisations.** Lane function as a random oracle; lane-to-wide reduction (each lane query maps to ≤ 1 wide query and 1 round; correct per `public3.md`).
- **Assumptions.** Retention 18/19, T = 32, k = 1,024, Q = 2^64, L = 2^46 (proof device), β = 10^−6.

### P3b.1 Security sketch (gate 1)

- **Target game.** Timed arbitrary-state game as P3a.
- **Reduction chain.** Pietrzak-style four-step proof, **checked correct** in `public3.md` section 2 (coverage ρ^k and exp(−L(β − ρ^k)) union over ≤ 2^N sets; freshness and round order; fixed-length codec D·w − s; state quantifier paid inside the m bits; activation and retries with fail-closed at the attempt cap). Finite point re-derived independently: a = 111, c = 121, e = 12,056, f = 47,931,456, **s = 1,861,633,425 bits saved**, coverage margin 13,911,856 (`public3/calc.py baseline`). Checker reproduces the handoff's expected JSON byte for byte (81,700 graph cases, 6,976 codec round trips, 3,488 payload round trips, 256 coverage cases). Concrete-primitive refinement and physical-deadline mapping open (as P3a).
- **Affordances used.** Deadline (T rounds); RO lanes. Trusted encoder: not used.
- **Concrete bound.** As P3a at the certified point: retaining 18/19 of |C| answers a batch within T = 32 rounds with probability ≤ 10^−6, **proved** (lane RO). Structural fact behind the width: positivity of s needs (f/N)(1 − (a + c)/w) to exceed m/C; with a + c = 232 pointer bits per charged word and f/N = 31/32 this forces **w above ≈ 10,500 bits, hence 48 lanes**; smaller Q, L help ≤ 1.5× (Q = 2^40, L = 2^30 gives a + c = 153, w above ≈ 7,000); retention is the strong lever, w scales like 232 / (f/N − m/C) (`public3.md` section 2, item 1; derived).
- **Known gaps.** Primitive refinement; weighted round model (roots and early nodes are orders of magnitude faster than late nodes).

### P3b.2 Accounting (gate 2)

Rate 1; pp and tags negligible; transient: the whole block (3 MiB) per decoded word at the late nodes; passes 1.05. Entropy zero (public). Audit block = word = 1.5 KiB (k = 1,024 → 1.2–1.5 MiB per audit); decode tile = whole block.

### P3b.3 Symbolic cost (gate 3)

- **Hash-input bytes per decoded byte = lanes × average parents: 49,128 at the baseline** (48 × 1,023.5). Cheapest certified points (`public3/calc.py sweep`, exact re-verification of every point; `public3.md` section 3): 39,916 at 18/19 / T = 32 / k = 1,024 (n = 2,048, 1,248 B words, 39 lanes); 25,575 at k = 4,096 (6.3 MiB blocks); 9,964 at T = 8; **1,785–2,040 at 0.90 retention, T = 8** (n = 256, 448–512 B words); no certified point at 18/19 with k = 256. Window (bounded-indegree) family: certifies nothing at the baseline for any d (`calc.py window`).
- Full decode pass of 76 GB at the baseline: **3.7 PB of hash input** ≈ 20 core-days at SHA-NI rates of 1.5–2.5 GB/s (typical, not measured) or 2–10 hours on one GPU at an assumed 100–500 GB/s SHA-256 (to be measured; never was, because no kernel result changes the gap); plaintext read of 76 GB at 3.35 TB/s = 23 ms → **10^5–10^6× slower than plaintext**; at the cheapest 0.90-retention point 136 TB per pass, 5–20 minutes on one GPU, still **10^4×** (`public3.md` section 4, derived with stated assumptions).
- Merkle–Damgård sequentiality: a late-node lane call processes ≈ 1.5 MB per lane through ≈ 24,000 dependent compression steps, 0.3–0.5 ms per dependent round on any hardware; good for the T-to-Δ mapping (T = 32 late rounds ≥ 10 ms for any attacker), fatal for honest decode (`public3.md` section 2, item 2).
- int32 / TC / HBM columns: not meaningful at this scale (compute-bound by four to six orders of magnitude). Serial depth per word: 48 parallel lanes × ≈ 24,000 dependent compression steps at late nodes.
- **Feasibility position** (D1 §3.8 slope rule; **derived**). 10^5–10^6× plaintext at b = 1 corresponds to an equivalent vector of ≈ 6 × 10^5 to 6 × 10^6 int32 per byte (R − 1 = 0.158 X); even at b = 8,192, where the measured drU excess per int32 per byte is 0.0045, R_seq would be ≈ 3,000–30,000. **Fits nothing at any batch, decode or prefill**; +100% would need b of order 10^7–10^8 tokens per pass (derived from the 1/b fall above the ridge; G1's model classes MW-DCR at 6 × 10^6× as "never", `region.md` §5.1, and P3b sits in the same decade). *Previous estimate: same verdict, stated without a batch figure.* KV: no (3 MiB pages, same cost, b = 1 column).

### P3b.4 Attacks

As P3a at the complete-block point: every strategy is inside the proof's arbitrary state (proved, lane RO). No primitive-level attack because no primitive is fixed. Nesting for KV: applies in principle; moot.

### P3b.5 Verdict

- **Status: closed for serving** (proved; cost structurally prohibitive; the two levers that could fix it, a wide-state primitive absorbing the parents once (≈ 48× saving) and a proved sparse envelope (≈ 1,000× saving), turn it into P3a below the complete point). Kept in the chart as the reference "proved-not-priced" row.
- **Probability estimates.** ≤ 2× or ≤ 1.1× as written: **0** (structural). As the parent of P3a: see P3a.5.
- **What would change the verdict.** Nothing at the level of engineering; only the two theory levers (then it is P3a).
- **Pointers.** `research/public3.md`, `research/public3/calc.py`, `research/public3/screen.csv`, the theory handoff's proof package and `expected-public-encoding-proof-checks.json`.

## Option P4: PIEs as written (Dagwood sandwich, full superconcentrators, PRP gates)

### P4.0 Identity

- **Name.** Fisch's Proofs of Replication (PIEs, "Tight Proofs of Space and Replication", 2019): the Dagwood Sandwich Graph with ℓ keyed depth-robust layers connected by B_k superconcentrators; ported to the harness as `butterfly` / "independent copies (PIEs)" in `research/dsag3.md` section 3.
- **Construction.** ℓ layers of a depth-robust graph (PIEs: independent unmirrored DRG copies; the harness also ran our ZigZag-mirrored drU d = 6 layers), 32 B labels, block of n words; between consecutive layers a **full superconcentrator** B_k realised as two back-to-back butterflies (a Benes network, 2 log2 n = 22 stages at n = 2048) of 2-in / 2-out **public-key PRP gates**, free to evaluate in either direction and with **no partial recovery** (an ideal permutation on the 64 B pair). PIEs' recommended point is **ℓ = 2**. Decode: invert the top layer, run the Benes backwards (22 stages), invert the next layer, and so on; whole-block only.
- **Setting.** Encoder **public** (this is a proof of replication with a public key); deadline **required**: PIEs' security is a bound on the parallel time (rounds) an adversary needs to answer from a small state, so a challenge deadline is part of the definition; data: weights (KV with the P6 caveat).
- **Idealisations.** Random oracle for the layer hash; ideal PRP (block cipher) for the gates; **Conjecture 1** for the passage from pebbling to arbitrary state; a depth-robustness certificate (α, β) for the base DRG.
- **Assumptions.** None concrete beyond the idealisations; the DRG certificate is the load-bearing input.

### P4.1 Security sketch (gate 1)

- **Target game.** Timed arbitrary-state game (PIEs' definition: state of s labels' worth, answer within d parallel rounds).
- **Reduction chain.** (i) Pebbling theorem, **proved** in the whole-word model: the goal-mode session's two-layer statement |Fast_d| ≤ s + a (orientation lemma, Menger connector lemma, case split on the marked finals, "individually fast implies jointly fast"), checked sound step by step in `dsag3.md` section 1, and with a = 0 for a full superconcentrator; PIEs' Theorem 1 gives the ℓ-layer version by pigeonhole (some layer has ≤ s/ℓ marks). The key-width condition (a 32 B key per 32 B node, the Davies–Meyer feed-forward) is necessary: the N = 16 counterexample with six stored one-word keys shows the theorem fails without it. (ii) Input to the theorem: d = δ(s/ℓ), the base graph's depth after removing the marked fraction; **no proved δ exists for drU d = 6** (we have S1's depth attacks, upper bounds on the true δ; `graphs2/depth2_*.csv`), so every window below is **attack-calibrated**. (iii) Pebbling ⇒ arbitrary state: **Conjecture 1** (open since 2019; `dsag3.md` 1, 5.4). (iv) Gates: the orientation lemma needs "no partial recovery" from the gate, i.e. an ideal PRP; a cheap ARX or linear gate voids the lemma (the theorem, not the attacks: `dsag3.md` 3.3, 5.3).
- **Affordances used.** Deadline (essential), RO layer hash, ideal PRP gates, W-independent public key. Trusted encoder: not used.
- **Concrete bound** (whole-word model, conditional on δ, `dsag3.md` section 2, retention 90%): with ℓ layers and s = 0.9 n stored words some layer has ≤ s/ℓ marks, so the certified depth is δ(s/ℓ):

| ℓ | removal s/ℓ | δ at n = 2048 (64 KB) | δ at n = 4096 (128 KB) | window at t_min = 58 ns (CPU) / 288 ns (GPU), 64 KB | 128 KB |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
| 2 (PIEs' point) | 45% | ≈ 260 | ≈ 350 | 15 / 75 µs | 20 / 101 µs |
| 3 | 30% | 764 | 1,199 | 44 / 220 µs | 70 / 345 µs |
| 4 | 22.5% | ≈ 1,055 | ≈ 1,818 | 61 / 304 µs | 105 / 524 µs |
| 5 | 18% | ≈ 1,240 | ≈ 2,320 | 72 / 357 µs | 135 / 668 µs |

  So PIEs' two-layer point certifies a regeneration window an order of magnitude too short for any network deadline at 64–128 KB (Δ_p = 50–100 µs CPU, 100–300 µs GPU, `spotcheck3.md` 4.2); to certify 100 µs against a 58 ns adversary with ℓ = 2 needs n ≈ 30k words (≈ 1 MB blocks); ℓ = 4 lands where the deadlines are (61 µs at 64 KB, 105 µs at 128 KB). All **conditional** on δ(·) being the true depth-robustness (attack-calibrated today) and on Conjecture 1 for anything beyond whole words. Additive-profile prediction (**conjectural**, `dsag3.md` 5.1): the exact threshold is 4 δ(n/4) ≈ 3,850 rounds at ℓ = 4, four times the pigeonhole value; the pigeonhole bound is what is provable today.
- **Known gaps.** (1) a proved δ for the base graph (or a DRG with a known certificate at n = 2048–8192, which changes the cost); (2) Conjecture 1; (3) the ℓ-layer pigeonhole written in the whole-word model with key-width charging ("a page", `dsag3.md` 5.2); (4) a concrete block cipher for the gates and the RO / ideal-cipher instantiation gap.

### P4.2 Accounting (gate 2)

Rate 1 (connectors are permutations; keys as wide as words are not stored). Public parameters: layer key, gate key, graph seed. Tags as P1. Transient: one block in SMEM plus the Benes tile (4–5 SMEM passes with register-resident low-stride stages). **Passes 1.05** (derived). Entropy zero (public). Audit blocks as P1 (C is read); decode tile = whole block.

Decode phase (b ≤ 256), fused: R_seq ≈ 1.07 + 0.158 × (126–150) ≈ **21–25×** (synthetic scale; ≈ 17–20× rescaled to the real kernel): **fits nothing**. - Reference points: plaintext 1.00×; P1 3.42× memcpy (measured); P2 4.4–4.6× (estimated); this option 15–17× (estimated, ℓ = 4).

- **Status: proved-not-priced** (whole-word model, conditional on δ and Conjecture 1) at ℓ = 4 with strong gates: 15–17× memcpy estimated; at PIEs' own ℓ = 2 point: priced at ≈ 1.8× P1 but the certified window (15 µs at 64 KB) is below every honest tail. **Closed for serving** as written.
- **Probability estimates.** ≤ 2× with a complete proof: **≈ 0.5%**; ≤ 1.1×: **≈ 0**.
- **What would change the verdict.** The linear-connector lemma (`dsag3.md` 5.3): if it holds, the strong PRP gates are unnecessary and PIEs' theorem transfers to P2's weak bfly11 (or weak Benes at 1.5–2.3× P1), collapsing P4 into P2. A DRG with a proved (α, β) at n = 2048–8192 at in-degree ≤ 6 would remove the δ condition for P2 and P4 alike.
- **Pointers.** Fisch 2019 (PIEs, Theorem 1, Conjecture 1, B_k); `research/dsag3.md` sections 1–4; the goal-mode write-up `main-security-proof.md` (checked in `dsag3.md` 1); `spotcheck3/dsag_game.py`; `research/graphs2/depth2_extra.csv`, `depth2_big.csv` (the δ grid).

## Option P5: the sparse-graph direction (fewer parents per decoded word)

### P5.0 Identity

- **Name.** Not a construction: the design direction "cut the per-decoded-word cost below the (4, 6) stack's ℓ·d = 24 parent reads and 4 mixer evaluations" by fewer layers, fewer parents, or register-resident windows (PLAN4 3.1's plan construction #1; `graphs2.md` reconciliation section 5; `spotcheck3.md`; `fam3.md` section 3).
- **Candidates on the table.** (a) ℓ = 2 or 3 stacks with drU d = 4–6 (measured kernels exist: (2, 6, 3) fused decode step 2.34× at 64 KB and 2.10–2.16× at 32 KB, B = 8 / 16, harness; (2, 6, 4) in vLLM 4.77× at B = 1 / 30K; (3, 4, 3) in vLLM 5.28× at B = 1 / 30K; `fused2.md`, `serving2.md`, measured); (b) ℓ = 4 with drU d = 5 (20 SMEM B/B instead of 24); (c) the window family `winW64L2u` (a dense window of 64 predecessors plus two long-range parents at **uniform** distance) at ℓ = 4: 12.8 SMEM B/B, 2.25× less fetch than bucket d = 6, kernel **not built** (the window must be register-resident for the fetch model to charge it zero; its injection cost into the mixer state is unpriced); (d) a single-layer forward-only hash-XOR over a sparse macro graph with narrow words (the P3a structure at 32 B words).
- **Setting.** Encoder **public**; deadline **required**; data: weights and KV.
- **Idealisations / assumptions.** As P1 (RO mixer, whole-word model, t_min); plus whichever theorem the direction would rest on (below).

### P5.1 Security sketch (gate 1): what is known, what would have to be proved

- **Known, exact (attack-based, whole-word model):** (i) **ℓ = 2 is out for every sampler**: ε_max = W/(2n) exactly in the encode-only game for any graph (phase-1 law, reproduced exactly in `graphs2.md`: 0.2441 at W = 1000, n = 2048), and 50% at W = n / all at 2n in the bidirectional game (`dsag3.md` 3.2, identity ℓ = 2); with a full butterfly connector ℓ = 2 still leaks 50% at W = 2n (P4). (ii) **No ℓ = 3 graph passes ε ≤ 1% at W = 2n**: drU d = 6 leaks 0.98% at W = n and **13.7%** at 2n; bucket d = 6 2.1% / 19.5%; drU d = 5 1.5% / 19.5%; the window `winW64L2u` 0.59% / 2.9% (the only ℓ = 3 family below 1% at W = n; its dropped labels re-encode in bulk at ≈ 3 hashes each once the cascade saturates) (`graphs2.md` reconciliation table 3, bidirectional, DM feed-forward on). At ℓ = 3 the leak is set by the effective degree (blocked-cascade size), not by the tail of the distance distribution. (iii) **ℓ = 4 with fewer parents:** drU d = 5 leaks 0.39% / **1.56%** at W = n / 2n (fails the 2n criterion; passes if W_max ≈ n); `winW64L2u` at ℓ = 4: **0 / 0.44%**, single-drop 5,637 / 6,010 / 6,144 (min / median / max), 12.8 B/B: the one sparse point that meets the attack criterion, at the cost of an unbuilt kernel. (iv) **Without the DM feed-forward no ℓ ≤ 5 graph is sound** (12–28% at ℓ = 3, W = n; ≈ 1% at ℓ = 5). (v) Depth regime, ℓ = 4: the window family has the best depth robustness per byte (α 0.70 for winW64L2u vs 0.42 for bucket d = 6 at ℓ = 1); the stacked-depth numbers at ℓ = 4 for the window are not in the ε_depth tables of `spotcheck3.md` (drU only).
- **What would have to be proved for any sparse point to be more than attack-based** (the list this section exists for):
  1. **A lower bound, not an attack, on bidirectional whole-word pebbling cost** for an ℓ-layer invertible stack with in-degree d and the decode rule available: the quantity ε_work(W) and ε_depth(D) of `spotcheck3.md`. Today there is none for any (ℓ, d); S3 conjectures a ≤ 2× slack on the attack values (`graphs2.md` caveats). For ℓ = 2 the exact attack shows no bound above W/(2n) exists, so the direction is ℓ ≥ 3 by theorem, and ℓ = 3 needs ε_work(2n) ≤ 1% which no measured graph attains; hence a sound sparse point is **ℓ = 4 with cheaper gathers**, not fewer layers.
  2. **A depth-robustness certificate (α, β) for the sampler at n = 2048–8192** (drU d = 5–6 or the window family), i.e. a proof of δ(·) rather than S1's depth attacks; `curve3.md` section 4: "attack searches are the wrong kind of evidence"; Blocki et al.'s log-indegree constructions are not needed at these n but come with certificates the drU sampler lacks.
  3. **The arbitrary-state bridge for an invertible stack at 32 B labels**: Conjecture 1 or a replacement. The forward-only route (Pietrzak Theorem 7) is closed for narrow words: its pointer loss is ≈ 2 log2(N(Q + 1)) ≈ 150 bits per label, which at 32 B labels certifies nothing above ≈ 41% retention (`dsag3.md` 5.4) and at 18/19 retention forces w above ≈ 10,500 bits (P3b), i.e. the wide words that make P3a expensive. So "sparse and narrow" has **no proof route today**, and "sparse and wide" is P3a with the open envelope.
  4. **For the window family specifically**: that a register-resident window is realisable at the modelled cost (kernel), and that the fetch model's zero charge for the window survives the mixer's absorption cost (66 words injected per node-layer versus 6; unpriced).
  5. **For P3a-style sparse single layers**: the envelope |Fast_T(P)| ≤ |P| + small for a degree-24–64 macro graph (the window envelope of the design certifies nothing at d ≤ 2,047; `public3.md` section 3).
- **Affordances.** As P1. **Concrete bound:** none beyond the attack table above; the best sound-looking sparse point (`winW64L2u`, ℓ = 4) has ε_work = 0 / 0.44% at W = n / 2n (attack-based, 40-target single-drop sample), unpriced.

Per layer: 0.200 per parent gather, 0.221 per double round, a fixed 2.37 per block. ## Option P6: KV under a public encoder and under a trusted encoder

### P6.0 Identity

- **Name.** The KV branch: the server seals KV-cache pages on the fly under a replica (slot) key as they fill, declares them as part of its resident volume, and decodes them on read in the attention path (`research/kv2.md`, W1; `research/curve3.md` section 3). Under a trusted encoder, KV is **out of scope** by decision (`research/TEAM4.md`, PLAN4 sections 0–1): the server is the encoder of its own KV.
- **Construction (public encoder).** KV page = one block of the P1 stack: **64 KB, n = 2048 words, chunk 32** (the smallest size meeting n ≥ 2048; P = 64 tokens of one layer's K or V for Qwen2.5-7B; 56 KB of KV per token across layers); sealed on a side stream when the page fills (staging copy + event, joined at the end of the step), the two newest pages per (sequence, layer) unencoded and excluded from challenges; decoded per step into a scratch consumed by attention (FlashAttention or SDPA); shared-prefix pages decoded once per step with an LSE merge (cascade); prefix-cache hits re-keyed from the portable pool key to the slot key. Encoder: the server (public); admission of a page = a new declaration.
- **Setting.** Public encoder, deadline required (as P1), data = KV, or weights + KV. Trusted encoder: not applicable (below).
- **Idealisations / assumptions.** As P1 per page. Plus the **admission model**: the verifier cannot check a KV page's plaintext against anything (it is the model's state on private prompts), so the server chooses the plaintext freely.

### P6.1 Security sketch (gate 1): what is and is not possible

**Under a public encoder.**

- Per page, security is exactly P1's: timed, whole-word, attack-based (ε_work 0.59% at W = 2n; ε_depth envelopes; Δ\* = 130 / 367 µs at 64 / 128 KB against t_min = 58 ns; `spotcheck3.md`, `curve3.md`). Pages are blocks; nothing changes at the encoding level. The plaintext exposure (partial page plus one in-flight page per sequence per layer, ≤ 127 tokens, 0.1–0.4% of a 30K–118K context) is data the adversary holds for free and the challenge protocol excludes (`kv2.md`, measured / derived).
- **The nesting attack** (`curve3.md` 3.1; attack, exhibited by argument, not run on hardware): the server admits a KV page j whose plaintext is an existing replica page, D_j := R_i (weights or KV), stores R_j = Enc(key, j, R_i), discards R_i. Probes on j: served from R_j. Probes on i: R_i[w] = Dec(key, j, R_j)[w] in ℓ = 4 dependent rounds (≈ 0.25 µs at CPU t_min; 490 GB/s in bulk on an H100). Storage one page, declared two; chained, m − 1 pages freed per m admitted at depth (m − 1) ℓ. Under protocol A the attacker decodes ε × 4 GB at 490 GB/s = 0.4 ms for ε = 5%, inside the 1.2 ms read; under protocol B far below any Δ. **Nothing at the encoding level fixes it**: the honest decoder is a public deterministic function of (R_j, metadata) and a correct encoder must encode every plaintext, R_i included. Per-page nonces make duplicate *plaintexts* cost a full re-encode but do nothing for D_j = R_i; a Fiat–Shamir pad PRF(key, j, H(D_j)) is a public function of committed data; a "plausible bf16" filter is defeated by embedding R_i in mantissas at 2× expansion (occupancy still 67%); comparing decoded pages with stored replicas is defeated by any public invertible map (`curve3.md` 3.1).
- **Remedy A: epoch re-keying** (`curve3.md` 3.2). Fresh replica key each epoch; every replica (weights and KV) re-encoded under it. A nest made in epoch t is dead at the boundary: a probe on page i under key_(t+1) needs Enc(key_(t+1), i, Dec(key_t, i, R_i)), a full ℓ(n − 1)-round chain, i.e. the ordinary regeneration bound. Within an epoch the attacker frees at most the bytes admitted in that epoch, so the occupancy claim degrades to (declared − admitted-this-epoch) / M; policy: re-key when admitted-since-last-rekey ≥ δ·M with δ the occupancy slack (5.26% minus ε_depth at the chosen Δ; ≈ 4 GB per H100 at 128 KB blocks, ≈ 2.5 GB at 64 KB). Prefix-cache hits count as admissions. **Cost** (derived from the measured encoder): re-key is encode-bound and in place (64 KB scratch, no double space), measured **6.3 GB/s at (3, 4, 3) / 64 KB** (`kv2.md` item 6), estimated 4–6 GB/s at (4, 6, 4): **13–20 s of GPU time per 80 GB per epoch**, plus a per-page epoch bit the tap must know during the transition. Epoch length is set by the KV admission rate (Qwen2.5-7B, 56 KB per token, 4 GB slack): decode-dominated agentic B = 8 at 240 tok/s: 13 MB/s admitted, epoch 300 s, **4–7% of GPU time**; agentic with a 30K prefill per turn per minute: 0.23 GB/s, epoch 18 s, **75–115%**; prefill-saturated (one 30K prompt per second): 1.7 GB/s, epoch 2.4 s, 540–830% (**infeasible**); 72B on 8 GPUs decode-dominated: 3–5%; 72B at one 30K prompt per second: infeasible. A faster encoder helps linearly with a ceiling of 3–4× (on-chip memory for blocks in flight: ≈ 1000 64 KB blocks → 15–20 GB/s). **Affordable for decode-dominated serving; unaffordable wherever prefill admits more than ≈ 0.1 GB/s of KV per GPU** (all derived, `curve3.md` 3.2).
- **Remedy B: computation integrity** (`curve3.md` 3.3; not built, not specified). If KV pages are certified as the declared model's output on declared tokens (a verified-computation-graph layer), the server cannot choose a page equal to a replica (it would have to steer a transformer's KV to a target string). With B, remedy A is unnecessary and the storage-to-answer question reduces to the single-block bound. Cost and design of B are open; it is a different research programme.
- **Remedy D (the default): weights-only PoUS.** Declare and encode only the weights; KV stays plaintext and uncounted. **Nesting-free as it stands**: weights cannot nest weights because the replica key is drawn after comm_D (commit-before-seed, `protocol2.md`), and model updates are admissions like KV (`curve3.md` 3.3). This is the "model weight residency" application.
- **What is possible with a public encoder (summary):** weights-only PoUS with no admission problem; KV PoUS with epoch re-keying in decode-dominated regimes (4–7% GPU time, occupancy claim degraded by admitted-this-epoch / M); KV PoUS with computation integrity if it is ever built; measured KV serving at 2.71× (session, B = 8) with the systems tricks of `kv2.md`. **What is not possible:** an encoding-level fix for nesting; KV PoUS in prefill-heavy regimes without computation integrity; any non-timed (entropic) KV guarantee (the server holds the key; the entropy of the KV encoding given its plaintext is zero); a KV occupancy claim that survives an 8 ns circuit inside the boundary (as P1).

**Under a trusted encoder.**

- **Not possible: a KV occupancy claim.** The server produces KV at runtime and is therefore the encoder of its own KV; the trusted-encoder model (encode once, erase secrets; PLAN4 section 1) does not apply to data generated by the party being audited. A trusted party that encodes what the server sends would have to be online at the admission bandwidth (13 MB/s to 9.6 GB/s per GPU or node, `curve3.md` 3.2) and **would still encode the nest**: Moran–Wichs encodings are incompressible for *any* fixed message, and the nesting attack is not a compression of a message but a choice of message; the trusted encoder would have to refuse pages equal to a public invertible function of an existing replica, which it cannot decide (`curve3.md` 3.4, "Remedy C, rejected"). Recorded as a scope decision in phase 4: the trusted-encoder path covers **static weights only** (`TEAM4.md`, PLAN4 section 0).
- **Not possible without extra machinery: the hybrid** (trusted-encoder weights, public-encoder KV with a KV occupancy claim). Derived here, not in any memo: a KV page can nest a *weight* replica block (D_j := the MW-encoded weight block R_i); the weight block is then answered by decoding the public KV encoding of it in ℓ rounds (the raw-block audit's 1.0 ms deadline over k = 207 blocks of 32 KB is 6.6 MB, decoded in ≈ 14 µs at 490 GB/s). Epoch re-keying of the KV branch does not kill this nest, because the weight encoding never changes (the trusted encoder's secrets are erased; re-encoding the weights needs the trusted party again). So the hybrid needs computation integrity for KV, or periodic trusted re-encoding of the weights, or it must not count KV.
- **Possible: weights-only guarantees, unaffected by KV.** The G1–G7 path (HILL entropy of the weight replica given W, raw-block audit, tap rule) is about the weight replica; the server may keep any KV it likes in plaintext, and plaintext KV has no effect on the weight claim (the audit reads raw blocks of the weight replica under a deadline the KV does not touch except through HBM contention, which `audit.md` measured under load). Encoded KV under the P1 stack may still be run as a *systems* feature (confidentiality of the cache at rest, side-channel hygiene) without any occupancy claim on it; its 2.71× cost is then paid for no PoUS benefit.

- **Known gaps.** The nesting attack has not been run on hardware (it is a two-line argument; nothing about it is uncertain). Remedy A's cost is derived from a measured encoder at (3, 4, 3), estimated at (4, 6, 4). Remedy B has no design. The epoch transition (accept either key for pages in the announced transition set) is unspecified in protocol form.

### P6.2 Accounting (gate 2)

- **Persistent state.** Rate 1 per page (an encoded page replaces the plaintext page; 64 KB). Public parameters: slot key per replica, epoch bit per page under remedy A (1 bit per 64 KB, negligible). Tags: as P1 (0.025% for protocol A; per-word paths for B). **Plaintext window**: ≤ 2 pages per (sequence, layer) unencoded (≤ 127 tokens), excluded from the claim; W = 1–4 plaintext pages saves nothing measurable (below 1%), W = 64 pages recovers only 1–3% (measured, `kv2.md`). Transient: one layer of decoded KV per step in HBM (1.9 GB at B = 8 / 118K, 1/28 of the pool) or the fused attention scratch; one 64 KB staging copy per page in flight. **Total persistent ≤ 1.0003 |KV| declared; passes 1.05.** Under remedy A: no double space (in-place re-key, 64 KB scratch).
- **Entropy rate.** Zero given the plaintext and the key (public encoder). Timed security only.
- **Block and tile sizes.** Page = block = decode tile = 64 KB (128 KB: +19% on the decode step, measured; no security need at KV volumes, `kv2.md`). Audit blocks as P1; coincide with the page for protocol A's 32 KB sectors only up to a factor 2.

**Batch and context effects.** The KV decode cost is proportional to context × batch (unlike the flat weight delta), so the KV-only ratio *rises* with context (turn-1 2.43× → turn-30 4.74×) and does not fall with batch: the plaintext step is itself KV-read-bound at ≈ 1.9 TB/s against a 438–491 GB/s decoder, giving a 3.7–4.1× per-step floor at 90–118K "whatever the systems layer does" (`kv2.md`). The two levers left are a cheaper mixer (no 2-round mixer has cleared cryptanalysis) and fusing decode into the attention kernel (removes the scratch write and re-read: ≤ 32% of the overhead); with both plus the tricks, ≤ 1.8× at τ = 1 is "plausible", without a cheaper decoder it is not (`kv2.md`).

**Feasibility position: the KV budget** (D1 `decoder.md` §3.8, "the KV-shaped case", **derived from measured pipe rates**; G1 `region.md` §7 as the model). A KV byte is read once per generated token by the one sequence that owns it, so the KV read never gets the batch amortisation that turns the weight GEMM compute-bound: it sits in the b = 1 regime of the region forever, streamed at 2.5–3.0 TB/s by the attention kernel (0.33–0.40 ps per byte) against 0.060 ps per int32 op per byte and 2.0–2.4 fs per int8 MAC. **Serialised: R = 1 + 0.15–0.18 X, so X ≤ 0.55–0.67 int32 per KV byte (or Y ≤ 14–20 int8 MACs, or Z ≤ 0.10 extra bytes) at R ≤ 1.10, and X ≤ 5.5–6.7 int32 (Y ≤ 140–200 MACs, Z ≤ 1.0 bytes) at R ≤ 2.0; perfectly overlapped in a fused attention kernel: X ≤ 6–7 at 1.10 and 11–13 at 2.0 (Y ≤ 150–200 / 280–400).** D1's measured b = 1 weight row (0.38 / 8 int32, 7.8 / 202 MACs, 0.02 / 0.86 bytes with a 1.07× kernel) is the empirical anchor between the two bounds; G1's calibrated fused model gives 1.3 int32 or 15 MACs at +10% and 9.8 int32 or 260 MACs at 2× (model). Two KV-specific constraints on top: the decode tile must be a KV page or page group (4–16 KB per head, 32–256 KB across heads), and an option that only fits at prefill batch cannot be used for KV at all; an unfused design (decode to scratch, then attention) is ≥ 3× for any decoder because the scratch round trip is two extra bytes per byte (G1 §7). **What this means for the drU KV path:** the stack is ≈ 30 int32 per byte (D1's counting rule), i.e. **R ≈ 5.5–6.4 per KV byte serialised** (5.8 on the synthetic weight vector at b = 1, measured), 45–55× over the +10% budget and 4.5–5.5× over the +100% budget; the measured per-byte KV decode of 438–491 GB/s against attention streaming at ≈ 1.9 TB/s (3.9–4.3 read-times per byte, `kv2.md`) is **consistent with ≈ 30 int32 per byte at M = 1**, and the **KV-only session 2.71×** (turn-30 TPOT 4.74× at 118K) is that per-byte figure diluted by the plaintext weight pass and the prefill in the session mix, not a sign that KV amortises. Consequences: **no drU-class KV encoding fits +100% or +10% at any batch**; the only class inside 2× for KV is a keystream XOR against a precomputed mask (ChaCha8 at 4.1 TB/s: R ≈ 1.4 serialised, ≈ 1.0 overlapped, D1 §3.5, §3.8), which is exactly what a public-key encoder cannot make secure (the server holds the key). Phase-2 criteria for the record: ≤ 1.8× at B = 8 not met with systems tricks alone (2.42× at (3, 4, 3), 2.71× at (4, 6, 3)), met only in the speculative column against a non-speculating baseline (1.15–1.26×; 1.73–1.88× against a speculating one); under remedy A add 4–7% in the decode-dominated regime. *Previous estimate (kv2's per-step floor): 3.7–4.1× per step at 90–118K "whatever the systems layer does", ≤ 1.8× at τ = 1 plausible with a cheaper mixer plus fusion; the region budget replaces this: fusion removes at most the scratch round trip (the ≥ 3× unfused floor), and a cheaper mixer would have to reach ≈ 6 int32 per byte for 2×, five times below the current 30.* **Prefill**: the 30K-turn prefill pays KV sealing (3.7 ms per page, hidden on the side stream) and, on cache hits, the pool re-key (0% if pipelined).

### P6.5 Verdict (one-paragraph statement)

**Status: public encoder: priced (KV-only 2.71× session at B = 8, weights + KV 3.67×, measured in the harness; 7–8× TPOT extrapolated in a lean engine), timed and attack-based per page like P1, and secure as a KV occupancy claim only with a protocol remedy against nesting: epoch re-keying at 13–20 s of GPU per 80 GB per epoch (4–7% in decode-dominated serving, infeasible where prefill admits more than ≈ 0.1 GB/s of KV per GPU) or computation integrity (unbuilt). Trusted encoder: not applicable to KV; the server is its own encoder and a trusted party that encodes what the server sends still encodes the nest, so the trusted-encoder path is weights-only by decision, and a hybrid that counts public-encoded KV next to trusted-encoded weights lets KV pages nest weight blocks across KV epochs. Weights-only PoUS is nesting-free in both settings.** Probability that a KV occupancy claim reaches ≤ 2× at low batch with a complete proof: **≈ 1%** (inherits P1's proof gap plus the admission problem); ≤ 1.1×: ≈ 0. What would change the verdict: a computation-integrity layer (turns the KV claim into per-block bounds and removes the epoch cost); a fused decode-in-attention kernel plus a cleared cheaper mixer (≤ 1.8× at τ = 1 becomes plausible); for the trusted setting, nothing short of a trusted party in the KV data path at GB/s, which the program has rejected. Pointers: `research/kv2.md` (items 1–7, dated entries), `research/curve3.md` section 3 and table 5, `research/serving2.md` Part A (KV in vLLM-like harness) and the lean-engine extrapolation, `research/protocol2.md` (slot keys, commit-before-seed), `research/TEAM4.md` (scope decision), `CATALOG.md` 3.7; code `porep/kernels/porep_kernels.cu`, `porep/bench/`; data `results/pod-c/phase2/rq7/`, `results/phase2/rq8/`.
