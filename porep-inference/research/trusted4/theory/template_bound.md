2. **Lemma 2 (targeted setup).** Letting the trusted setup depend on W, `(pp, C) ← SetupEncode(W)` with `|pp| ≤ ε_p |W|`, does not remove MW20's surjectivity-plus-trapdoor-sampling requirement in the standard model, and in the random-oracle model reduces it to MW20 Definition 6.1 restricted to the B targets (lossy in simulation, indexing-hard in reality, sampleable by the encoder), for which the only known instantiation is an SLF; the RO case is closed on cost (section 5 item 1, D1's box), not on structure (R4 F17, 2026-09-20 (third session)). (a) For fixed c the map `pp ↦ Decode(pp, c)` has at most `2^(|pp|)` outputs, so the average fibre of a target under `Decode(pp, ·)` has at most `2^(|pp| + |C| − |W|)` elements and the encoder must invert a map of image density ≥ `2^(−|pp|)` at a prescribed target. (b) In the standard model (plain, CRS or W-dependent pp) MW20 Theorem 8.1's simulatable attacker applies verbatim to the set `{(pp, c) : Decode(pp, c) = W*}` and gives: no black-box proof from a single-stage assumption of incompressibility or HILL entropy given W above `|pp| + |C| − |W|` bits; rate `1 − ε'` therefore needs `|pp| ≥ (1 − ε'(1 + ε)) |W|`, and in that regime pp is a second data-length object that the server must hold (or recompute from (c, W)), not incompressible storage. In the random-oracle model, where the counting does not apply, any HILL simulator must make the decoder lossy at all B prescribed targets simultaneously while the real decoder is nearly injective there and fibre-indexing-hard at them (weaker than one-way; 8.3), and the encoder must sample the real fibres: this is MW20 Definition 6.1 restricted to the targets, as a specification, not as a proof that only an SLF meets it. QWW21's targeted lossy functions relax lossy trapdoor functions in the two directions we cannot afford: they drop the inversion trapdoor (Definition 3.1 has none) and target an *input* x* while we must hit a prescribed *output* W_i; their injective mode is expanding (`m ≥ n`), so most targets have no preimage, and their lossiness rate is `1/λ` (QWW21 section 1.1, "a fascinating open problem"). The requirement degrades gracefully in `|pp|`: the black-box ceiling is `(ε + |pp|/|W|)/(1 + ε)` of the codeword.
3. **Lemma 3 (equivalence).** In the program's untimed game, raw-block audit soundness given W and MW20 incompressibility (Definition 4.1 with the message as side information, pp counted in the state) are the same property up to the audit margin: a compressor to `S` bits with success π is a k-block cheater with state S and success ≥ π for every k; a product-form cheater with state S and per-block success p is an efficient compressor to `S + B + (1 − p) B |c|` bits, so incompressibility at `β = (1 − ε')|C|` bounds p by `1 − (ε − ε') + 1/|c|` and `Pr[k correct] ≤ π + p^k`. For joint (simultaneous-reveal) strategies the list compressor of Theorem 2c (`theory.md`) becomes efficient when the exact frequency list is replaced by R sampled challenge tuples, so the equivalence holds there too (the compressor is defined by the sampled hit set, R4 F20; `R = 2^26.9`, `p_η = 0.978897`): k = 217 / 653 by the incompressibility route against 209 / 631 by the HILL route (product form: 214 / 640 against 207 / 620). HILL entropy implies incompressibility (MW20 Theorem 5.2) but not conversely, and is what `theory.md` actually proves. Consequence: there is no weaker primitive to derive; the single-block audit *is* incompressibility given W, MW20 Theorem 8.1 applies to it, and the only free parameters are what is charged to the state (pp accounting) and the idealisation (random oracle versus a data-length CRS).
4. **Lemma 4 (public encoder).** If Encode is public (no secret erased), the codeword has zero entropy given (pp, W, public coins), the server stores an optimal compression of W plus the coins and reruns Encode on demand (all of W; no block-locality is needed in the untimed game, R4 F23); for compressible or degenerate W (PLAN4 allows `W = 0^n`; trained bf16 weights compress to 0.66 to 0.70, ZipNN and DFloat11, R4 F27) this passes every untimed audit from reduced or negligible state. Untimed incompressibility given W is impossible for public encoders; they need a deadline and a sequential-cost argument (phases 2 and 3).
5. **What is left open (section 5).** A trapdoor family in the sense of MW20 Definition 6.1/6.2 whose lossy twin has a small image *by construction* (not rank-plus-noise, not a subgroup of an unknown-order group, not an exactly rank-deficient linear map over a ring with efficient elimination), with expansion ≤ 1.05, entropy rate ≥ 0.969, 128-bit mode indistinguishability, and a forward map of at most 30 int8 MACs per decoded byte for the +10% budget or 330 for +100%, of which the random-oracle offset already costs 3 to 7 int32 per byte (so the +10% and +50% budgets are unreachable in the RO model for any MW-template construction, and +100% leaves 2 to 3 int32 or about 80 int8 MACs per byte for the function itself). ## 0. Conventions and the objects

Sizes: W has n bits, B blocks of `|w|` bits; C has B blocks of `|c|` bits, `|C| = B |c| + |pp_C|` where `pp_C` is the part of the public parameters stored with C (pk, seed); expansion `X = |c|/|w| ≤ 1 + ε` with ε = 0.05 in the program; entropy rate `ρ = (e − ℓ)/|c| ≥ 1 − ε'` with ε' = 0.031 (DCR: 1/32). The audit is that of `theory.md` section 5 (state S = (1 − ε*)|C|, ε* = 5.26%, k blocks with replacement, tags held by M). The MW20 objects are quoted in `theory.md` sections 1 and 2 and `constructions.md` section 1.1; Definition 6.1 (SLF: SurGen, LossyGen, D(pk), Inv_td, surjective-mode regularity "(pk, x, f(x)) statistically indistinguishable from (pk, Inv_td(y), y) for uniform y", lossy-mode image inside a set L_pk of size ≤ 2^ℓ, pk indistinguishability) and Definition 6.2 (domain elements in d bits, `H_inf(D(pk)) ≥ e`, range a group with oblivious sampler) are used by name.

Entropy conventions (ours). For a distribution X on a set of size ≤ 2^b, `H_inf(X) ≤ b`, and the ε-smooth min-entropy satisfies `H_inf^ε(X) ≤ b + log2(1/(1 − ε))`. For a product of `n` i.i.d. coordinates with Shannon entropy h each, `H_inf^ε(X^n) = n h ± O(sqrt(n) log(1/ε))` (asymptotic equipartition). Smooth min-entropy does not increase under functions. A discrete Gaussian coordinate of width s has min-entropy `log2 s` (up to `± 0.1` for s ≥ 8), Shannon entropy `h_sh = log2 s + 2.05`, and an entropy-coded storage cost `b = h_sh + 0.3` (Huffman on the top bits, T2 section 1.1) or `log2 s + 3.9` fixed-width with a 2^(−64) tail cut.

## 1. Theorem 1: the template lower bound

### 1.1 The family (definition)

**Definition 1.1 (rank-plus-noise MW-template encoding).** An encoding in the MW20 template (Theorem 7.1 in the CRS model or Theorem 5.3 in the RO model) whose SLF* has the following shape, over `R = Z` (N = 1, plain LWE) or `R = Z[X]/(X^N + 1)` (ring or module), with modulus q, `L = log2 q`:

- Domain: `x in R^m` with i.i.d. coordinates from a fixed distribution χ_x of Shannon entropy `h_sh` and min-entropy `h`, stored in b bits per coordinate (b ≥ h_sh by source coding, b ≥ h trivially); `d_bits = m N b`.
- Surjective mode: `f_A(x) = round_g(A x)` for `A in R_q^(d x m)`, where `round_g` maps `Z_q` to `Z_p`, `log2 p = L − g`, coordinatewise (g = 0 is the exact variant); range `R_p^d`, `r_bits = d N (L − g)`; the trapdoor sampler `Inv_td(y)` returns a sample of χ_x^m conditioned on `f_A(x) = y`.
- Lossy twin: `A' = Ā S + E` with `Ā in R_q^(d x k)` uniform, `S in R^(k x m)` (uniform mod q, or small with entries bounded by `β_c`), `E in R^(d x m)` with i.i.d. coordinates of standard deviation σ; `A'` is pseudorandom under (module-)LWE with secret dimension kN, so the two modes are indistinguishable.
- Leak: `ℓ = log2 |L_pk|` for a set `L_pk` containing `f_{A'}(x)` with probability `1 − negl` over `x ← χ_x^m`.

The encoding's expansion is `X = d_bits / r_bits = (m/d) b/(L − g)` and its (HILL) entropy rate is `ρ = (e − ℓ)/d_bits` with `e = H_inf(χ_x^m)` (MW20 Theorem 7.1: α = k' d, β = k'(e − ℓ)). With smooth min-entropy in place of e (T2 section 1.1; `theory.md` Lemma A holds verbatim for smooth and for average-case min-entropy, `theory.md` 5.2), `ρ = (m N h_sh − ℓ)/(m N b) − o(1)`, which is what the template can prove; we use this form.

**Assumptions used below (named so each inequality can cite them).**

- (D) Product domain: χ_x^m i.i.d. coordinates with entropies (h, h_sh) as above. Gaussian χ_x is the case of every known sampler (GPV, MP12, and the idealised sampler at the smoothing parameter, R2 1.3); a bounded uniform coordinate of the same entropy changes `h_sh − h` and the second moment by ≤ 0.25 bits (R2 1.2, entropy-power inequality) and nothing else below.
- (R) Regularity mod q: the surjective-mode regularity of Definition 6.1 is established by `A x mod q` being (smooth-)uniform on `R_q^d` for `x ← χ_x^m` (leftover hash over the ring, LPR13 section 7, as in T2 3.1.1), equivalently the trapdoor sampler samples χ_x^m on a single coset of `Λ^⊥(A)` at a uniformly chosen lift v of the target y (GPV/MP12, T2 3.1.1 "Inv_td(y): pick v uniform with round_p(v) = y"). MW20 Definition 6.1 only asks for `round_g(A x)` to be uniform; (R) is the stronger property every known construction has, and for a structural reason (R4 F2, F34, applied 2026-09-20 (third session)): every efficient discrete-Gaussian coset sampler in the literature (Klein/GPV08 Theorem 4.1, Peikert 2010's convolution sampler, MP12's perturbation-plus-gadget sampler) requires a width `s ≥ ‖B̃‖ · ω(sqrt(log m))` for a trapdoor basis B of `Λ^⊥(A)`, and for every basis `‖B̃‖ · sqrt(ln(2m(1 + 1/ε))/π) ≥ η_ε(Λ^⊥(A))`, the smoothing parameter; at `s ≥ η_ε(Λ^⊥(A))` the regularity lemma (GPV08 Corollary 5.4, MP12 Lemma 2.4) makes `A x mod q` within 2ε of uniform on `R_q^d`. So (R) is a consequence of "efficient Gaussian coset sampler", not an observation about the literature. A sampler that conditions on the *rounded* value `round_g(A x) = y` at a width below `η_ε(Λ^⊥(A))` would escape; the only samplers known below smoothing are exponential-time (Aggarwal, Dadush, Regev, Stephens-Davidowitz 2015), and rejection sampling on the rounded value costs `2^(dN(L − g))` trials (R4 F2's check of the two candidates a designer would try; the other, working mod p with `p | q`, is the exact variant g = 0 with modulus p, inside the theorem).
- (N) Noise hidden by rounding or charged: in lossy mode the output is `round_g(Ā (S x) + E x)`; E is independent of S and has full row rank on the kernel of S (a random Gaussian matrix does, with overwhelming probability), so `E x` is not determined by `S x`.
- (H) Hardness anchor: 128-bit indistinguishability of `A' = Ā S + E` from uniform needs `k N ≥ c_H L` with `c_H = 47` (T2 3.1.4 and R2 1.4: lattice estimator, ternary secret, σ = 3.2, core-SVP 0.292 β, `n/L = 46.8` at β = 444, consistent with the ADPS16 root-Hermite closed form `1/(4 log2 δ(444)) = 46.9`); a realistic BKZ cost model gives `c_H ≈ 42`. This is a "best known attack" anchor, not a reduction. The anchor was fitted at σ = 3.2 with ternary secret (R4 F8): a twin with a smaller error (binary error is not obviously broken by Arora–Ge at `mN ≈ 2^22` samples, below `(kN)² ≈ 2^30`) lowers `T − 1` by `log2(3.2/σ)` and hence L by about `12.7 log2(3.2/σ)` bits, with an anchor that has to be re-derived; the numbers below are at σ = 3.2.
- (S) Storage: coefficients are stored with redundancy `β := b/h_sh ≥ 1` (Huffman: `β − 1 = 0.3/h_sh`; fixed-width: `1.85/h_sh`).

### 1.2 Statement

**Theorem 1 (ours; formalising T2 3.1.2 with R2's corrections 1.2, 1.6; R4's F1 to F5, F10, F13, F14 applied 2026-09-20 (third session)).** Every rank-plus-noise MW-template encoding (Definition 1.1) with expansion X ≤ 1 + ε whose security proof is the template's, i.e. whose entropy rate is the *template-certifiable* rate `(e − ℓ)/d_bits ≥ 1 − ε'` (R4 F1: the theorem bounds what the MW20 template can certify for such an encoding, not what an adversary's compressor can do; an encoding of this shape proved by a direct compression argument, route (ii) of section 5 item 5, is outside its scope), satisfies, with `T − 1 := log2 s + ½ log2(mN) + 2.4` (the Shannon entropy per output coordinate of the error `E x` before rounding, σ = 3.2, Gaussian χ_x of width s; general χ_x: `log2(σ_E sqrt(2πe))` with `σ_E` the standard deviation of a coordinate of `E x`) and `r_S := log2 s + ½ log2(mN) + 0.43` (small S, the Shannon entropy per coefficient of `S x` for ternary S, `½ log2(2πe · (2/3) mN s²/(2π))`; R4 F4: the earlier constant 1.6 was `log2(2 · 3.8) − ½ log2(2π)`, the logarithm of T2's tail-bound support for a box-shaped `L_pk`, an upper bound on one construction's leak and not a lower bound over all `L_pk`; `r_S = L` for uniform S):

~~~
(I0)  hardness:        k N  >=  c_H L                                                   [H]
(I1)  gap vs expansion: g  <=  L (1 − β/X),  i.e.  L  >=  g X/(X − β)  >=  g X/(X − 1)   [R, S]
(I2)  regularity:      m/d  >=  L / h_sh   (Gaussian: L/(log2 s + 2.05))                 [D, R]
(I3)  rank leak:       m/k  >=  r_S / (ε' b),  and for uniform S:  d/k  >=  1/ε'         [N, D]
(I4)  master:          L (2 − β (1 + 1/X − ε'))  >=  (T − 1) + (k/d) r_S                 [D, R, N, S]
      and separately   (β − 1)/β  <=  ε'                                                 [S]
(I5)  block:           r_bits = d N (L − g)  >=  c_H L^2 (d/k) β / X                     [I0, I1]
(I6)  forward cost, per output ring element: m/d input ring elements read, m/d forward
      transforms of size N per RNS residue; per output coefficient per residue: m modular
      multiplications; per decoded byte: X/4 (about 0.26) int32 words of codeword and

At the program's point (X = 1.05, ε' = 0.031, Huffman storage) the inequalities give (calc; re-run 2026-09-20 (third session) with R4's reproduction script `review/r4_template_bound_check.py` and the corrected `r_S`, positive part of (C2) enforced): `L ≥ 21 g` (I1); `L ≥ 12.7 (T − 1 + (k/d) r_S)` (the factor is `1/(2 − β(1 + 1/X − ε'))`, 12.72 at β = 1 and growing with β, 13.04 at β = 1.001; R4 F5), hence the smallest feasible L is `683` (k = 1, d = 100), `782` (d = 4), `923` (d = 2), `1,751` (d = 1; the master inequality alone gives 1,237 there but its equality point violates (C2) once `g ≥ T − 1`, R4 F3); `m/d ≥ 12.7` in the limit of large d and log2 s, `m/d ≥ 17.0` at d = 4, log2 s = 300 (R2: 17.1), `m/d ≥ 19.1` at d = 4, log2 s = 100; `log2 N ≥ 15.5`; blocks ≥ 2.6 MB by the closed form with `L ≥ 683` and `d/k ≥ 1` (loose), and the joint minimum of `d N (L − g)` over feasible points with `d ≤ 128`, `k ≤ 4` is about 10 MB (9.7 MB at d = 2, k = 1, L = 923; R4 F10); transforms ≥ 220 to 250 int32 per decoded byte and total ≥ 250 to 280 with T2's pointwise and offset constants (the infimum over all d up to 512 and k up to 4 is 248, unchanged by the `r_S` correction; at d ≤ 16 the floor is R2's 280 to 330; the realisable optima are T2's 504 to 561 idealised and 737 to 753 with MP12).

### 1.3 Proofs

**(I0)** is assumption (H) restated: the lossy pk is a (module-)LWE instance with secret dimension kN and modulus 2^L; below `c_H L` the estimator gives fewer than 128 bits. Nothing else in the theorem depends on the constant.

**(I1) and (I2).** Under (R), `A x mod q` is within statistical distance negl of uniform on `R_q^d`, which has `d N L` bits, so `H_inf^{negl}(A x mod q) ≥ d N L − 1`. Smooth min-entropy does not increase under the function `x ↦ A x`, so `H_inf^{negl}(x) ≥ d N L − 1`. Under (D) and AEP, `H_inf^{negl}(x) ≤ m N h_sh + O(sqrt(mN) log(1/negl))`, which gives (I2) up to `o(1)` in m/d. Independently, x is stored in `d_bits = m N b` bits, so `m N b ≥ H_inf^{negl}(x) − 1 ≥ d N L − 2`. Then `X = m N b / (d N (L − g)) ≥ L/(L − g)`, i.e. `g ≤ L (1 − 1/X)`; with (S), `m N b = β m N h_sh ≥ β d N L` gives the sharper `g ≤ L (1 − β/X)`. Solving for L gives the two forms in (I1). At X = 1.05 and β → 1, `L ≥ 21 g`. ∎

*Remark (why (R) is load-bearing).* Under Definition 6.1 alone the range is `R_p^d` with `d N (L − g)` bits and the same argument gives only `X ≥ 1`. The gap enters through (R), i.e. through the fact that every known preimage sampler samples a coset of `Λ^⊥(A)` at a lifted target v, which needs `A x mod q` to be near-uniform so that the lifts are equally likely. A sampler that produced χ_x^m conditioned on the *rounded* value directly would escape (I1); none is known, and section 1.5 lists this as the first escape.

**(I3).** In lossy mode `f_{A'}(x) = round_g(Ā u + E x)` with `u = S x in R_q^k`. Claim: with overwhelming probability over Ā, the map `u ↦ round_g(Ā u + e)` is injective on the support of `S x`, uniformly over all e with `‖e‖_inf ≤ τ` (τ the tail bound on `‖E x‖_inf`). For `u ≠ u'` in the support, `Ā (u − u')` is uniform on `R_q^d` when N = 1 (Ā uniform, `u − u' ≠ 0`); for `R_q` with q split into N slots it is uniform in every slot where `u − u'` is nonzero, and a nonzero `u − u'` of coefficient `ℓ_2` norm at most `2 τ_c sqrt(N)` (small S) lies in the product of at most `|T| ≤ N (log2(2τ_c) + ½ log2 N)/L` slot ideals, because an element of the product of `|T|` slot ideals has algebraic norm at least `q^(|T|)`, hence canonical-embedding norm at least `sqrt(N) q^(|T|/N)` by the arithmetic-geometric mean over the embeddings, and for power-of-two cyclotomics `‖σ(v)‖_2 = sqrt(N) ‖v‖_coef`, so `‖v‖_coef ≥ q^(|T|/N)` (R4 F9: the earlier text mixed the two norms and dropped the `½ log2 N`; the argument needs q fully split, and a partially split q has fewer slots and only improves the bound); at the feasible points the fraction of non-uniform slots is 0.059 at L = 683, 0.078 at L = 3,640 and 0.008 at L = 41,000 (calc), so at least 90% of the dN coordinates are uniform. The probability that every one of those coordinates lies within `2 · 2^g + 2τ` of 0 mod q is at most `((4 · 2^g + 4τ)/q)^(0.92 dN) ≤ 2^(−0.92 dN (L − g − 4))` for `τ ≤ 2^g`; a union over the at most `2^(2 k N r_S)` pairs is negligible when `2 k N r_S < 0.90 d N (L − g − 4)`, which holds at every feasible point since `k r_S ≤ ε' m b ≤ ε' X d (L − g)`. On this event `f_{A'}(x)` determines `S x`, so `H_inf^{negl}(f_{A'}(x)) ≥ H_inf^{negl}(S x)`, and `ℓ ≥ H_inf^{negl}(S x) − 1` because `L_pk` must contain the image with probability `1 − negl`. For uniform S (MW20's own lossy mode), `S x` is uniform on `R_q^k` given x has full support in each slot, so `ℓ ≥ k N L − negl`. For small S, `S x` is a sum of mN independent scaled Gaussians per coefficient, `kN` coefficients with covariance `(s²/2π) S Sᵀ` of full rank kN, and its smooth min-entropy is `≥ k N (log2 s + ½ log2(mN) + 0.43) − o(kN) = k N r_S` (the entropy of a Gaussian of variance `(2/3) mN s²/(2π)` per coefficient for ternary S, rounded to integers: `½ log2(2πe · (2/3) mN s²/(2π)) = log2 s + ½ log2(mN) + 0.43`; R4 F4). The rate constraint `ℓ ≤ ε' m N b − m N (b − h_sh)` then gives `k N r_S ≤ ε' m N b`, i.e. `m/k ≥ r_S/(ε' b)`. For uniform S the same constraint reads `k N L ≤ ε' m N b`, and `m b = X d (L − g)` (definition of X) gives `k/d ≤ ε' X (L − g)/L ≤ ε' X`, so `d/k ≥ 1/(ε' X) ≈ 30.7`, which is R2 1.6's "d at least 32 k" up to the factor X. ∎

**(I4).** Fix `u = S x`. The x with `S x = u` form a coset of `ker S` of dimension `(m − k) N`; by (N) the restriction of E to that coset has full row rank, so `E x` ranges over a set of diameter about `2τ` in every coordinate as x ranges over the coset, and given u the output `round_g(Ā u + E x)` takes, per coordinate, at least `⌊2τ_typ / 2^g⌋` distinct values, where `τ_typ` is the typical spread of a coordinate of `E x` conditioned on `S x = u` (which differs from the unconditioned spread by a factor `sqrt(1 − k/m)` because conditioning on the k N linear constraints removes that share of the variance; we absorb it in the `± 0.5` bit slack). In entropy terms, the conditional Shannon entropy of the rounded output given u is at least `(T − 1) − g` bits per output coordinate when `g ≤ T − 1` (quantising a Gaussian of standard deviation `σ_E` to cells of width 2^g leaves `log2(σ_E sqrt(2πe)) − g` bits; `log2 σ_E = log2 σ + log2 s + ½ log2(mN) − 1.33`, and with σ = 3.2 this is `log2 s + ½ log2(mN) + 0.35`, so `T − 1 = log2 s + ½ log2(mN) + 2.4`), and by AEP over dN coordinates the smooth min-entropy of the output given u is `≥ d N ((T − 1) − g)^+ − o(dN)`. Two remarks on this step (R4 F6, F7). The high-resolution formula `log2(σ_E sqrt(2πe)) − g` *under*-estimates the exact entropy of a rounded Gaussian at every cell width (by 0.06 bits at cell = σ_E, 0.19 at 2σ_E, and by 0.95 once the cell is 8σ_E, where the formula is negative and the positive part takes over; R4 script H), so the leak lower bound is conservative in the direction the theorem needs. And the dN coordinates of `E x` given u are not independent (they are linear functionals of one Gaussian on a coset of `ker S`), so the AEP is over the joint entropy `½ log2 det(2πe Σ)` with `Σ ∝ E P Eᵀ`, P the projector onto `ker S`; by Marchenko–Pastur at aspect ratio `d/m ≈ 1/14` the normalised spectrum costs about `−(d/m) log2(e)/2 ≈ −0.05` bits per coordinate, inside the `± 0.5` bit slack. Hence

~~~
ℓ  >=  H_inf^{negl}( f_{A'}(x) )  >=  H_inf^{negl}(S x) + d N ((T − 1) − g)^+  >=  k N r_S + d N ((T − 1) − g)^+.
~~~

The rate constraint `ℓ + m N (b − h_sh) ≤ ε' m N b` becomes, after dividing by dN and writing `ρ_m = m/d`,

~~~
(k/d) r_S + ((T − 1) − g)^+ + ρ_m h_sh (β − 1)  <=  ε' ρ_m β h_sh.                       (C2)
~~~

If `g ≥ T − 1` the positive part vanishes and (C2) requires `β − 1 < ε' β` (the separate constraint) and `ρ_m ≥ (k/d) r_S /(h_sh (1 + ε'β − β))`. If `g < T − 1`, add the expansion constraint (I1) in the form `g ≤ L − ρ_m β h_sh / X` (from `X (L − g) = ρ_m b = ρ_m β h_sh`) to (C2):

~~~
(T − 1) + (k/d) r_S  <=  g + ε' ρ_m β h_sh − ρ_m h_sh (β − 1)
                     <=  L − ρ_m h_sh [ β/X − ε' β + β − 1 ]
                     <=  L − L [ β (1 + 1/X − ε') − 1 ]                 (using ρ_m h_sh >= L by (I2); the bracket is positive)
                      =  L (2 − β (1 + 1/X − ε')).
~~~

This is (I4). At β = 1 it reads `L (1 − 1/X + ε') ≥ (T − 1) + (k/d) r_S`, and `1 − 1/1.05 + 0.031 = 0.0786`, so `L ≥ 12.7 (T − 1 + (k/d) r_S)`; the factor grows with β: 13.04 at β = 1.001 (Huffman storage at log2 s = 300), 15.5 at β = 1.0073, 20.6 at β = 1.0157 (calc; R4 F5). *Feasibility check (R4 F3).* (I4) is necessary, not sufficient: it was obtained by adding (C2) with its positive part to the expansion constraint, and its equality case is a feasible point only when `g_max = L(1 − β/X) ≤ T − 1`; otherwise the equality credits a *negative* error leak against the rank-plus-storage overrun, and (C2) with the positive part, together with (I1), must be checked separately. The optimum of the full system is at `u := (m/d) h_sh = max(L, (k/d) r_S/(1 + ε'β − β))`, not always `u = L`. At d/k = 100 and 4 the equality points of (I4) are feasible (`g = 22.8 < T − 1 = 33.8`, `g = 25.7 < 30.9`); at d/k = 1 the equality point (L = 1,237) has `g_max = 40.6 > T − 1 = 30.6` and the rank term alone exceeds the entropy budget, so the smallest feasible L there is 1,751 (log2 s = 21, `g = T − 1 = 34.7`), not 1,237 (calc). Because `T − 1` and `r_S` grow like `log2 s + ½ log2(mN)` while `h_sh = log2 s + 2.05`, dividing (I4) by `h_sh` and using (I2) gives the m/d floor

~~~
m/d  >=  L/h_sh  >=  12.7 · [ (log2 s + ½ log2(mN) + 2.4) + (k/d)(log2 s + ½ log2(mN) + 0.43) ] / (log2 s + 2.05),
~~~

which tends to 12.7 as `d → ∞` and `log2 s → ∞` (β → 1), equals 17.0 at d = 4, log2 s = 300, mN = 2^23 (16.93 to 16.95 depending on whether mN is fixed or self-consistent; R2's 17.1 at the same point), and 19.1 at d = 4, log2 s = 100 (calc). The separate constraint `(β − 1)/β ≤ ε'` says the storage redundancy alone must fit the 3.1% budget: `h_sh ≥ 9.4` (log2 s ≥ 7.3) with Huffman coding, `h_sh ≥ 58` (log2 s ≥ 56) with fixed-width storage. ∎

**(I5).** `r_bits = d N (L − g) ≥ d N L β/X` by (I1), and `N ≥ c_H L/k` by (I0): `r_bits ≥ c_H L² (d/k) β/X`. For `k ≤ d` this is at least `c_H L²/X`, T2's "block ≥ 47 L² bits" with the factor `1/X`. For `k > d` (more secret rank than outputs, not excluded by the definitions) the block shrinks by `d/k` but (I3) forces `m/d = (m/k)(k/d) ≥ (k/d) r_S/(ε' b)`, so the product `r_bits · (m/d) ≥ c_H L² r_S β /(ε' b X) ≈ 1,500 L²` bits-times-transforms is invariant: smaller blocks are paid for linearly in forward transforms per output. ∎

**(I6).** The forward map is `x ↦ round_g(A x)`: every one of the m input ring elements enters every output ring element through a generic public coefficient, so per output ring element the decoder reads `m/d` input ring elements (N coefficients of b stored bits each) and, in whatever domain the product is computed, forms for each of the N output coefficients and each of the `RES = ⌈L/31⌉` RNS residues a linear form in m variables with generic coefficients, which needs m multiplications (a linear form with m algebraically independent coefficients has multiplicative complexity m: Winograd 1970, "On the number of multiplications necessary to compute certain functions", for multiplications by generic constants; R4 F11). Per decoded byte (`(L − g)/8` bits per output coefficient): codeword loads `≥ (m/d) N b / (N (L − g)/8) = 8 (m/d) b/(L − g) = 8 X` bits, i.e. `X/4 ≈ 0.26` int32 words per byte plus the unpacking of `(m/d) b/(L − g) ≈ X` coefficients of b bits each; public key `d m N L` bits per `d N (L − g)` output bits, `m L/(L − g)` bits per output bit, `m L/(8(L − g))` bytes per output byte without batching (R2 1.6: at least 37 bytes per byte at m = 35). These counts hold for every ring. With `R = Z[X]/(X^N + 1)` and NTT multiplication, each input ring element needs one forward NTT per residue, `(N/2) log2 N` butterflies at about 8 int32 each (T2 3.1.3, estimated constant), so the transform stage is `(m/d) · 8 · (N/2) log2 N · RES / (N (L − g)/8) = 32 (m/d) RES log2 N/(L − g) ≈ 1.03 (m/d) log2 N · L/(L − g)` int32 per byte; with `log2 N ≥ log2(c_H L/k)` from (I0). For plain LWE (N = 1) the "transform" is the dense product itself, `m (L/32)²` int32 MACs per output coordinate, worse by a factor of order `L/log2 N` (T2 row 1e). ∎

### 1.5 What escapes Theorem 1

The theorem is about Definition 1.1; each of its inequalities names the assumption it uses, and dropping the assumption is the escape:

- **Not rank-plus-noise (escapes (I1), (I3), (I4)); and not template-certified at all (R4 F1).** The theorem bounds the *template-certifiable* rate `(e − ℓ)/d_bits`, i.e. what MW20's proof can output for an encoding of this shape; an encoding of this very shape whose incompressibility is proved by a direct compression argument in an idealised model (section 5 item 5, route (ii)) is not bounded by Theorem 1, which says nothing about adversaries' compressors. A lossy twin whose small image is not `Ā S + E` has no error to round away and no rank term: (I1) and (I4) disappear, `m/d` can be 1 + o(1) (T2 6.2's hypothetical twin), and (I5), (I6) shrink to the block and read bounds of the plain map. No lattice example is known (T2 6.3, R2 summary); section 5 specifies it.
- **Regularity without regularity mod q (escapes (I1)).** A preimage sampler that samples χ_x^m conditioned on the rounded value `round_g(A x) = y` directly, without lifting y to a uniform v, would let `A x mod q` be far from uniform and remove the `L/(L − g)` expansion factor. All known efficient samplers (GPV, Klein, MP12, Lyubashevsky–Wichs) are coset samplers at a lifted target (R2 1.3) and provably operate at or above the smoothing parameter, where regularity mod q is automatic (assumption (R), R4 F2); none conditions on a rounded value, and no polynomial-time sampler below smoothing is known (R4 F34).
- **Non-product domains (weakens (I2), (I4) by constants; wording per R4 F33).** For independent coordinates of a different *shape*, the entropy-power inequality (R2 1.2) says no distribution of the same entropy has smaller error spread than the Gaussian, so (I4)'s `T − 1` cannot fall by more than 0.25 bits, and (I2) holds with `h_sh` the marginal entropy by subadditivity (R4 F12). For *correlated* coordinates the relevant fact is stronger: the spread of a coordinate of `E x`, averaged over the rows of E, is `σ² · tr Σ_x`, which for fixed joint entropy `½ log2 det(2πe Σ_x)` is minimised by `Σ_x = s² I` (AM–GM), so correlations at equal joint entropy only *increase* `T − 1`. The one correlated domain that shrinks the residual leak, `x = M z` with z of lower dimension, is the same scheme with z as the stored codeword and `A M` as the public matrix, a smaller instance of the family with structured error `E M` (next bullet).
- **Structured error (R4 F32; (N) is stated for i.i.d. E).** *Low-rank E* (`E = E_1 E_2`): `A' = [Ā | E_1] · [S ; E_2]` is exactly rank-deficient and Gaussian elimination detects it (T2 6.3); excluded without assumption. *Sparse E* (rows with w nonzeros out of mN): the noise-free entries are exact linear equations in S, and with `mN ≫ kN` samples per row they determine S; excluded. *Smaller σ or a non-Gaussian error shape*: not excluded; it shifts L by about `12.7 log2(3.2/σ)` bits and needs a new hardness anchor (assumption (H), R4 F8).
- **A cheaper hardness anchor (scales (I0), (I5), (I6) by a constant).** A realistic BKZ model gives `c_H ≈ 42`; a structured-A assumption with a credible analysis could lower it further, but (I6)'s `log2 N` dependence is logarithmic in `c_H`, so a factor 2 in `c_H` is 1 int32 per byte in the floor.
- **Exact variant with a non-noise leak accounting.** The exact variant (g = 0) is inside the theorem: (I4) with g = 0 charges the whole `T − 1` to entropy, forcing `m/d ≥ (T − 1 + (k/d) r_S)/(ε' b) ≈ 41` at L = 41,000 (R2 1.2's correction of T2's 67).
- **Not an MW-template proof at all.** If the audit bound is derived from something other than HILL entropy of C given W (Lemma 3 says what that something would have to be), Definition 1.1 does not apply. Lemma 3 shows there is no such weaker something for the untimed game.

## 2. Lemma 2: W-dependent ("targeted") setup

### 2.1 Setting

The adviser's proposal: let the trusted setup depend on the data, `(pp, C) ← SetupEncode(W)`, so that the public parameters can be "aimed" at W, in the hope that the encoder no longer needs a surjective function with trapdoor preimage sampling (MW20 Definition 6.1) but only something weaker, in the spirit of QWW21's targeted lossy functions. Requirements kept from the program: `Decode(pp, C) = W` with Decode public and deterministic; `|C| ≤ (1 + ε)|W|`; the server holds (pp, C) and the adversary knows W; the audit is the raw-block audit given W; the security notion is HILL entropy of C given (pp, W) (MW20 Definition 5.1 with W as side information), or incompressibility given (pp, W) (Definition 4.1 with W in aux), which Lemma 3 shows to be the audit itself. We write `t = |pp|`, `n = |W|`, `D_pp := Decode(pp, ·)`, and `F_pp(W) := D_pp^(−1)(W)` for the fibre of W. Block structure (B blocks, `W_i = Decode(pp, c_i)`) is used only in 2.4.

### 2.2 Statement

**Lemma 2 (ours).** Let (SetupEncode, Decode) be as in 2.1 with `|pp| ≤ t` and `|C| ≤ α`.

(a) *Counting.* For every fixed codeword value c, the map `pp ↦ Decode(pp, c)` takes at most `2^t` values. Consequently (i) some pp has `|Im D_pp| ≥ 2^(n − t)`, i.e. image density at least `2^(−t)` in `{0,1}^n`; (ii) for W uniform and `pp ← SetupEncode(W)`, `E[ log2 |Im D_pp| ] ≥ n − t`; (iii) for W uniform, `E[ log2 |F_pp(W)| ] ≤ t + α − n`, so the real fibres at the targets have average size at most `2^(t + α − n)` and the real entropy of C given (pp, W) is at most `t + α − n` bits. The encoder must therefore produce a preimage of the prescribed target W under a map of image density at least `2^(−t)` whose fibre at W has on average at most `2^(t + α − n)` elements.

(b) *Standard-model separation.* For every `β ≥ t + α − n` there is a perfectly simulatable (in the sense of MW20 section 8 and Wic13) attacker against β-incompressibility of C given (pp, W); hence β-incompressibility, and a fortiori β-HILL entropy, of C given (pp, W) has no black-box proof from any single-stage game assumption in the plain or CRS model, with or without W-dependence of pp. Entropy rate `1 − ε'` of a codeword of length `α = (1 + ε) n` therefore requires `t ≥ n − ε' α = (1 − ε'(1 + ε)) n`, i.e. `t ≥ 0.967 n` at the program's parameters.

(c) *Large-pp regime.* If `t ≥ (1 − ε'(1+ε)) n`, then either pp is stored (server state ≥ `t + |C| ≥ (2 + ε − ε'(1+ε)) n`, twice the budget) or pp is recomputable from (C, W) (as in `pp = f(C) ⊕ W`), in which case the object with entropy given W is C alone and pp carries W: the scheme is "store a random string and separately store W", and the audit certifies the random string, not W.

(d) *Random-oracle model.* If Decode queries a random oracle (MW20 Theorem 5.3's setting), (b) does not apply, but any HILL simulator `Sim(W) → (pp', RO', C')` for the real distribution must satisfy: (i) `Decode^(RO')(pp', c'_i) = W_i` for every block with overwhelming probability; (ii) each fibre `F'_i = {c : Decode^(RO')(pp', c) = W_i}` has size at least `2^(β_blk)`, so the simulated decoder is lossy at all B prescribed targets simultaneously; (iii) the real decoder is nearly injective at the targets ((a)(iii) holds for every fixed oracle) and must be *fibre-indexing-hard* at them (wording per R5 F1.4 and R4 F17/F18, 2026-09-20 (third session); the earlier text said "one-way", which is stronger than what is proved or needed, see 8.3): an efficient algorithm that, given (pp, W_i, oracle access), outputs a short index of c_i within `F_pp(W_i)` compresses C to `t + α − n + o(1)` bits and breaks the audit outright; (iv) the encoder outputs elements of the real fibres at B prescribed targets whose joint distribution is computationally indistinguishable from one with `β_blk` bits of min-entropy per fibre. Properties (ii) to (iv) are MW20 Definition 6.1's lossy mode, one-wayness without the trapdoor, and surjective-mode regularity with trapdoor sampling, restricted from all of the range to the B targets.

(e) *Graceful degradation.* For `0 < t < n` the black-box ceiling of (b) on the entropy rate is `(ε + t/n)/(1 + ε)` of the codeword; the image density bound of (a) is `2^(−t)`; the average fibre bound is `2^(t + α − n)`. All three move linearly in t: at t = 0 they are MW20's obligations for a public-decoder encoding, at `t = n` they vanish and the scheme is (c).

### 2.3 Proofs

**(a).** (i): the `2^t` values of pp give at most `2^t` values of `Decode(pp, c)` for fixed c. For correctness on every W the union over pp of `Im D_pp` is `{0,1}^n`, so `max_pp |Im D_pp| ≥ 2^n/2^t`. (ii): for each W the chosen pp(W) has `W ∈ Im D_pp(W)`, so `Σ_pp |Im D_pp| ≥ |{W}| = 2^n` (the images over the at most `2^t` distinct pp values cover all W), and the average over the pp values of `|Im D_pp|` is at least `2^(n − t)`; and the set of W served by a pp with `|Im D_pp| ≤ 2^(n − t − j)` has at most `2^t · 2^(n − t − j) = 2^(n − j)` elements, so `Pr_W[ log2 |Im D_pp(W)| ≤ n − t − j ] ≤ 2^(−j)` and `E_W[ log2 |Im D_pp(W)| ] ≥ n − t − 2`. (iii): the pairs (pp, c) number at most `2^(t + α)` and each decodes to one W, so `Σ_W |{(pp, c) : Decode(pp, c) = W}| ≤ 2^(t + α)`; for uniform W, `E[ |F_pp(W)| summed over pp ] ≤ 2^(t + α − n)`, and `F_pp(W)(W)` is a subset of that union; Jensen gives the bound on `E[log2 |F|]`. The real entropy `H(C | pp, W) ≤ E[log2 |F_pp(W)|]` because C lies in the fibre. ∎

**(b).** MW20's section 8 implication for the CRS model ("any (α, β)-incompressible encoding with a CRS of size t also yields an (α + t, β)-incompressible encoding in the plain model: we simply choose a fresh random CRS during encoding time and append it to the codeword. Therefore, we get the same type of black-box separation for any encoding in the CRS model as long as β ≥ α + t − k") uses only that the CRS is chosen at encoding time and appended; it does not use that it is uniform. Apply Theorem 8.1 to the plain-model scheme `Enc'(W) := (pp, C)` with `(pp, C) ← SetupEncode(W)`, `Dec'(pp, c) := Decode(pp, c)`, expansion `α' = t + α`, message length k = n: for `β ≥ α' − k = t + α − n` the attacker `A_h` of the proof of Theorem 8.1 (choose `W*` minimising `|C_{W*}| = |{(pp, c) : Decode(pp, c) = W*}| ≤ 2^(t + α − n)`, compress `(pp, c)` to `h(i(pp, c))` with h a random permutation of the index set, expand by `i^(−1)(h^(−1)(w))`) wins with probability 1 with `|w| ≤ t + α − n`, and the stateful efficient simulator `A'` of the same proof (hard-coded `W*` and q random elements of `C_{W*}`, a table of answered queries) is perfectly indistinguishable from `A_h` for a random h; by Theorem 4.2 of Wic13 as cited by MW20 no black-box reduction from a single-stage game assumption can use `A_h`. Two points specific to our game. First, our adversary knows W; `A_h` already has `W*` hard-coded, and knowledge of W by the compressor and expander only helps the attacker, so the separation holds for incompressibility *given W*. Second, our compressor is charged for pp if pp must sit on the server, or not if pp is public and recomputable. The separation is proved with pp inside the codeword (charged accounting); it covers the free accounting because a proof of INC given (pp, W) at β bits is a fortiori a proof of INC of `(pp, C)` at β bits (a charged-accounting adversary that must recover (pp, c) from β bits without pp is weaker than a free-accounting one), so the separation excludes `β_free ≥ t + α − n` as well (R4 F15, F16; the earlier sentence argued from the *length* of the index of c within `F_pp(W*)`, which is not how the simulator argument transfers: a simulator cannot sample `F_pp(W*)` for a pp it did not choose). Also, the W-dependent setup is the selective setting (message fixed before pp), which is the setting MW20's remark covers. HILL entropy `β` implies `(β − λ)`-incompressibility (MW20 Theorem 5.2), so the separation covers HILL proofs at `β − λ ≥ t + α − n`. Solving `(1 − ε') α ≤ t + α − n` gives `t ≥ n − ε' α`. ∎

**(c).** If pp is on the server it is state; `t + |C| ≥ (1 − ε'(1+ε)) n + (1 + ε) n`. If not, the server recomputes pp from what it holds, which must then determine pp: Decode(pp, C) = W with C and W known determines the *restriction* of `Decode(·, C)` at pp but not pp itself unless `pp ↦ Decode(pp, C)` is injective on the relevant set, which is the case `pp = f(C) ⊕ W` (then `pp` is a function of (C, W), and `C` given `(pp, W)` is `C` given `f(C)`: its HILL entropy equals that of C given f(C), which for an injective f is zero and for a lossy f is the fibre entropy of f, i.e. real entropy of a random string). In both cases the pp of length about n is a second copy of the data and the audited object C is a random string whose relation to W runs through pp. ∎

**(d).** (i): the adversary knows W and Decode, so the distinguisher "decode `c'_i` under `(pp', RO')` and compare with `W_i`" separates any simulator that fails (i) with noticeable probability. (ii): `H_inf(c'_i | pp', RO', W) ≥ β_blk` and `c'_i ∈ F'_i` force `|F'_i| ≥ 2^(β_blk)`. (iii): (a)(iii) is a counting statement for each fixed oracle, so the real fibres are small; if `Idx(pp, W_i, c_i)` outputs `j ≤ |F_pp(W_i)|` and `Idx^(−1)(pp, W_i, j) = c_i` efficiently with oracle access, then `Cmp(C) = (j_1, …, j_B)` has `Σ log2 |F_pp(W_i)| ≤ t + α − n + B` bits on average and `Exp` recovers C, breaking incompressibility given W at every β above that, and by Lemma 3 (i) breaking the audit with probability 1 from state `t + α − n + B ≈ (ε + t/n) n ≪ (1 − ε*)|C|`. (iv): the encoder outputs `c_i ∈ F_pp(W_i)` for the prescribed targets, and the real joint distribution of `(pp, C)` given W is computationally indistinguishable from the simulated one with `β_blk` bits per fibre. In MW20's construction these four properties are exactly: lossy-mode small image at `y_i = f(c_i)` (ii), fibre-indexing-hardness without td (iii; MW20's construction has the stronger one-wayness of `f_pk`, R2 1.0's "if small preimages could be found without the trapdoor, lossy pks would be distinguishable", which the argument does not need), and surjective-mode regularity with `Inv_td` (iv) restricted to the B targets `embed(W_i) + u_i`. Nothing in (i) to (iv) is weaker than Definition 6.1 except the restriction of "all of the range" to "the B targets", which 2.4 shows QWW21's relaxation does not supply. ∎

**(e).** Immediate from (a), (b) with t as a parameter. ∎

### 2.4 QWW21's targeted lossy functions and why the relaxation does not transfer

What QWW21 define (Definition 3.1, read): a TLF with input length n, output length `m ≥ n` and lossiness ℓ consists of `InjectiveGen(1^λ) → fk`, `LossyGen(1^λ, x*) → fk` for a target input `x* in {0,1}^n`, and a deterministic `F_fk : {0,1}^n → {0,1}^m`, with: Injectivity (fk from InjectiveGen makes `F_fk` injective w.o.p.), ℓ-Lossiness (for `x*` uniform and `fk ← LossyGen(1^λ, x*)`, `H_inf(x* | fk, F_fk(x*)) ≥ ℓ`), and Indistinguishability (`(x*, fk_inj) ≈_c (x*, fk_loss)` for all x*). The introduction (section 1, "Targeted Lossy Functions") states the two relaxations relative to Peikert–Waters LTDFs: "they do not require an inversion trapdoor in injective mode", and "the lossy mode of the function is initialized with some target input, and the function is only required to lose information about this particular target". Their constructions (section 1.2, read) are `F_fk(x) = h_a(G(x))` with G an injective PRG from λ to 3λ bits and `h_a(z) = chop(a · z)` over `F_(2^(3λ+1))`, so `F_fk` maps λ bits to 3λ bits; in lossy mode a is programmed so that `G(x*_0)` and `G(x*_1)` collide, giving ℓ = 1 bit, amplified by parallel repetition; "the lossiness rate of our constructions, defined as ℓ/n where n is the domain size, is only 1/λ ... Achieving a higher lossiness rate in Minicrypt is a fascinating open problem" (section 1.1). Section 3.2 adds tags (T-ALBO), and the relaxed-injectivity variants require only that the injective mode determine a property P(x).

Mapping to our setting (ours). The only way to place a TLF in the encoding is `Decode(pp, c) = F_fk(c)` with `pp = fk`, so the codeword c is the TLF's *input* and the data W its *output*. Then:

1. **Wrong side of the function.** QWW21 target an input `x*` and make `F_fk(x*)` lose ℓ bits about `x*`; we must hit a prescribed *output* `W_i`: the encoder needs `c_i` with `F_fk(c_i) = W_i` for a `W_i` fixed before setup. A TLF has no inversion algorithm (the first relaxation removes it), and in injective mode `F_fk` is expanding (`m ≥ n`; in the construction 3λ against λ), so a uniformly chosen or adversarially fixed `W_i` has no preimage at all with probability `1 − 2^(n − m)`. Lemma 2(a) says the encoder must invert a map of image density `≥ 2^(−t)` at prescribed targets; QWW21's functions have image density `2^(n − m)`, in their constructions `≤ 2^(−2λ)` (the definition only gives `m ≥ n`; R4 F19), and no inverter. Setting `x* := c_i` (choose the codeword first, then program fk so that `F_fk(c_i) = W_i`) is possible for a universal hash (solve `h_a(G(c_i)) = W_i` for a), but then the fibre of `W_i` is `{c : G(c) in h_a^(−1)(W_i)}`, of size at most the number of collisions of `h_a` on `Im G`, which is 2 in their construction and, for any h from `3λ` bits to fewer bits, still bounded by the collision structure of a *public* hash: the fibre is what the server enumerates, and 2.2(d)(iii) says it must be indexing-hard.
2. **Lossiness rate.** We need the fibre at every one of B targets to have `2^(0.969 |c|)` elements in the simulated world (2.2(d)(ii)), i.e. lossiness rate 0.969 of the input; QWW21 achieve rate `1/λ` and state higher rates as open. Parallel repetition does not raise the rate (their footnote 6 and section 1.1).
3. **Indistinguishability given the target is the right notion but for the wrong variable.** QWW21's `(x*, fk_inj) ≈ (x*, fk_loss)` implies indistinguishability given `(x*, F_fk(x*))`, so if the codeword were the target input, indistinguishability given (C, W) would follow. But the simulated mode we need is lossy at B *outputs* chosen by the adversary (W is arbitrary, PLAN4 section 1), not at one random input; making `F_fk` lossy at B adversarial outputs with rate 0.969 while injective-looking elsewhere is not a targeted lossy function, it is a surjective lossy function with a trapdoor, restricted to B points.
4. **The Minicrypt point cuts the other way.** QWW21's motivation is that TLFs live in Minicrypt (injective PRGs). Lemma 2(d)(iii) and (iv) require fibre-indexing-hardness at prescribed targets *together with* an encoder that samples the fibres there: a trapdoor in the abstract sense of 8.3. Whether the object is Cryptomania is open (R4 F18, applied 2026-09-20 (third session); the earlier sentence "a trapdoor function is a Cryptomania object" was false as written): many-to-one trapdoor functions with exponential fibres exist from one-way functions (Bellare, Halevi, Sahai, Vadhan, CRYPTO 1998, ePrint 1998/019, Theorem 1, as read by R4), only trapdoor functions with polynomially bounded preimages are known to give public-key encryption (their Theorem 2), and the real fibres here have `2^((ε + t/n) n)` elements on average (2.2(a)(iii)); only the twin route's uniform-target inverter (Definition 6.1) is known to give public-key encryption. What is not open is that QWW21's relaxations point the other way (no inverter, rate 1/λ, wrong side of the function); whatever object works has the abstract trapdoor, and Lemma 3 says it must supply incompressibility given W, to which MW20 Theorem 8.1 applies.

Conclusion for the options chart: "targeted setup" is not a new option. In the standard model it is excluded by 2.2(b) exactly like the plain and short-CRS models; in the random-oracle model 2.2(d) *restates* the requirement rather than closing it: what survives there is MW20 Definition 6.1 restricted to the B targets (lossy in simulation, indexing-hard in reality, sampleable by the encoder), for which the only known instantiation is an SLF; (d) is a specification of the object, not a proof that only an SLF meets it, and the closure of the RO case is on cost (section 5 item 1's offset budget, measured by D1), not on structure (R4 F17, F35). What W-dependence *can* buy is bookkeeping: pp may absorb per-block offsets (the `u_i`) that the RO otherwise generates, at the cost of `|pp|` counted against the 1.05 budget, which is the CRS-versus-RO trade of `theory.md` section 4 under a different name.

### 2.5 What escapes Lemma 2

- **A non-black-box or multi-stage proof in the standard model.** Theorem 8.1's separation is against black-box reductions from single-stage assumptions; a proof from a multi-stage assumption (e.g. an assumption that itself speaks about compressors) or a non-black-box technique is not excluded. No such proof is known for any incompressible encoding; MW20 use the RO or a data-length CRS.
- **Interactive or stateful decoding.** The counting in (a) assumes Decode is a deterministic function of (pp, c); a decoder that consults the verifier, or a verifier that decodes, is outside the program (T2 3.4).
- **A lossy function that is lossy at prescribed outputs and one-way there, with a trapdoor, whose lossy twin is not rank-plus-noise.** This is exactly the object of section 5, not a relaxation of it.
- **Non-negligible correctness failure.** (a) assumes Decode(pp, C) = W always and Decode deterministic (2.1); with failure probability μ over the encoder's coins the counts change by `μ 2^n`, immaterial; a Decode that is correct only for the encoder's own coins is a randomised decoder, outside 2.1 (R4, 2.5 addition).
- **pp derived from W through the random oracle** (`pp = RO(W)`-style, free for everyone to recompute). Inside (d): (a)(iii) holds for every fixed oracle, so the fibre counting and the indexing-hardness requirement are unchanged; only the accounting of pp moves (R4, 2.5 addition).

## 3. Lemma 3: audit soundness given W is incompressibility given W

### 3.1 The three properties

All three are stated for the program's untimed game: the adversary knows (pp, W), the encoding C is block-structured (B blocks of `|c|` bits), pp is whatever the server must hold besides C (pk, seed; for a CRS-model scheme the CRS), and "state" means all server memory (`theory.md` 5.1). Running times are those of the algorithms named; `T_A2` is the time of one challenge answer.

- **AS(S, k, δ)** (audit soundness). For every `(A1, A2)` with `σ = A1(pp, C, W)` of exactly S bits and `A2(σ, I)` answering `I ← [B]^k` with k blocks: `Pr[A2(σ, I) = C_I] ≤ δ`. *Product form*: `A2(σ, I)_j = A2'(σ, i_j)`. *Joint*: arbitrary `A2`. (Tag forgery is a separate additive term, `theory.md` 5.1.)
- **INC(β, π)** (incompressibility given (pp, W); MW20 Definition 4.1 with `A.Select` outputting W and `aux = (W, pp)`, i.e. the message as side information and pp available to compressor and expander). For every `(Cmp, Exp)` with `w = Cmp(pp, W, C)` of exactly β bits: `Pr[Exp(pp, W, w) = C] ≤ π`. MW20 ask π = negl for PPT adversaries; we keep π and the running time explicit. Charging pp: if pp must be on the server it is inside β and S alike; if it is public and recomputable it is side information in both; the two accountings differ by `|pp|` in both β and S, so every statement below is invariant under the choice, for the equivalence of this section, as long as it is the same on both sides (R4 F22: the invariance does not extend to Lemma 2(b)'s separation threshold, where the free and charged games differ, and 3.4 treats them separately).
- **HILL_blk(h)** (HILL entropy given W with per-block independence; MW20 Definition 5.1 with W as side information, in the form of `theory.md` Lemma 1): there is a distribution `Sim` on `(pp', C')` (and the RO table in the RO model) computationally indistinguishable from the real `(pp, C)` given W, under which the blocks are independent given `(pp', W)` with `H_inf(C'_i | pp', W) ≥ h`.

### 3.2 Statement

**Lemma 3 (ours).** Let `β − S =: Δ` be the audit margin in bits (`Δ = (ε − ε')|C|` when `β = (1 − ε')|C|` and `S = (1 − ε)|C|`).

(i) *Compressor ⇒ cheater.* If `(Cmp, Exp)` breaks INC(S, π) then `A1 := Cmp`, `A2(σ, I) := Exp(pp, W, σ)` restricted to I, breaks AS(S, k, π) for every k, with running time `T_Cmp + T_Exp`. Hence AS(S, k, δ) ⇒ INC(S, δ), for every k ≥ 1 and against compressors of the audit adversary's running time.

(ii) *Cheater ⇒ compressor, product form (efficient).* If `(A1, A2')` is a product-form cheater with state S, there is a compressor to `β` bits, of running time `T_A1 + B T_A2'`, that succeeds exactly when the cheater's per-block success `p(σ, C) := |{i : A2'(σ, i) = c_i}|/B` is at least `p_0 := 1 − (β − S − B)/(B |c|)`. Hence INC(β, π) against compressors of that running time ⇒ `Pr[p(σ, C) ≥ p_0] ≤ π` ⇒ AS_prod(S, k, π + p_0^k).

(iii) *Cheater ⇒ compressor, joint strategies (efficient, by sampling).* For any `η in (0, 1)` and `λ' ≥ 1`, if `(A1, A2)` is a joint cheater with state S, there are k compressors to β bits (one per challenge position), each of running time `T_A1 + B R T_A2 + O(B |c|)` with `R := ⌈(λ' + log2 B) ln 2 / η⌉` samples per block, such that INC(β, π) against compressors of that running time ⇒

~~~
AS_joint(S, k, δ)  with  δ = k π + p_η^k + k η + k 2^(−λ'),
p_η := ( 1 − (β − S − B − λ)/(B |c|) ) · |c| / (|c| − log2 R).
~~~

(iv) *HILL.* HILL_blk(h_blk) ⇒ AS_prod and AS_joint with the bounds of `theory.md` Theorems 2a to 2c (information-theoretic compressors in the hybrid; `p* = (1 − ε)/ρ + …`), and HILL_blk(h) ⇒ INC(B h − λ, 2^(−λ) + Adv_dist) (MW20 Theorem 5.2 with the message as side information). The converse, INC ⇒ HILL, does not hold in general (Yao-type and HILL-type conditional entropy differ: Hsiao, Lu, Reyzin, "Conditional computational entropy, or toward separating pseudoentropy from compressibility", EUROCRYPT 2007, Section 3, a distribution (X, Z) with Z a uniform polynomial-length string whose conditional Yao entropy exceeds its conditional HILL entropy under quadratic residuosity; verified by R4 (F26) and by R5 against the text), and AS ⇒ HILL is not claimed.

*Numbers at the operating point* (`ε = 5.263%`, `ε' = 1/32`, `|c| = 98,304`, `B = 6.18 · 10^6`, `λ = 256`, `λ' = 64`, calc): (ii) gives `p_0 = 0.978630`, hence k = 214 for δ = 1% and 640 for 10^(−6) (against `theory.md` 2a/2b's `p* = 0.977939`, k = 207 and 620); (iii) at `η = 2^(−21)` gives `R = 2^26.9`, `p_η = 0.978898`, k = 217 for 1%, and at `η = 2^(−35)`, k = 654 for 10^(−6) (against Theorem 2c's 209 and 631). The Yao route costs 3 to 4% more blocks than the HILL route because it pays `|c|` bits for every wrong block instead of `h_blk`, and `log2 R` instead of `log2(1/η)` per listed block.

**Consequence (the "weaker primitive" question).** By (i) and (ii)/(iii), for the untimed raw-block audit the property AS(S, k, δ) given W and the property INC(β, π) given (pp, W) are the same property up to the margin Δ, the block count k and the additive π, with efficient reductions in both directions. A "weaker primitive from which the audit follows" would have to imply INC given (pp, W), which is the single-block audit itself (k = 1 in (i)); so there is nothing weaker to derive. What remains free is (1) what is charged to S and β (pp accounting: is the CRS or pk on the server, is the RO seed counted), and (2) the idealisation under which INC given W is *proved*: MW20 Theorem 8.1 (Lemma 2(b) here) excludes black-box standard-model proofs of INC given W at `β ≥ |pp| + |C| − |W|`, so a proof at rate `1 − ε'` lives in the RO model (Theorem 5.3, HILL via a programmable oracle) or in a CRS model with `|crs| ≥ (1 − ε'(1 + ε))|W|` (Theorem 7.1), the latter excluded by the 1.05 memory budget (`theory.md` section 4).

### 3.3 Proofs

**(i).** `A1 = Cmp` outputs exactly S bits; on the event `Exp(pp, W, σ) = C`, `A2` answers every index correctly, so `Pr[accept] ≥ π` for any k. The contrapositive is the implication. ∎

**(ii).** Fix `(σ, C)`; let `Bad := {i : A2'(σ, i) ≠ c_i}`. `Cmp` outputs `(σ, bitmap of Bad, (c_i)_{i in Bad})`, padded to exactly β bits, if `S + B + |Bad| |c| ≤ β`, and a fixed dummy string otherwise; `Exp` runs `A2'(σ, i)` for `i ∉ Bad` and copies the listed blocks. `Exp` recovers C exactly on the first case, which is `|Bad| ≤ (β − S − B)/|c|`, i.e. `p(σ, C) ≥ p_0`. Running time: `A1` once, `A2'` on every block, plus copying. For the audit, indices are i.i.d. uniform, so `Pr[all k correct | σ, C] = p(σ, C)^k` in product form, and `E[p^k] ≤ Pr[p ≥ p_0] + p_0^k ≤ π + p_0^k`. ∎

**(iii).** Follow `theory.md` 5.5 with the inefficient list replaced by sampling. Fix `(σ, C)` and a position j. For a block index i let `f_j(i) := Pr_{I : i_j = i}[A2(σ, I)_j = c_i]` over the other `k − 1` coordinates and `L_j(η) := {i : f_j(i) ≥ η}`. The compressor `Cmp_j` (definition per R4 F20, 2026-09-20 (third session): the earlier text listed `L_j(η)` itself, whose membership test `f_j(i) ≥ η` is the inefficient step of `theory.md` Theorem 2c that sampling is meant to remove) outputs `(σ, seed, bitmap of the hit set Ĥ_j, (pos_i)_{i in Ĥ_j}, (c_i)_{i ∉ Ĥ_j})`, padded to β bits or replaced by a dummy if too long, where: `seed` (λ bits) expands to R independent uniform tuples `I^(1), …, I^(R)` with `i_j` left open; `Ĥ_j := {i : some r ≤ R has A2(σ, I^(r)[i_j := i])_j = c_i}` is computed by trying every block against the R samples; for `i in Ĥ_j`, `pos_i in [R]` is the index of the first such r. `Ĥ_j ⊇ L_j(η) \ miss`, where `miss` is the set of blocks of `L_j(η)` hit by no sample, and blocks outside `L_j(η)` that happen to be hit only shorten the output. `Exp_j` regenerates the tuples from the seed and, for each i with a position, runs `A2` once at that tuple. Correctness: exact by construction. Length: at most `S + λ + B + |L_j| log2 R + (B − |L_j| + |miss|) |c|` (every hit block costs `log2 R ≤ |c|` bits instead of `|c|`, so the length is maximal when exactly `L_j(η) \ miss` is hit), where `|miss|` is the number of blocks of `L_j(η)` not hit; `Pr[miss_i] ≤ (1 − η)^R ≤ 2^(−λ' − log2 B)`, so `Pr[|miss| ≥ 1] ≤ 2^(−λ')`. Running time exactly `T_A1 + B R T_A2 + O(B|c|)` (all B blocks are tried). INC(β, π) against this compressor gives `Pr[S + λ + B + |L_j| log2 R + (B − |L_j|)|c| ≤ β] ≤ π + 2^(−λ')`, i.e. with probability `≥ 1 − π − 2^(−λ')`, `|L_j(η)| /B ≤ p_η` with `p_η` as displayed (solve the length constraint for `|L_j|`). Union over the k positions. Then, exactly as in `theory.md` 5.5 step (b), `Pr_I[pass] ≤ Pr_I[∀ j : i_j in L_j(η)] + Σ_j Pr_I[pass ∧ i_j ∉ L_j(η)] ≤ Π_j |L_j(η)|/B + k η ≤ p_η^k + k η`, using that the coordinates of I are independent and uniform (sampling with replacement) and that `L_j` depends on `(σ, C)` only. Total: `k (π + 2^(−λ')) + p_η^k + k η`. ∎

**(iv).** The first part is `theory.md` Theorems 2a to 2c verbatim (their Lemma A is the information-theoretic instance of (ii)/(iii)). The second is MW20 Theorem 5.2's proof with W added to the adversary's input, which the proof allows since W is fixed before the CRS (selective security) and the compressor in the proof is the given one. ∎

### 3.4 Where the random-oracle variant sits

MW20 section 8 ("Implication" paragraphs, read): a CRS-model encoding with CRS length t yields a plain-model one with expansion `α + t`, hence Theorem 8.1's separation for `β ≥ α + t − k`; a *composable* encoding with a shared CRS of any length can be re-blocked to a short-CRS one, hence the separation extends to composable schemes with a shared CRS; Theorem 8.2 separates *adaptively* secure CRS-model schemes at `β ≥ α − k + 2ℓ(λ)` regardless of CRS length. Our audit is composable across blocks and shares pp across all of them, and INC given W is the audit (Lemma 3(i)); so in the standard model the only proofs not excluded are selective ones with a CRS of length `≥ β − α + k ≈ (1 − ε'(1 + ε)) |W|` (Theorem 7.1's data-length CRS), which the 1.05 budget rejects (`theory.md` section 4). The random-oracle model escapes because the oracle is not part of the codeword and can be programmed (Theorem 5.3, proof by hybrids over the RO table): this is the boundary, and it is the boundary for *every* option in the chart, not for the SLF family in particular.

### 3.5 What escapes Lemma 3

- **Timed games.** With a deadline, AS is no longer INC: a server that can *recompute* blocks slowly is caught by time, not by memory. Lemma 4 says every public-encoder option is in this regime.
- **Responses that are not raw blocks.** For aggregate or hashed responses the cheater-to-compressor direction needs an extraction step (PoR-style), and the tap rule loses its byte-for-byte accounting (`theory.md` section 8). The equivalence is for raw blocks.
- **Knowledge of W.** If the server does *not* know W (not our setting: PLAN4 section 1 fixes W as public), INC given W is stronger than needed and MW20's plain Definition 4.1 (no side information) applies; Theorem 8.1 excludes that too.
- **Non-black-box proofs.** As in 2.5.
- **Concrete running time.** (iii)'s compressor makes `B R ≈ 2^(49.5)` calls to A2 at `η = 2^(−21)`; INC has to hold against compressors of that size, which for a lossy-hybrid proof it does (information-theoretic), and for a hypothetical direct assumption would be a concrete-security parameter to state.
- **Adaptive challenges.** Step (b) of the proofs uses i.i.d. uniform challenge indices; a sequential audit whose later indices depend on earlier responses needs the argument rerun per round. PLAN4 section 3's audit is one-shot per round, which is the assumption used (R4, 3.5 addition).
- **Composition of INC-only objects (R5 note 1.5).** Lemma 3 works with INC of the *whole* C given W. MW20 section 5 (as read by R5) leave open whether INC-only (Yao-type) encodings compose across messages and conjecture counterexamples; HILL objects compose for free (MW20 Theorem 5.3, `theory.md` Lemma 1's per-block independence). So a *per-block* INC-only object, the direct-route object of section 5 item 5 (ii), needs its own composition lemma (per-block INC given the other blocks and W implies whole-encoding INC given W) before Lemma 3's k-block plug-in applies to it; a direct-route proof stated for the whole encoding at once needs none.

## 4. Lemma 4: public encoders need a deadline

**Lemma 4 (ours).** Let (Setup, Encode, Decode) be an encoding in which Encode is public: `C = Encode(pp, W; r)` with pp public, r either absent or public (part of C or pp), and no secret erased. Then:

(a) `H_inf(C | pp, W, r) = 0` and hence `H_HILL(C | pp, W, r) = 0`: any simulator's `C'` must equal `Encode(pp', W; r')`, or the distinguisher recomputes Encode and compares. The (real or HILL) entropy of C given (pp, W) is at most `|r|`, and MW20 Theorem 8.1 caps real entropy given W at `|C| − |W|` bits (`≈ ε |C|`) for any efficiently decodable encoding, so the codeword's retained entropy is at most `min(|r|, ε|C|)`.

(b) The server strategy "store `(pp, r, Z(W))`, where `Z` is any lossless compressor of W, and answer challenge i by re-encoding block i" passes every untimed raw-block audit with probability 1 from state `|pp| + |r| + |Z(W)|`. For W with `|Z(W)| ≤ (1 − ε)|C| − |pp| − |r|` this is a successful cheater; PLAN4 section 1 admits arbitrary W including `W = 0^n`, for which `|Z(W)| = O(log n)`; for bf16 weights of a trained model, entropy coding alone gives `|Z(W)|/|W|` well below `(1 − ε)(1 + ε_x) ≈ 0.995` (about 0.66 to 0.70 for bf16 weights: ZipNN, Hershcovitch et al., arXiv 2411.05239, 66 to 67% of the original size, exponent byte compressing about 3x; DFloat11, Zhang et al., arXiv 2504.11651, NeurIPS 2025, about 11 bits per weight, 70%, bit-exact; verified by R4, F27). Hence no statement of the form AS(S, k, δ) with `S = (1 − ε)|C|` and δ below 1 holds for all W; untimed incompressibility given W is impossible for public encoders. For an *incompressible* W the same strategy needs `|W| = |C|/(1 + ε_x) = 0.952 |C|` of state, above `(1 − ε*)|C| = 0.947 |C|`, so it must drop 0.5% of W and answers 99.5% of blocks: the audit then certifies that the server holds W, with an effective rate `1/(1 + ε_x) = 0.952` (margin 0.5 points instead of 2.1, k = 873 for 1%; R4 F24), and it never certifies that the server holds C, which is the memory-occupancy claim the program makes.

(c) The only leverage left is time: the cheater of (b) must run Encode on the challenged blocks between challenge and response, so an audit with deadline Δ is sound against it only if `Δ` is below the time to encode the k challenged blocks at the adversary's speed `t_min`, and proving that needs a lower bound on that time: the timed model with a sequential-cost assumption of phases 2 and 3 (regeneration bounds, `research/protocol2.md`; depth-robust or sequential encoders). Any option in the chart with a public encoder must therefore be filed under "deadline Δ with stated `t_min`" in `TEMPLATE.md` section 0, and its section 1 must state the timed game.

*Proof.* (a) C is a deterministic function of `(pp, W, r)`, all known to the adversary; a simulator whose `C'` is not `Encode(pp', W; r')` is distinguished by one run of Encode; conditioning on `(pp, W)` alone leaves at most `|r|` bits of uncertainty; Theorem 8.1's count `|C_m| ≤ 2^(α − k)` for the smallest fibre bounds real entropy given W by `α − k` for the worst message (and the average-case bound `t + α − n` of Lemma 2(a)(iii) with t = 0 gives the same on average). (b) The cheater decompresses `Z(W)` and reruns `Encode(pp, W; r)` in full, then answers with the exact `c_i`; time is not counted in the untimed game, and block-locality of Encode is not needed (R4 F23, 2026-09-20 (third session): the earlier parenthetical inferred local encodability from local decodability plus determinism, which does not follow, since the encoder may choose `c_i` inside the fibre of `w_i` using all of W and the seed, and is not needed here; locality matters only in the timed regime of (c), where it decides how much of Encode the cheater must rerun per challenge). The response equals `c_i` exactly, so tags verify. State as stated. (c) The cheater of (b) is caught only if it fails to produce `c_i` in time; that is a statement about the running time of Encode on the adversary's hardware, i.e. the timed model. ∎

*What escapes Lemma 4.* Nothing in the untimed game: the lemma is a two-line counting statement. In the timed game the entire phase 2 and 3 apparatus (depth-robust graphs, `t_min`, ASIC margins) returns, which PLAN4 section 0 lists as exactly what the trusted-encoder setting was chosen to avoid. A public encoder with a *secret* verifier key that the server never sees does not change (a) or (b), since the server decodes from public data (T2 3.4).

## 5. What the four results leave open: the missing object

The four results close, respectively: every rank-plus-noise lattice family (Theorem 1), W-dependent setup as a route around trapdoor preimage sampling (Lemma 2), any "weaker primitive" for the untimed game (Lemma 3), and every public-encoder scheme in the untimed game (Lemma 4). What they leave open is one object. This section specifies it at the level of a definition so that a candidate can be checked against it line by line; the wording follows T2's `constructions.md` section 6.3 (properties 1 to 4 there) and adds what Lemmas 2 to 4 force.

**Specification 5.1 (the missing object).** A family `(SurGen, LossyGen, f, D, Inv)` in the sense of MW20 Definitions 6.1 and 6.2 (an `(r, r', d, e, ℓ)`-SLF*) such that:

1. *Shape and cost (T2 6.3 property 1, sharpened by Theorem 1 (I6); budgets revised 2026-09-20 21:40 PDT with D1's measurements).* `f_pk` maps `1.03 n` words of w bits to n words, `w ≤ 32`, on tiles of 1 to 25 KB (`n w` from `8 · 10^3` to `2 · 10^5` bits), with pk of at most a few tile-sizes shared by all tiles; forward cost per decoded byte, *including* the random-oracle offset `u_i = RO(seed, i)`, within the budget of the target batch. Two budget rules are on record. *T2's derived rule* (T2 6.2, from PLAN4 3.1's memory-bound GEMM baseline and the memcpy rate): at most 0.5 int32 or 30 int8 tensor-core MACs per byte for +10%, 2.8 int32 or 165 MACs for +50%, 5.6 int32 or 330 MACs for +100%; offset about 7 int32 per byte for a ChaCha8-class XOF and about 3 for a counter-mode XOF (T2 3.1.3, estimates). *D1's measured box* (`decoder.md` section 3.7, fused `mma.sync` bf16 kernel at 1.05x cuBLASLt on the Qwen2.5-7B sequence, synthetic decode in registers, min of 10 launches): at `b ≤ 8`, `R_seq ≤ 1.10` admits 1.2 int32 issue slots or 13 int8 tensor-core MACs per weight byte, `R_seq ≤ 1.5` admits 5.4 or 116, `R_seq ≤ 2` admits 9.1 or 210; at `b = 32` the fused kernel's own overhead (1.29x) leaves 0 and 0 at 1.10 and 8.1 or 163 at 2x; the ChaCha8 XOF was measured at 4.1 TB/s, about 4 issue slots per byte at the 16.65 T IMAD/s rate (6.8 SASS per byte co-issued over two integer pipes), and Philox4x32-10 at 5.8 TB/s, about 3 slots per byte (`decoder.md` section 3.5). Both rules give the same qualitative verdict; the measured one is the reference from here on. The offset alone (3 to 4 slots per byte) exceeds the +10% budget (1.2 slots), so in the RO model the +10% budget is unreachable for *any* MW-template encoding regardless of the SLF, and +50% leaves 1 to 2 slots per byte; the +100% budget leaves about 5 int32 issue slots per byte of CUDA-core work for the function itself after a ChaCha8 offset and about 6 after a Philox offset (9.1 minus 4 or 3), or, on the tensor-core path, up to 210 int8 MACs per byte with the whole int32 side (offset, reductions, nonlinearity) under 9.1 slots; at `b = 32` nothing fits below 2x and 8.1 minus the offset, 4 to 5 slots, at 2x. *Coordinator note (2026-09-20 21:50 PDT): D1's section 3.8 batch sweep (13 batch sizes, 3,400 rows) tightens the box: at `b ≤ 8` the fused kernel's own `R_seq` is 1.07 to 1.08 and the slope is +0.158 `R_seq` per int32 op per byte, so `R_seq ≤ 1.10` admits 0.3 to 0.4 int32 issue slots or 7 to 8 int8 MACs per weight byte (not 1.2 / 13), 1.5x admits 4.3 / 103, 2x admits 8 / 200; at `b = 16` nothing fits at 1.10; only prefill chunks (`b = 8,192`, decode-then-GEMM) open to 56 / 144 int32 at 1.5x / 2x. The synthetic 7-IMAD ChaCha8 stand-in measured 1.83x at decode batch; the real ChaCha8 keystream decoder run bit-exactly inside the fused GEMM (D1 section 3.9) measures 1.28x at `b ≤ 8` (Philox 1.17x, a minimal non-RO mixer 1.09x on a 1.07x kernel baseline), because ARX code co-issues over two integer pipes; it leaves the SLF about 1.4 int32 or 35 int8 MACs per byte under 1.5x and about 4.5 int32 or 120 MACs under 2x. The verdict is unchanged and sharper: the offset alone is roughly ten times the +10% allowance, and +100% leaves about 4 to 5 slots per byte after the offset.* *Coordinator note (2026-09-21 07:00 PDT, after the user's review): "unreachable for any MW-template encoding in the RO model" above is a measured statement about every credible offset generator identified (CUDA-core PRGs; Philox is near the floor of the ARX class), not a lower bound on offset generation. The one computational shape that could fit under 1.10 is a tensor-core-native PRG or hash at ≤ 7 int8 MACs per byte, itself an unanalysed cryptographic object (phase 2's open question of a matrix-multiply hash standing in for a random oracle); that is the falsifier of the +10% closure.* A data-length CRS instead of the RO (MW20 Theorem 7.1) costs 1 byte moved (about +85 to 100% of the sequence time by D1's box: the weight stream *is* the runtime) and about 1.5 int32 per byte but doubles memory, which the 1.05 budget excludes (`theory.md` section 4, R2 section 4 row "6.2").
2. *Surjective mode (T2 6.3 property 2; Lemma 2(d)(iv); wording revised 2026-09-20 21:40 PDT, see 8.3).* `f_pk` regular under the public domain distribution `D(pk)` with a trapdoor sampler `Inv_td` producing `D(pk)` conditioned on the target, for every target in the range (Definition 6.1), and fibre indexing hard at the targets without td (Lemma 2(d)(iii), whose proof shows exactly that an efficient short index of `c_i` inside its fibre compresses C; this is weaker than one-wayness of `f_pk`): a trapdoor in the abstract sense of Corollary 8.3 (a secret tied to pk that lets the encoder do what the public cannot); every known instantiation at this rate is an SLF or trapdoor-permutation family (MW20 6.1 and 6.2, DGO19), and the implication "abstract trapdoor implies trapdoor function implies public-key encryption" is *not* claimed here (8.3 says what is missing; decisively, many-to-one trapdoor one-way functions with super-polynomial fibres exist from any one-way function, Bellare–Halevi–Sahai–Vadhan, CRYPTO 1998, ePrint 1998/019, Theorem 1, and only polynomially-bounded-preimage trapdoor functions are known to give public-key encryption, their Theorem 2; R4 F18). Trapdoor permutations from sub-exponential iO and one-way functions exist and are not factoring-based (Bitansky, Paneth, Wichs, TCC 2016-A, ePrint 2015/126, abstract and Section 1 read), which only shows that "trapdoor" does not mean "number theory"; on cost they are irrelevant here. No Minicrypt relaxation (QWW21-style) can replace it (2.4).
3. *Lossy mode (T2 6.3 property 3; Theorem 1).* The image of `f_pk` in lossy mode has size at most `2^(0.031 · 1.03 n w)` *by construction*, i.e. `ℓ ≤ 0.031 d` exactly, not up to a noise term: any error that has to be rounded away re-introduces (I1) and (I4) and the `m/d ≥ 13` floor; any rank term re-introduces (I3). So the lossy twin is not `Ā S + E` over `Z_q` or `R_q`.
4. *Indistinguishability (T2 6.3 property 4; Theorem 1 (I0)).* The two pk distributions are 128-bit indistinguishable for a function whose whole input is one tile, with no coupling between the hardness parameter and the word or tile size of the kind `k N ≥ 47 L` (which pushes tiles to MB, Theorem 1 (I5)).
5. *Encoder, target property and proof route (Lemmas 3, 4; revised 2026-09-20 21:40 PDT: the earlier text specified the proof as HILL entropy, which over-specifies).* The encoder holds a secret tied to pk (td, or the coins of Setup) and erases it (Lemma 4: a public encoder is timed-model material). The property the object must have is INC(β, π) given W in the sense of section 3, with β the server's state bound `S = 0.947 |C|` plus Lemma 3's margin and π the audit's cryptographic error: by Lemma 3 that *is* raw-block audit soundness, up to the stated k and δ, and nothing weaker suffices. Two proof routes are admissible. (i) *The twin route*: an indistinguishable lossy twin gives HILL entropy of C given (pk, seed, W), which implies INC by Lemma 3(iv) (MW20's proof; standard model with a data-length CRS, or RO model with a short seed; MW20 Theorem 8.1 excludes short public parameters in the standard model). HILL implies INC; the converse is not claimed and is not needed. (ii) *The direct route*: a compression argument in an idealised model (random oracle, ideal cipher or ideal permutation), Pietrzak-style, bounding directly the probability that a bounded-state adversary with q oracle queries reproduces a block; this is DGO19's and GLW20's route and the one B1 is exploring for single-round trapdoor-permutation inversion (`OPTIONS.md` rows B1 to B3, memo `tdp.md`, not yet on record). Properties 3 and 4 above, and the SLF framing of the whole specification, are *twin-route-specific*: they describe the object that makes route (i) work. Route (ii) needs no lossy mode and no indistinguishable twin; it needs a public map whose inversion at a target costs the encoder one trapdoor operation and costs everyone else, in the ideal model, either a stored block or a query budget of `2^λ`. So the missing object is, precisely: *property 3 (a hidden small image with an indistinguishable twin), or a direct INC proof in an idealised model*, in both cases at the cost of property 1 and with the abstract trapdoor of 8.3. The on-record constraint for the idealised-permutation route is GLW20 (ePrint 2020/617, as read by T2, `constructions.md` row 5a and section 3.5, log 19:00): the DGO19 chain of TDP inversions through an ideal permutation is secure only with `r = n b λ` rounds (b the number of blocks of the whole file), and an iO-based TDP breaks any `o(n b)` round count; so a chain is decode cost linear in the file size, and a *non-chain* structure, single-round per block with the security carried by the ideal-model query bound alone, is what route (ii) would need (B1's question). pk and seed are charged to the 1.05 budget in either route. *Composition (R5 note 1.5, added 2026-09-20 (third session)).* Lemma 3 is stated for INC of the whole C given W. MW20 section 5 leave open whether INC-only (Yao-type) encodings compose across messages and conjecture counterexamples, while HILL objects compose for free (Theorem 5.3; `theory.md` Lemma 1's per-block independence). A route-(ii) object defined *per block* therefore needs its own composition lemma (per-block INC given the other blocks and W implies whole-encoding INC given W) before Lemma 3's k-block statements apply to it; equivalently, its INC property should be stated for the whole encoding at once (3.5).

**Which mechanisms for property 3 are excluded, and by what (coordinated with T2 6.3's list).**

- *Rank-plus-noise over `Z_q`, `R_q` (LWE, ring/module-LWE, LWR, NTRU-quality trapdoors; Jin et al. 2024's compact LWR LTF as the surjective extension).* Theorem 1, for every such twin whose security proof is the template's (rate certified as `(e − ℓ)/d_bits`; R4 F1): the noise forces the gap (I1) or the leak (I4), the rank forces (I3), and hardness forces (I5); the closed-form floor is about 250 int32 per byte at expansion 1.05 and blocks of at least 2.6 MB (loose closed form; about 10 MB at the joint minimum, R4 F10); Corollary 1.2 excludes word-size moduli below expansion 1.33. Excluded by shape and size *on the twin route*; a rank-plus-noise map proved by a direct compression argument in an idealised model (item 5, route (ii)) is not excluded by Theorem 1, and no such proof is known.
- *Exactly rank-deficient linear maps over a ring with efficient linear algebra (any field, `Z_q` with known q, `Z_N` with hidden factorisation).* T2 6.3: Gaussian elimination computes the rank, or over `Z_N` produces a factor; the small kernel is elimination-detectable, so the twin is distinguishable without any assumption. Excluded.
- *Subgroups of groups of unknown order (DCR, Damgård–Jurik, Φ-hiding RSA; MW20 6.1).* Property 3 holds exactly and property 2 holds (MW20 Section 6.1; `theory.md` Lemma 1 is this case), but property 1 fails by seven to eight orders of magnitude: the `s = 31` exponentiation on 98,304-bit elements is about `3 · 10^7` 64-bit-limb-equivalent MACs per byte as GMP executes it (mw3's 92 s per 12 KiB word at 1.115 G limb-MACs/s per core) and `2` to `3.5 · 10^8` 32-bit MACs per byte in schoolbook form (T2 `twins.md` row 1a; the `10^4` figure that stood here until 2026-09-20 21:50 PDT is the Φ-hiding RSA figure of T2 row 4b, corrected by T2), and the hidden order is the security, so no small-word analogue exists. Excluded by cost. *Remark (added 2026-09-20 21:40 PDT; Corollary 8.4).* Within this family the lossiness is capped: lossy RSA under Φ-hiding (Kiltz, O'Neill, Smith, CRYPTO 2010, full version ePrint 2011/559, Section 5) loses `log2 e` bits per element and is broken by Coppersmith once `e ≥ N^(1/4)`, so an RSA twin gives lossiness below 25% of the element and cheap exponents (`e = 3`, `257`) give a negligible fraction; Damgård–Jurik's `s/(s + 1)` (MW20 6.1, `theory.md` Lemma 1) is the only factoring-based route to 95% (`s ≥ 19`), at mw3's cost.
- *Encrypted rank deficiency (PW08 DDH/QR matrix LTDFs, BKPW12).* Expansion above 2 and one exponentiation per output group element (T2 rows 4c). Excluded by rate and cost.
- *Noise over `F_2` or small fields (LPN, codes).* Lossiness needs sparse inputs, sparse inputs kill surjectivity (T2 3.2); uniform-domain LWR (R2 2.3) needs `d/m ≥ 31` and has no regular sampler. Excluded by structure.
- *Targeted setup (Lemma 2).* Not a mechanism for property 3: W-dependence of pk changes the accounting of offsets, not the lossy twin.
- *Symmetric keyed permutations, MQ trapdoors, isogeny group actions, obfuscation-based lossy functions.* T2 6.3's entries stand: no trapdoor (ciphers), MinRank-detectable small image and `n²/2` cost (MQ), no non-injective mode of the action itself and `10^7` word operations per element (group actions), cost by orders of magnitude (iO).

**What would have to be discovered (T2 6.3's closing paragraph, restated with the four results; budgets revised 2026-09-20 21:40 PDT with D1's box).** A trapdoor family (in the abstract sense of 8.3) of word-level keyed maps `f_pk` on 1 to 25 KB tiles with a public key shared by all tiles, evaluable within about 5 to 6 int32 issue slots per byte on CUDA cores after the RO offset or within 210 int8 MACs per byte on tensor cores at `b ≤ 8`, that either is exactly `(1.03 : 1)`-regular with a trapdoor sampler in one mode (property 2), exactly `2^(−0.031 n w)`-compressing in the other (property 3), fibre-indexing-hard at prescribed targets without the trapdoor, and whose two modes are 128-bit indistinguishable given the key (property 4), with the small image hidden by something other than noise (Theorem 1), unknown group order (cost and the 8.4 cap), or a detectable kernel (elimination); or admits a direct INC proof in an idealised model without a chain (item 5). Such an object is a public-key-flavoured primitive at symmetric-key cost; no assumption family currently offers one (T2 6.3), and Lemma 3 says that the audit cannot be proved from anything weaker, so its absence is the program's outcome (ii) of PLAN4 (a proved-secure construction exists, the DCR one of `theory.md`, and no known family passes the cost gate). Against D1's measured box the RO offset alone (about 4 slots per byte for ChaCha8, about 3 for Philox) exceeds the +10% budget of 1.2 slots at `b ≤ 8`, so no MW-template object meets +10% in the RO model whatever `f_pk` costs; +100% leaves about 5 int32 slots per byte on CUDA cores after a ChaCha8 offset and about 6 after Philox (9.1 minus 4 or 3), or 210 int8 tensor-core MACs per byte with the int32 side under 9.1, and that is the whole room in which `f_pk` has to fit.

**Not open (for the record).** Lowering the retention target r (T2 6.1) rescales Theorem 1's ε' and moves the lattice floor to 87 to 145 int32 per byte at r = 0.5, still 9x to 15x over budget, and does not touch Lemmas 2 to 4. Trusted-verifier secrets do not change the server's public decoder (T2 3.4). Deterministic trapdoor encodings have zero entropy given W (`theory.md` section 9; T2 5a with GLW20's `r = n b λ` rounds).

## 8. Corollaries: the affordances closed, and the necessary condition stated

Added 2026-09-20, 21:15 to 22:45 PDT, after the user's discussion with an outside reviewer of the "affordances" argument; sections 0 to 7 are unchanged except for the dated revision notes in section 5 (items 1, 2, 5, the unknown-order bullet and the closing paragraph). The options chart (`research/OPTIONS.md`) lists, for each option, the affordances it uses: a model-dependent (W-dependent) setup, an `Eval` interface that returns `X · W` without materialising W, batching, large security blocks, one key for the whole model, and tile reuse inside a GEMM. The four corollaries below sort these into *cryptographic escapes* (routes around the object of section 5) and *performance affordances* (ways to pay for that object more cheaply), and state what every scheme meeting Specification 5.1 must contain. Notation is that of sections 0 and 2.1: `n = |W|`, `α = |C|`, `t = |pp|`, `D_pp := Decode(pp, ·)`, `F_pp(W) := {c : D_pp(c) = W}` (the fibre of W), `X = α/n` the expansion, `r = 18/19 = 0.947` the retention fraction of the audit's cheater (`S = r α`), and INC(β, π) given W as in 3.1. Corollaries 8.1 to 8.3 are ours; 8.4 is a reading of the cited papers.

### 8.1 Counting, two forms

**Corollary 8.1 (counting, with the quantifiers).** Let (SetupEncode, Decode) be correct on every `W ∈ {0,1}^n`, with SetupEncode possibly randomised, `|pp| ≤ t`, `|C| ≤ α`, and let W be uniform for the average-case statements.

(i) *The target-dependence burden (Lemma 2(a) in information form).* `H(W | C) ≤ t` and hence `I(W ; C) ≥ n − t`: across all W the C-component of `SetupEncode(W)` carries at least `n − t` bits of dependence on W, and a t-bit model-dependent setup moves at most t bits of that burden out of C. In particular "sample C first, then program pp so that `D_pp(C) = W`" (C independent of W) is correct only if `t ≥ n`.

(ii) *Fixed pp: fibres partition the codeword space.* For every fixed pp the fibres `F_pp(W)`, `W ∈ {0,1}^n`, are pairwise disjoint subsets of `{0,1}^α`, so `Σ_W |F_pp(W)| ≤ 2^α` and the fibre of a uniform W has on average at most `2^(α − n)` elements. Consequently the statistical min-entropy of C given (W, pp), averaged over W, is at most
~~~
E_W[ H_inf(C | W, pp) ]  ≤  α − n + I_pp,     I_pp := n − E_pp[ H_inf(W | pp) ]  ∈ [0, t],
~~~
where `I_pp` is what pp reveals about W (zero for a W-independent setup such as MW20's (pk, seed), however many coins it uses; at most t for a targeted setup). At X = 1.05 with W-independent pp this is `(1 − 1/X) α = 0.048 α`: the codeword has under 5% of its length in real entropy given the message and the public parameters, against the 94.7% that r = 18/19 requires. Incompressibility at r = 18/19 is therefore necessarily computational, in every model: MW20 say so in their overview ("in reality the codeword c has very little actual entropy given crs, m. This is inherent since we want c to be almost the same size as m and it has to decode to m so there is no space left to inject actual entropy", MW20 section 1.3 "Our Techniques", the paragraph following their lossy-mode sketch; the proof of Theorem 8.1 in their section 8 is the same count, `|C_m*| ≤ 2^(α − k)` for the message with the smallest fibre). Every admissible proof is therefore either indistinguishability from a lossy twin in which the fibres are large (route (i) of item 5) or a query-bounded argument in an idealised model (route (ii)).

(iii) *What counting does not give.* Neither (i) nor (ii) bounds the fibre of a *particular* W: for a fixed pp, one message can have a fibre of size `2^α − 2^n + 1` while every other has a single codeword, and the Markov tail `Pr_W[ |F_pp(W)| ≥ 2^(α − n + j) ] ≤ 2^(−j)` is all that (ii) yields pointwise. For the adversary's W (arbitrary, PLAN4 section 1) the relevant quantity is not the fibre's size but whether it can be *indexed* efficiently (8.3): a large fibre that the public can index is as compressible as a small one, and a small fibre is compressible only if its elements can be named.

*Proof.* (i) `W = D_pp(C)` is a function of (pp, C), so `H(W | C, pp) = 0` and `H(W | C) ≤ H(W | C, pp) + H(pp | C) ≤ 0 + log2 |supp pp| ≤ t`; then `I(W ; C) = H(W) − H(W | C) ≥ n − t`. If C is independent of W then `I(W ; C) = 0`, so `n ≤ t`. The same computation with the encoder's coins included in the conditioning changes nothing, since W is still a function of (pp, C). (ii) Disjointness: Decode is a function, so no c lies in two fibres. `C ∈ F_pp(W)` gives `H_inf(C | W = w, pp) ≤ log2 |F_pp(w)|` pointwise. For W independent of pp and uniform, Jensen gives `E_W[ log2 |F_pp(W)| ] ≤ log2 E_W[ |F_pp(W)| ] ≤ log2 (2^α / 2^n) = α − n`. In general, for each pp, `Σ_w P(w | pp) log2 |F_pp(w)| ≤ log2 Σ_w P(w | pp) |F_pp(w)| ≤ log2 ( max_w P(w | pp) · 2^α ) = α − H_inf(W | pp)`, and averaging over pp gives `α − n + I_pp`; `I_pp ≤ t` because `E_pp[ H_inf(W | pp) ] ≥ H̃_inf(W | pp) ≥ H_inf(W) − t` (Jensen for the first inequality, since average min-entropy is `−log2 E[2^(−H_inf)]` and `−log2` is convex; the chain rule of Dodis, Ostrovsky, Reyzin, Smith 2008, Lemma 2.2(b), for the second). The numbers: `1 − 1/1.05 = 0.0476`. (iii) The example is `D_pp(c) := W_0` for all c outside a set of `2^n − 1` codewords in bijection with the other messages. Markov: `Σ_W |F_pp(W)| ≤ 2^α` gives `#{W : |F_pp(W)| ≥ 2^(α − n + j)} ≤ 2^(n − j)`. ∎

*What escapes 8.1.* Nothing: both forms are counting. What 8.1 does not decide is which computational route (twin or idealised model) a scheme takes; that is item 5 of section 5.

### 8.2 The Eval interface

**Setting.** Instead of a public `Decode`, the server is given a public algorithm `Eval(pp, X, C)` meant to return `X · W` for activation matrices X of a conforming class 𝒳 (a b × n' matrix over the representable values, W an n' × m' matrix of bf16 entries, the product over the ring 𝓡 in which the GEMM is exact, e.g. integers or a fixed-point ring after the bf16 mantissas are lifted; PLAN4 section 1 defines the server's task as `Y = A · W` and its correctness as `w_i = Decode(pk, c_i)`, i.e. bit-exact). The affordance claimed is that never materialising W might remove the public-decoder obligations of sections 2, 3 and 8.1.

**Corollary 8.2 (Eval is Decode).** (a) If `Eval(pp, X, C) = X · W` exactly for every `X ∈ 𝒳` and 𝒳 contains the standard basis vectors `e_1, …, e_n'` (or any X invertible over 𝓡, or any spanning set of size poly(n')), then `Decode(pp, C) := (Eval(pp, e_j, C))_j` (respectively `X^(−1) · Eval(pp, X, C)`) is a public decoder of cost at most n' Eval calls (one call and one inversion), and everything proved for public decoders, Lemma 2, Lemma 3, Lemma 4 and Corollary 8.1, applies to the Eval scheme verbatim. This uses exactness only; approximate floating-point linearity does not escape, because the program's correctness requirement is bit-exact recovery of W, so the exact map exists and (a) applies to it. (b) If moreover Eval is *bilinear*, i.e. `Eval(pp, X, ·)` is 𝓡-linear in C for each fixed X, as every GEMM-like map that feeds C's words into the multiply-accumulate stage is, then `W = L_pp(C)` with `L_pp := Eval(pp, I, ·)` a public 𝓡-linear map from `𝓡^(M)` (the codeword, `M log2 |𝓡| = α`) onto `𝓡^(N')` (the weights, `N' log2 |𝓡| = n`), and there is an efficient injective description of C given W of `α − n` bits: Gaussian elimination (Smith or Howell normal form over `𝓡 = Z/2^w` or `F_p`) gives a public right-inverse `R_pp` with `L_pp R_pp = id` and a basis of `ker L_pp`, and `ι(C) := coordinates of C − R_pp(W) in that basis` is a bijection between `F_pp(W) = R_pp(W) + ker L_pp` and `𝓡^(M − N')`, of `α − n` bits, invertible by one matrix-vector product. Hence INC(β, π) given W fails with π = 1 at every `β ≥ α − n`, i.e. at every retention `r ≥ 1 − 1/X = 0.048`, and by Lemma 3(i) the audit fails at every k from state `α − n + t`. (c) Consequently any nonlinearity that produces security must act on C *before* the multiply-accumulate stage: the server's pipeline is `C → (nonlinear public map, per tile) → W-tile → MMA`, the nonlinear map is a public decoder in the sense of (a), and its cost per decoded byte is exactly what Theorem 1 (for rank-plus-noise twins) and Specification 5.1 (in general) bound. "Eval instead of Decode" and "model-dependent setup" (Lemma 2, 8.1(i)) are therefore closed as cryptographic escapes. Batching (b), large security blocks (tiles), one key for the whole model, and tile reuse within a GEMM are performance affordances only: they change how often and how the public decoder is paid for, not what it must compute, and D1's box (`decoder.md` section 3.7, quoted in item 1) measures what each buys at each b.

*Proof.* (a) With `X = e_j`, `X · W` is the j-th row of W; n' calls give W exactly. With an invertible X in 𝒳, `X^(−1) (X · W) = W` over 𝓡. If 𝒳 contained neither, the server could not serve activations spanning `𝓡^(n')`, i.e. could not serve generic inputs, which contradicts the server's task. The cost statement is immediate. Exactness: PLAN4 section 1 requires `w_i = Decode(pk, c_i)` for the honest server, and the audit's tags check the *codeword* blocks bit for bit (`theory.md` 5.1), so an Eval that only approximates `X · W` does not describe an honest server of the program; where it is exact, (a) applies. (b) Bilinearity at `X = I` gives `W = L_pp(C)` with `L_pp` 𝓡-linear and public (pp and Eval are public). Correctness on every W makes `L_pp` surjective onto `𝓡^(N')`, so `|ker L_pp| = |𝓡|^M / |𝓡|^(N') = 2^(α − n)` and every fibre `F_pp(W) = R_pp(W) + ker L_pp` has exactly `2^(α − n)` elements (a coset of the kernel). Over a field, `R_pp` and a kernel basis come from Gaussian elimination in `O(M N'^2)` ring operations; over `Z/2^w` (bf16 mantissas and integers lifted to machine words) the Howell form (or Smith form over the local ring `Z/2^w`) gives the same in polynomial time, and the kernel is free of rank `M − N'` (a surjection onto a free module over a local ring splits, and finitely generated projective modules over a local ring are free; R4 F29), so its coordinates total `(M − N') w = α − n` bits. `Cmp(pp, W, C) := ι(C)`, `Exp(pp, W, u) := R_pp(W) + Σ u_i k_i` recover C exactly, so `(Cmp, Exp)` breaks INC(α − n, 1) given W; Lemma 3(i) converts this into a cheater with state `α − n` (plus t if pp is charged) that answers every challenge. `(α − n)/α = 1 − 1/X`. (c) If the map `C ↦ Y` is linear in C at every stage, (b) applies; if some stage is nonlinear in C, that stage precedes the MMA (which is bilinear by definition) and outputs the W-tile that the MMA consumes, so it is a decoder for that tile, public because pp and the pipeline are public; its cost per byte of W is the forward-map cost that Theorem 1 (I6) and item 1 of Specification 5.1 bound. The last sentence restates D1's section 3.7: the fused kernel's measured budget at each b is what remains after the MMA, for whatever public map the tile passes through. ∎

*What escapes 8.2.* (a) needs 𝒳 to span; an Eval restricted to a proper subspace of activations would decode only a projection of W, but then the server cannot compute `X · W` for activations outside that subspace, which the program requires. (b) needs bilinearity over a ring in which the GEMM is exact; a map that is linear over the reals with floating-point rounding is not linear over any ring, but by (a) it is a public decoder anyway, so it escapes (b) only to fall under (a) and section 5. Nothing escapes (c).

### 8.3 The necessary condition, stated abstractly

**Corollary 8.3 (fibre indexing versus fibre sampling).** Fix pp, and let `s ≥ 0`.

(a) If there is an efficient injective map `ι_pp,W : F_pp(W) → {0,1}^(α − n + s)` with an efficient inverse on its image (an *indexing* of the fibre of W), computable from (pp, W) and oracle access where the model has one, then `(Cmp := ι, Exp := ι^(−1))` breaks INC(α − n + s, 1) given W, so INC given W fails at every `β ≥ α − n + s`, i.e. at every retention `r ≥ (α − n + s)/α`, and by Lemma 3(i) the raw-block audit fails at every k from state `α − n + s` (plus t if pp is charged). At the program's parameters this is every `s ≤ (r − 1 + 1/X) α = 0.90 α`: for the audit to be sound the fibre of the adversary's W must admit no efficient indexing into fewer than `0.947 α` bits, although by 8.1(ii) its average size is `2^(0.048 α)`.

(b) Correctness requires the encoder to produce, for every W, including adversarially chosen W, an element of `F_pp(W)`, efficiently (*sampling* the fibre).

(c) Hence every scheme meeting Specification 5.1 has, for the same pp: fibre indexing hard for the public (a), fibre sampling easy for the encoder (b). The encoder's advantage is a secret tied to pp, the trapdoor td or the coins of Setup from which pp was derived and which pp does not reveal, and Lemma 4 is the case where that secret is absent or public. This is a trapdoor in the abstract sense: a secret associated with the public parameters that enables an efficient operation the public cannot perform. Nothing here says the operation is inverting a permutation, nor that pp defines a function with a trapdoor in the sense of Definition 6.1 or of trapdoor permutations: the necessary condition is the pair (a), (b), not any particular algebraic object.

*Proof.* (a) `Cmp(pp, W, C) := ι(C)` has `α − n + s` bits; `Exp(pp, W, u) := ι^(−1)(u) = C` since ι is injective on the fibre and C lies in it. This is a `(β = α − n + s, π = 1)` compressor of running time `T_ι + T_ι^(−1)`; INC(β', π) fails for every `β' ≥ β` by padding. Lemma 3(i) gives the cheater. The number: `r α ≥ α − n + s` iff `s ≤ (r − 1 + 1/X) α = (0.947 − 0.048) α`. (b) is the correctness condition of 2.1 read for the encoder. (c) If the encoder's fibre sampler used only information the server holds after encoding (pp, W, public coins), then C is a function of those and Lemma 4(a) and (b) apply: the state `(pp, coins, Z(W))` passes every untimed audit. So the sampler uses something the server does not hold: coins ρ of Setup that were erased, or a trapdoor td derived from them; pp was produced by the same Setup from the same coins, which is the sense in which the secret is tied to pp, and by (a) pp must not reveal enough of it to make indexing efficient. ∎

**The reading of Lemma 2(d)(iii) and of section 5 item 2 ("one-way at the targets").** What 2.2(d)(iii) proves, and what its proof uses, is exactly (a): an efficient short index of `c_i` within its fibre compresses C. That is *weaker* than one-wayness of `D_pp` at the targets (finding *some* preimage of `W_i`), because a fibre can be easy to enter and hard to index (the public may know one canonical element of `F_pp(W_i)` and still have no short name for the element the encoder chose); and it is one-wayness that would be needed to call `D_pp` a trapdoor *function*. Item 2 has been reworded accordingly (revision note in place). The wording "one-way at the targets" in 2.2(d)(iii), its proof, 2.4 items 1 and 4, and the summary has now been replaced by "fibre-indexing-hard at the targets" in place (2026-09-20, third session; R5 F1.4, R4 F17/F18); the proofs there prove the weaker statement and nothing downstream uses the stronger one.

**Does the abstract trapdoor imply public-key encryption?** Not by any argument we can give, so section 5 item 2's earlier "a trapdoor family, hence a Cryptomania object" has been softened. The implication one would want is: private fibre sampling (b) plus public indexing-hardness (a) gives a trapdoor function, hence public-key encryption. Three obstacles are stated so that a later memo can close or refute them (the third added 2026-09-20 (third session) per R4 F18). First, indexing-hardness is not one-wayness (previous paragraph); a trapdoor function needs the public to be unable to find *any* preimage, and (a) does not give that. Second, in a W-dependent setup the encoder samples the fibre only at the W it was set up for, so pp does not come with an inverter for arbitrary targets; a trapdoor *function* needs inversion everywhere on the range (MW20 Definition 6.1 asks for exactly that, `Inv_td(y)` for uniform y, and Lemma 2(d)(iv) recovers it at the targets in the RO model). Third, and decisive on its own: even granting one-wayness everywhere and an encoder that samples the fibres, a many-to-one trapdoor function with super-polynomial fibres is not known to give public-key encryption; Bellare, Halevi, Sahai and Vadhan (CRYPTO 1998, ePrint 1998/019, Theorem 1, read by R4) build such functions from *any* one-way function, and only polynomially-bounded-preimage trapdoor functions are known to yield public-key encryption (their Theorem 2). The real fibres here have `2^((ε + t/n) n)` elements on average (2.2(a)(iii)), so the object of (c) is not known to leave Minicrypt. What the *twin* route does give (R4 F30): a lossy function with lossiness rate about `0.969 − 0.05` of the input, hence collision-resistant hashing (Peikert–Waters 2008; QWW21 footnote 2), which is outside black-box one-way functions (Simon 1998) but not Cryptomania; and a twin with the uniform-target inverter of Definition 6.1 is a lossy trapdoor function, hence public-key encryption (PW08). So the Cryptomania claim is true for the twin route with a full inverter and open for the abstract object. What is true is the softer statement: every scheme meeting the specification has a trapdoor in the abstract sense of (c), and every known instantiation at this rate is an SLF or a trapdoor permutation (MW20 6.1 and 6.2 from DCR and LWE; DGO19 and GLW20 from RSA-type permutations in the ideal-permutation model). The literature qualifier: trapdoor permutations exist from sub-exponential indistinguishability obfuscation and one-way functions (Bitansky, Paneth, Wichs, "Perfect structure on the edge of chaos", TCC 2016-A, ePrint 2015/126, abstract and Section 1 read: "the first candidate that is not based on the hardness of factoring"), so "trapdoor" does not mean "factoring or lattices"; on cost such constructions are irrelevant here (T2 6.3's iO entry).

*What escapes 8.3.* Interactive decoding (the server talks to a party holding a secret) and secret-verifier schemes change who "the public" is but not (a): the server's own decoder must be public to the server, which is the adversary (T2 3.4). Timed schemes escape (a) in the same way Lemma 4 does: indexing may be efficient but slow, and the deadline is the security.

### 8.4 The lossiness cap for factoring-based twins

**Corollary 8.4 (cited; the standard-model wall for the B lane).** An MW-template encoding at retention `r = 18/19` needs entropy rate at least `r + margin = 0.947 + Δ/α ≈ 0.969` given W (Lemma 3(ii), `theory.md` 5.3), i.e. a lossy twin whose image loses about 95% of the codeword's bits. For factoring-based twins:

(a) *Lossy RSA under Φ-hiding* (Kiltz, O'Neill, Smith, "Instantiability of RSA-OAEP under chosen-plaintext attack", CRYPTO 2010; full version ePrint 2011/559, Section 5 "Lossiness of RSA", read): in lossy mode the public exponent e divides `p − 1`, the RSA map `x ↦ x^e mod N` is `e`-to-1 on its domain, and `LTDP1` is `log2 e`-lossy, with `Adv_ltdp ≤ 2 · Adv_ΦA` (their Proposition 5.1). Their "Parameters for LTDP1" paragraph: Coppersmith's method on `r(x) = e x + 1 mod p` factors N in lossy mode in polynomial time if `log2 e ≥ (1/4) log2 N`, and in time `O(N^ε)` if `log2 e ≥ (1/4 − ε) log2 N`; their example is a 2048-bit modulus with `ε = 0.04` giving `430` bits of lossiness for 80-bit security. So the lossiness of an RSA twin is at most a `1/4 − ε` fraction of the element, `r ≤ 0.25 − ε` for the encoding built on it (at 3072 bits with `ε = 128/3072 = 0.042` for 128-bit security, 640 of 3072 bits, rate 0.208), and the cheap exponents that make RSA fast (`e = 3`, `e = 2^8 + 1`, `e = 2^16 + 1`) give lossiness `log2 3`, 8 or 16 bits per 3072, rate below `0.6%`. KOS10's multi-prime and "two-or-m-primes" variants (their Section 5, later paragraphs) raise the lossiness by a constant factor under stronger assumptions and have been the target of lattice attacks since (Cryptanalysis of multi-prime Φ-hiding, ePrint 2016/155, abstract only); none approaches 0.95.

(b) *Damgård–Jurik* (MW20 Section 6.1; `theory.md` Lemma 1): the lossy mode of `f_pk(a, r) = c^a · r^(N^s) mod N^(s+1)` on `Z_(N^s) × Z_N^*` (with `c = ψ(0, r̂)`, `theory.md` 2.1) has image of size `|Z_N^*|` and fibres of size exactly `N^s`, lossiness `s/(s + 1)` of the element, so rate 0.969 at `s = 31` and rate ≥ 0.95 at `s ≥ 19`; it is the only factoring-based lossy twin on record that reaches the program's rate, and its forward map is a `(s + 1)`-limb exponentiation: mw3's measured 92 s per 12 KiB word at `s = 31`, of order `3 · 10^7` limb-MACs per byte as GMP executes it and `2` to `3.5 · 10^8` 32-bit MACs per byte in schoolbook form (T2 `twins.md` row 1a; section 5's unknown-order bullet; corrected from `10^4` by the coordinator, 2026-09-20 21:50 PDT).

(c) Consequently, for the B lane (RSA-type permutations, `OPTIONS.md` rows B1 to B3), no standard-model lossy twin exists at the program's rate: a proof for a factoring-based scheme other than Damgård–Jurik is route (ii) of item 5, a direct INC argument in the ideal-permutation model, with GLW20's chain-length constraint on record. This is why B1's question is posed in the idealised model and not as an SLF.
